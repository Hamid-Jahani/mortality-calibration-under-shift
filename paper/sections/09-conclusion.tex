\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Mortality prediction intervals are priced, reserved against and hedged,
and until now none had been scored against a realised structural break
alongside a matched control. This paper pre-registered a calibration
audit---ten forecasting families crossed with seven uncertainty
mechanisms, fifty admissible cells, one sampling interface, one scoring
path---and has now run all three registered regimes: a stable control of
thirteen expanding origins from 1990 to 2014 at a two-year step, with no
break anywhere in its test windows; the primary shift regime, every
interval built strictly from data through 2019 and held against realised
2020--2024 mortality in twenty populations of the first HMD vintage with
final data through 2024 (sixteen for the multi-output GP, whose forty-year
floor excludes four short panels); and the 1914--1922 placebo, eleven
populations over nine horizons. The post-sweep gate records no machine-failure row in
any of the three, so no number below rests on a broken run.

The control changes the question the title asks. Most of the
miscalibration this audit measures predates the pandemic. With nothing
breaking in the test window, the classical cores under their own
predictive law already cover 0.682 (Poisson Lee--Carter), 0.702 (SVD
Lee--Carter) and 0.731 (APC/RH) against a nominal 95\%, and the
LSTM-$\kappa_t$ deep ensemble covers 0.638. The break does not create
those failures; it deepens some and repairs others.

On the registered hypotheses the audit reads as follows. \Hone, demoted in
advance to a harness-validity check, is supported: the RMSE and CRPS
orderings disagree at the registered top-three inversion, and against the
Poisson log score the point-accuracy ordering nearly dissolves
($\rho=0.12$). \Htwo\ fails in the form it was registered, and the failure
is the more interesting result. The registered one-sided clause---nominal
95\% intervals under-cover in the shift regime for every model
family---is not supported. Marginal coverage does fall from control to
break in 34 of the 50 admissible cells, but a fall is degradation only
for an arm that began below nominal, and the two-sided quantity actually
tested, $|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ (Addendum~3, \S8), grows in
only 27 of 50. The arms that were over-dispersed in the control move
\emph{towards} the nominal level as the surface breaks---the sparse VAR
under its native law from 0.988 to 0.937, the NB head under a deep
ensemble from 0.988 to 0.956---while the under-covering arms move further
away, Poisson Lee--Carter from 0.682 to 0.624 and APC/RH from 0.731 to
0.654. What the pandemic does to this grid, on the rate scale, is sort it
by the side of nominal each cell started on; on the liability scale of
\Hfive\ the mechanism differs, because arms that already cover almost
nothing have no room left to fall. A study that had scored the break alone
would have read the same 34 cells as uniform degradation; the control is
what makes the sorting visible, and the registration is what obliges us
to report the registered direction as unsupported.

\Hthree\ requires the most careful statement. Joint path coverage over
$h=1,\dots,H$ lies below marginal coverage in all 100 of the (regime,
cell) pairs the two five-horizon regimes provide, with a mean gap of
0.127 in the control and 0.168 at the break, and the placebo repeats it
over nine horizons---but that ordering is close to arithmetic, since
independence alone would open a gap of $0.95-0.95^5=0.176$, wider than
either mean. The informative comparison is against each arm's own
implied independence, $\text{marginal}^H$, and there the finding is
sharp in the opposite direction: joint coverage \emph{exceeds} that
benchmark in 50 of 50 control cells, 50 of 50 shift cells and 45 of 46
placebo cells, by as much as 0.34. Horizons are strongly positively
dependent, which is the dependence \citet{dowd2010backtesting} name in a
footnote and do not measure; measuring it is what this hypothesis
contributes. The registered comparator of Addendum~3---each cell's own
model-implied joint coverage---is not computable from the columns the
sweeps emitted, and we report no verdict against it. The collapse is not a symptom of
bad marginal calibration that good calibration removes: Poisson
Lee--Carter falls from 0.624 marginal to 0.298 joint at the break, and
the best-covered marginal arms lose the path as well. Only the copula
band, the one mechanism constructed over the path, holds joint coverage
of 0.805--0.927. The quantity a liability depends on is therefore
mis-stated by the quantity the literature reports, in quiet periods as
much as at a break.

\Hfour\ is refuted as a universal and supported precisely where the
family-level split of Section~\ref{sec:discussion-split} places it: the
old-age concentration belongs to the families that forecast through an
extrapolated period index and to the LSTM, the calibrated arms are
near-flat in age, and three arms reverse the direction outright. It too
is already present in the control, where SVD Lee--Carter under its native
law covers 0.888, 0.760 and 0.501 across ages 0--24, 25--64 and 65--99
with no break in the test window---a gradient of nearly four tenths that
owes nothing to the pandemic. This resolves an open point against the
published prior: \citet{dowd2010backtesting} report their age-84
forecasts backtesting better than their age-65 ones, and Addendum~3
pre-committed to treating a reversal as informative rather than as
grounds for re-testing. The resolution is that the age profile of
miscalibration is a property of the family--mechanism pair and not of
mortality forecasting as such, and for the index extrapolators the
old-age deterioration is real, large and present without any break at
all.

\Hfive\ is the result an actuarial reader should carry away, and it is
the most severe in the paper. Calibration does not survive integration
across ages. In the stable control, SVD Lee--Carter under its native law
covers 0.253 of its nominal 95\% intervals for $\annuity$ and 0.256 for
$e_{65}$, and the LSTM deep ensemble covers 0.157 for $\annuity$: an arm
whose rate-level marginal coverage is 0.702 delivers an annuity interval
that misses roughly three times in four in a quiet period. Integration
concentrates the rate-surface failure rather than averaging it away,
which is the opposite of the attenuation \citet{cairns2011mortality}
describe for point applications. Annuity factors and life-expectancy
intervals from these families carry essentially no reliable interval
whether or not a pandemic intervenes, and any reserving or
longevity-hedging use of them needs that figure stated in front of it.

The crossed design delivered the attribution it was built for, and the
control shows that this attribution is not a pandemic effect either.
Split conformal wrapping significantly improves the 95\% interval score
of exactly the families whose native law was broken and significantly
worsens exactly those whose native law was sound, with the same sign in
both regimes: a mean $\IS_{0.05}$ difference of $+0.694$ in the control
and $+0.700$ at the break for SVD Lee--Carter, both favouring the split
band, against $-0.873$ and $-1.024$ for the NB head, both favouring its
native law. Wrap what is broken and do not wrap what is calibrated is a
standing rule of interval construction, not crisis advice. That sign
pattern, not any single coverage number, is the study's central
methodological result, and it is the kind of result only a design that
separates architecture from uncertainty mechanism can produce.

The remaining contribution is the protocol itself: a pre-registration
hash-stamped before the first fit, a harness that recovered nominal
coverage on synthetic truth and matched the reference implementation to
machine precision, one predictive-sample interface for every family,
cluster-bootstrap forecast-comparison inference that treats a pandemic as
one event rather than thousands of cells, and---the component this study
found it could not do without---a matched control regime that says how
much of a break's apparent damage was there beforehand. The title's
question has a more interesting answer than yes or no: most of these
intervals were never calibrated to begin with, and the break mainly
re-orders them. The next break will not announce itself either; the
protocol is built to score it, and to apportion the score. The repair
belongs to the models that come after.
