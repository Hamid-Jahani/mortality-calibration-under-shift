\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Mortality prediction intervals are priced, reserved against and hedged. The
literature that produces them has, with few exceptions, evaluated them by
the accuracy of their centres, and where it has looked at their coverage it
has looked without a sharpness penalty and without a break in the data. The
COVID-19 years supply the break, the June 2026 HMD vintage supplies the
final data through 2024, and the pre-registered design set out here
supplies the audit: ten families crossed with seven uncertainty mechanisms,
scored through one code path by proper scores, interval scores, marginal
and joint path coverage, PIT, a Murphy decomposition and the life-table
functionals an actuary values, with inference that treats a pandemic as one
event rather than as thousands of independent cells.

Whatever the tables turn out to hold, three things will be true of them.
The hypotheses they test were fixed before the data were touched. The
harness that produced them recovered nominal coverage on synthetic truth
and matched the reference implementation to machine precision. And every
failure they document can be attributed---to the architecture, to the
uncertainty mechanism, or to the two together---because the design crossed
them. That attribution, and the protocol that makes it possible, is what
this study adds; the repair belongs to the models that come after.
