\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Notation and the scoring target}
\label{sec:metrics-target}

Fix a population, a sex and an origin $T$. For age $x$ and horizon $h$ the
observed quantities are the death count $D_{x,T+h}$ and the known exposure
$E_{x,T+h}$. Two targets are scored. On the \emph{count} scale the target is
the half-up rounded count $\tilde D_{x,h}=\lfloor D_{x,T+h}+\tfrac12\rfloor$
(Section~\ref{sec:data-rounding}). On the \emph{rate} scale the target is
the observed crude rate on the half-count lattice,
\begin{equation}
  y_{x,h} = \log\frac{\max(\tilde D_{x,h},\tfrac12)}{E_{x,T+h}}.
\end{equation}
A distributional cell of the grid supplies $n$ predictive sample paths
$m^{(j)}_{x,T+h}$, $j=1,\dots,n$, jointly over $(x,h)$. These are
\emph{latent} rates; the quantity that is observed carries Poisson sampling
noise on top of them, so every rate-scale score is computed on the
Poisson-inclusive samples
\begin{equation}
  z^{(j)}_{x,h} = \log\frac{\max(D^{\ast(j)}_{x,h},\tfrac12)}{E_{x,T+h}},
  \qquad D^{\ast(j)}_{x,h}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\big(E_{x,T+h}\,m^{(j)}_{x,T+h}\big),
  \label{eq:poisson-inclusive}
\end{equation}
which is the predictive law of the observed rate (Addendum~2~\S1) and the
same construction already used for the count-scale scores. Both sides of
every rate-scale score therefore share one lattice and one continuity
correction (Addendum~3~\S5); scoring latent samples against observed rates,
or fractional observed deaths against an integer lattice, each make a
perfectly calibrated forecast look miscalibrated at small expected counts.
$\hat F_{x,h}$ denotes the empirical distribution of $\{z^{(j)}_{x,h}\}_j$
and $\hat F^{-1}_{x,h}(p)$ its empirical quantile. Conformal cells are the
exception: they supply an interval $[l_{x,h},u_{x,h}]$ at the 95\% level
rather than a distribution, and are scored from it directly
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics-interval}).

All scores are negatively oriented: smaller is better. A cell's scores are
averaged over the ages it is defined on---100 ages for every family but
CBD, 45 for CBD---and the number of ages and (horizon, age) cells entering
every mean is recorded; means are never taken over a mixture of defined and
undefined cells, and arms with different age supports are never averaged
into one ranking. Averages over cells are denoted by an overbar; averaging
is always within population--sex first, then across populations with equal
weight, so that a large population cannot dominate a small one, so that
the cluster structure of Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference} is preserved,
and so that any ranking inversion is not the exposure-weighting artefact of
Section~\ref{sec:related}.

\subsection{Point accuracy}

\begin{equation}
  \RMSE = \Big(\overline{(\hat y_{x,h}-y_{x,h})^2}\Big)^{1/2},\qquad
  \MAE = \overline{|\hat y_{x,h}-y_{x,h}|},\qquad
  \hat y_{x,h} = \hat F^{-1}_{x,h}(\tfrac12),
\end{equation}
together with the error in period life expectancy at birth,
$\hat e_0 - e_0$, where $\hat e_0$ is the median of the per-sample
$e^{(j)}_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:metrics-actuarial}). These are the metrics
the literature reports; \Hone\ is the check that ranking by them is not
ranking by a proper score.

\subsection{Continuous ranked probability score}

For a predictive distribution $F$ and outcome $y$,
\begin{equation}
  \CRPS(F,y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big(F(u)-\ind\{u\ge y\}\big)^2\,du
  = \E_F|Z-y| - \tfrac12\,\E_F|Z-Z'|,
\end{equation}
with $Z,Z'\sim F$ independent \citep{gneiting2007strictly}. From samples we
use the plug-in estimator with sorted samples $z_{(1)}\le\dots\le z_{(n)}$,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\CRPS} = \frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z^{(j)}-y|
  - \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(2j-n-1\big)\,z_{(j)},
  \label{eq:crps-sample}
\end{equation}
which is the $O(n\log n)$ form of the second term. CRPS is computed on the
rate scale with the Poisson-inclusive samples~\eqref{eq:poisson-inclusive}
for every distributional cell, and on the count scale as a registered
sensitivity: the same estimator applied to the count samples
$D^{\ast(j)}_{x,h}$ against the \emph{unrounded} $D_{x,T+h}$, which needs
no rounding convention. It is strictly proper and reduces to MAE for a
point forecast, which is what makes the RMSE-versus-CRPS ranking comparison
of \Hone\ well posed.

\subsection{Poisson log score and the rounding convention}
\label{sec:metrics-logscore}

Each sample path implies a Poisson mean $\lambda^{(j)}_{x,h} =
E_{x,T+h}\,m^{(j)}_{x,T+h}$ for the death count, so the predictive count
distribution is the equal-weight mixture
$p(d)=\frac1n\sum_j \mathrm{Poisson}(d;\lambda^{(j)}_{x,h})$. The log score is
\begin{equation}
  \LS = -\log\Big(\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{Poisson}\big(\tilde D_{x,h};\lambda^{(j)}_{x,h}\big)\Big),
  \qquad \tilde D = \big\lfloor D+\tfrac12\big\rfloor,
\end{equation}
evaluated by log-sum-exp. Because HMD deaths are fractional
(Section~\ref{sec:data}), the score is defined at the half-up rounded
count; the sensitivity is the count-scale CRPS above, which is well defined
for any real $D$. Both conventions were fixed before estimation. For the
distributional NB head the mixture over gamma-distributed rates reproduces
the negative-binomial predictive exactly, so the family is scored on its
own law.

\subsection{Interval score and coverage}
\label{sec:metrics-interval}

For the central $(1-\alpha)$ interval $[l,u]$ the interval (Winkler) score
is
\begin{equation}
  \IS_\alpha(l,u;y) = (u-l) + \frac{2}{\alpha}(l-y)\,\ind\{y<l\}
  + \frac{2}{\alpha}(y-u)\,\ind\{y>u\},
  \label{eq:winkler}
\end{equation}
\citep{winkler1972decision,gneiting2007strictly}. For a distributional cell
$[l,u]=[\hat F^{-1}(\alpha/2),\hat F^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)]$ from the
Poisson-inclusive samples, reported at $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.2,0.05\}$. For a
conformal cell $[l,u]$ is the wrapper's own band at its construction level
$\alpha=0.05$, read from the bounds with no sampling step and no Poisson
composition (Addendum~3~\S6); the 50\% and 80\% rows are not applicable and
are shown as such, because a quantile of a uniform-in-interval sample would
describe the filler rather than a forecast. The interval score is the
width-penalised criterion whose absence from the mortality literature
Section~\ref{sec:intro} documents: an interval wide enough to cover
everything has coverage one and an interval score equal to its width.
Alongside it we always report the pair (empirical coverage, mean width),
never coverage alone. Because the 95\% interval score is defined
identically for distributional and conformal cells, it is the loss on
which every comparison that involves a conformal arm is decided
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference}).

\subsection{Marginal, joint and model-implied path coverage}
\label{sec:metrics-coverage}

\emph{Marginal} coverage is the pointwise hit rate,
\begin{equation}
  C^{\mathrm{marg}}_{1-\alpha}(h) = \overline{\ind\{\,l_{x,h}\le y_{x,h}\le u_{x,h}\,\}},
\end{equation}
averaged over ages and populations for each horizon $h$, and pooled over
$h$ for a single number. It is the quantity every mortality paper that
reports coverage reports.

\emph{Joint path} coverage asks whether the whole trajectory of a unit
(one population--sex--age) lies inside its bands:
\begin{equation}
  C^{\mathrm{joint}}_{1-\alpha} = \overline{\prod_{h=1}^{H}\ind\{\,l_{x,h}\le y_{x,h}\le u_{x,h}\,\}},
  \qquad H=5 \text{ (shift, stable)},\ H=9 \text{ (placebo)}.
\end{equation}
A path counts as covered only if every horizon is inside its band. This is
the object an annuity valuation actually depends on, because a liability is
a functional of the path of future mortality, not of one year of it. For
pointwise bands at level $1-\alpha$, joint coverage is bounded above by the
minimum marginal coverage and, if horizons were independent, would equal
$(1-\alpha)^H$; the strong horizon dependence of random-walk paths puts it
between the two. That arithmetic is why the registered comparison of joint
with marginal coverage was near-tautological (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}).
The comparator is therefore each cell's \emph{model-implied} joint
coverage, the fraction of its own sample paths that stay inside its own
pointwise bands at every horizon,
\begin{equation}
  C^{\mathrm{impl}}_{1-\alpha} = \overline{\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{h=1}^{H}
  \ind\{\,l_{x,h}\le z^{(j)}_{x,h}\le u_{x,h}\,\}},
\end{equation}
computed from the same Poisson-inclusive paths and the same bands. A
forecaster whose paths are as dependent across horizons as reality has
$C^{\mathrm{joint}}\approx C^{\mathrm{impl}}$; the gap
$C^{\mathrm{impl}}-C^{\mathrm{joint}}$ is the registered quantity of
\Hthree, can be of either sign, and is tested as a paired difference over
units, clustered by population. The marginal-to-joint gap
$C^{\mathrm{marg}}-C^{\mathrm{joint}}$ is still reported as the descriptive
measurement that no mortality paper has made. Only the copula conformal
mechanism targets the joint level by construction; for that arm
$C^{\mathrm{joint}}$ is read from its simultaneous band and no
model-implied value exists. One gap in the production pipeline is stated
rather than silently carried: the runner's column contract carries the
realised joint coverage but does not yet emit $C^{\mathrm{impl}}$, so the
registered paired test of Addendum~3~\S8 awaits a recomputation of the
implied coverage from each cell's reproducible sample paths (seeds are
recorded per row), and until that column exists the joint-versus-marginal
results of Section~\ref{sec:results} are the descriptive measurement, not
the registered test of \Hthree.

\subsection{Randomised probability integral transform}

A forecaster is probabilistically calibrated if $F(Y)$ is uniform
\citep{gneiting2007probabilistic,dawid1984present}. Because the predictive is
an empirical distribution of $n$ samples on a lattice and the target may
tie with them, we use the randomised PIT of \citet{czado2009predictive},
\begin{equation}
  u_{x,h} = \frac{\#\{j: z^{(j)}_{x,h} < y_{x,h}\} + V\,\big(\#\{j: z^{(j)}_{x,h}=y_{x,h}\}+1\big)}{n+1},
  \qquad V\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1),
\end{equation}
which is exactly uniform under the null for any finite $n$. We report the
ten-bin histogram, the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance from uniformity, and the
shape---U (over-confident), hump (under-confident), slope (biased). The KS
statistic is accompanied by its nominal $p$-value, which is
\emph{descriptive only}: PIT values across ages and horizons within a
population are dependent, so the independence the KS null assumes does not
hold (Addendum~2~\S4). The formal test is a cluster-bootstrap test of the
pooled histogram (Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference}), not a cell-level KS
$p$-value. PIT values of conformal cells are placeholders and are reported
only in Appendix~\ref{app:secondary}.

\subsection{Calibration by age}

All coverage and PIT statistics are additionally computed within the age
bands $0$--$24$, $25$--$64$ and $65$--$99$---the same partition the
conformal wrappers are Mondrian-stratified on, so that a conformal cell's
band-level coverage is read at the resolution it was calibrated at---and
as single-age curves. \Hfour\ compares the $65$--$99$ and $25$--$64$ bands
in the shift regime; a reversal of the registered direction is reported as
such (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}). For CBD the $0$--$24$ band is empty
and the $25$--$64$ band contains ages 55--64 only; its by-age results are
stated on that support.

\subsection{Murphy decomposition}

For a proper score $S$ the mean score decomposes as
\begin{equation}
  \overline{S} = \mathrm{UNC} - \mathrm{RES} + \mathrm{MCB},
\end{equation}
uncertainty of the outcome minus resolution of the forecasts plus
miscalibration \citep{murphy1973new,pohle2020murphy}. It separates
\emph{why} a forecaster scores badly: a well-calibrated but vague
forecaster has low RES; a sharp but wrong one has high MCB. Two
decompositions are emitted per cell. The first is Murphy's original
partition of the Brier score, taking as outcome the 95\% interval-hit
indicator of each cell and as forecast the nominal level $0.95$ that every
cell asserts; with a constant forecast a single bin is occupied, the
three-term identity is exact, and the partition degenerates to
$\mathrm{MCB}=(0.95-\text{empirical coverage})^2$ and $\mathrm{RES}=0$,
so it restates the coverage gap on the Brier scale and is reported for
that reason only. The second is the divergence form of the decomposition
\citep{broecker2009reliability} applied on the PIT scale with the uniform
distribution as reference: the ten-bin PIT histogram is scored against
the flat claim every calibrated forecaster makes, and the emitted
miscalibration term is the squared distance of the histogram from
uniformity---the departure a single level's coverage gap cannot see.
Under this reference the reliability and resolution terms coincide
numerically, because the PIT transform standardises every forecaster to
the same claim and discards sharpness; no resolution component with
independent content is emitted, since one would require a sample-based
partition such as the rank-histogram CRPS decomposition of
\citet{hersbach2000decomposition}, which is out of scope. The isotonic
(CORP) estimator of \citet{dimitriadis2021stable} is the stable
alternative and is not emitted; the decomposition is descriptive, and no
hypothesis rests on it.

\subsection{Actuarial functionals}
\label{sec:metrics-actuarial}

Each sample path's rate vector $m^{(j)}_{\cdot,T+h}$ is converted to a period
life table by the conventions of Section~\ref{sec:data}, giving samples of
\begin{equation}
  e^{(j)}_x = \frac{T^{(j)}_x}{l^{(j)}_x},\qquad
  \annuity^{(j)} = \sum_{t\ge0} v^{t}\,{}_t p^{(j)}_{65},\quad v=\frac{1}{1.02},
\end{equation}
where ${}_t p_{65}=l_{65+t}/l_{65}$ from the sample's own table treated as
static, i.e.\ the annuity-due factor at 2\% on the period table of the
first test year $T+1$ \citep{dickson2020actuarial}: 2020 in the shift
regime, 1914 in the placebo, $T+1$ at each stable origin. The predictive
tables are built from the latent rate paths $m^{(j)}_{\cdot,T+1}$ (not the
Poisson-inclusive draws, which describe one year's crude rate rather than
a mortality schedule); the realised values are computed from the observed
$m_{x,T+1}$ by the same routine, with the observed life-table convention of
Section~\ref{sec:data-rounding}, so that any life-table approximation
cancels in the comparison. Both sides use the maximal contiguous scored age
range from age~0, with the range recorded; for CBD, $e_0$ is undefined and
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ are computed on the 55--99 table. For $e_0$,
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ we report the point (median) error, the empirical
coverage of the central 95\% interval and its width, in each regime;
\Hfive\ compares the shift and stable departures from nominal coverage.
The registered quantity is ex-post coverage, and the named alternative is
attenuation---that whatever miscalibration the rates carry is damped by the
integration a life table performs (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}). We
also report the 99.5\% upper quantile of $\annuity$ from each cell as the
longevity tail an internal model would carry; this quantile is
\emph{descriptive}, is not part of any registered hypothesis, and is
labelled as such wherever it appears.

\subsection{Forecast-comparison inference}
\label{sec:metrics-inference}

\paragraph{Effective sample size.} The shift regime contains one common
shock. Within a population, ages and horizons share the same realised
pandemic; across populations, the shock itself is shared. Treating cells as
independent would produce standard errors that are wrong by orders of
magnitude. Population is therefore the cluster ($G=20$ in the shift and
stable regimes, $G=11$ in the placebo; fewer where the admissibility floor
or the common-cell rule removes a population, with the effective $G$
printed beside every statistic), and every test resamples clusters.
Even so, $p$-values in the shift regime are statements about differences
between forecasters \emph{given this event}, not about future pandemics;
that is what an event study can deliver, and the placebo regime is the
second event that makes a cross-event comparison possible.

\paragraph{Loss series and the common-cell rule.} Per-unit losses are
per-horizon means over ages within a population--sex--origin: CRPS on the
rate scale for contrasts among distributional arms, and the 95\% interval
score for any contrast that includes a conformal arm, whose CRPS is a
placeholder that the analysis code refuses to rank. Every contrast is
computed on the intersection of (origin, population, sex) cells in which
each compared arm produced a valid row (Addendum~3~\S11), the intersection
size is reported, and a non-finite loss halts the computation rather than
being imputed or dropped in place: on the runner's real pattern of missing
cells, imputation was measured to invert a model confidence set silently,
eliminating the good models with certainty. Arms with different age
supports (CBD against the rest) are never placed in one loss matrix.

\paragraph{Diebold--Mariano with a wild cluster bootstrap.} For forecasters
$A$ and $B$ with per-unit losses $L_{A,i}$, $L_{B,i}$, the differential
$d_i = L_{A,i}-L_{B,i}$ has mean $\bar d$ and cluster-robust variance
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar d) = \frac{G}{G-1}\,\frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}
  \Big(\sum_{i\in g}(d_i-\bar d)\Big)^2,
\end{equation}
giving the statistic $t=\bar d/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}$
\citep{diebold1995comparing}. Its null distribution is obtained by the wild
cluster bootstrap of \citet{cameron2008bootstrap} with the null imposed:
residuals $d_i-\bar d$ are multiplied by a cluster-level weight $w_g$ drawn
from the six-point distribution of \citet{webb2023reworking}, which has
enough distinct sign patterns at $G=20$ where Rademacher weights do not
\citep{mackinnon2017wild}; $B=4999$ replications. Negative $\bar d$ favours
$A$.

\paragraph{Model confidence set.} Following \citet{hansen2011model}, the MCS
at confidence $1-\alpha=0.90$ is built by the $T_{\max}$ statistic and the
$e_{\max}$ elimination rule, with the bootstrap distribution of the
statistic obtained by resampling \emph{populations} as blocks (2{,}000
replications)---the analogue of their stationary block bootstrap for our
panel. Sets are formed within a common age support only---the full-age
classical families, with a second set that admits CBD only when its
restricted support does not make the losses incomparable---and on the loss
appropriate to the arms: rate-scale CRPS among distributional arms, the
95\% interval score among the three conformal mechanisms within each
family. The native-versus-split-conformal contrast within each family is a
Diebold--Mariano test on the interval score.

\paragraph{Hypothesis tests.} \Hone\ is assessed by the Spearman correlation
of RMSE and CRPS rankings and the count of top-3 inversions, with a
cluster-bootstrap interval for the correlation; per the demotion of
Section~\ref{sec:design-status} it is reported as a validity check.
\Htwo\ is the paired contrast of $|\text{coverage}-0.95|$, shift minus
stable, per family and mechanism, tested by the wild cluster bootstrap
above applied to the unit-level absolute coverage gaps. \Hthree\ is the
paired difference of model-implied and realised joint coverage over units,
clustered by population. \Hfour\ and \Hfive\ are paired contrasts of
coverage (ages 65--99 minus 25--64; shift minus stable departure from
nominal for $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$) tested the same way.
