\subsection{H5: propagation into life expectancy and annuity factors}

% tab-h5-actuarial is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; it carries its own \caption and \label{tab:h5-actuarial}.
\inputtable{tab-h5-actuarial}

Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} reports, for each family--mechanism cell and
regime, the empirical coverage of the central 95\% interval and the mean
error for period life expectancy at birth and at 65 and for the annuity-due
factor at 65 and 2\%, each computed on the period table of the first test
year from the cell's own sample paths and compared with the same functional
of the observed rates. The functionals are taken from the latent rate paths
of every distributional cell, before the Poisson composition that the
rate-scale scores use. Three conventions of the table govern its reading.
Conformal cells have no such paths---their samples are uniform draws inside
a band, a convention for interval metrics only---so their derived-quantity
rows are not applicable and the table marks them \emph{n/a} rather than
tabulating a distributional claim; the conformal arms therefore support no
statement in this section, and every count below is over the twenty
distributional arms. CBD is fit on a 45-age support, ages 55--99: it
contributes no $e_0$, and although $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ require only
ages 65--99 and are exact on its truncated table, they rest on a fit that
never saw the younger ages---the difference from the other families is one
of fitting support, not of integration range---so its rows are not on the
same footing as the full-age families'. And no row carries its regime's
full complement of (population, sex, origin) units---520 over thirteen
origins in the stable control, forty in the shift regime, twenty-two at the
placebo---because error rows (Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}) are excluded from
every mean: the fullest stable rows reach 442 of 520, the nine non-GP
families scoring eighteen of the twenty stable populations and the
multi-output GP sixteen. $n$ and $n_{\mathrm{err}}$ are tabulated per cell,
and the sparse VAR, the multi-output GP and the LSTM carry the thinnest
rows---the sparse VAR's native law is scored on 297 of the 520 stable units
and its Poisson bootstrap on 238, the thinnest distributional row in the
control---while at the placebo, among the distributional rows, only the
sparse VAR and the GP fall short of the full twenty-two units. \Hfive\ is
restricted by Addendum~3 (\S8) to the ex-post coverage of these derived
intervals, with
the attenuation alternative---that life-table integration averages a
rate-surface failure away \citep{cairns2011mortality}---as the named
competitor. Both outcomes are informative: the first says the reserve is
wrong, the second says the audit's headline is a statement about rates
rather than about liabilities.

\Hfive\ was registered as: \emph{interval miscalibration propagates to
derived quantities---empirical coverage of 95\% intervals for $e_{65}$ and
the annuity factor $\annuity$ at 2\% departs from nominal by more than the
corresponding stable-regime departure}. Read strictly, as the universal
ordering the registration states, it is not supported: the shift departure
exceeds the stable departure for 14 of the 20 distributional arms on
$e_{65}$ and for 12 of 20 on $\annuity$---a majority, not the universal the
clause asserts, and the same standard by which Section~\ref{sec:results}
declines to call \Htwo's registered form supported at 27 of 50. What
survives is the
substantive claim the registered ordering stood proxy for, and it is the
most actuarially consequential finding of this audit: miscalibration of the
rate surface reaches the reserve, it does so in every regime, and---like
the rest of the audit---it is not a pandemic effect. The derived intervals
are already broken in the stable control, the regime constructed so that
nothing breaks in it. SVD Lee--Carter under its own law covers 0.256 of the
realised $e_{65}$ and 0.253 of the realised $\annuity$ against nominal 0.95
over its 442 scored stable units; the LSTM ensemble covers 0.182 and 0.157 and its
dropout arm 0.205 and 0.191; Poisson-LC covers 0.434 and 0.430, the
neural-LC ensemble 0.468 and 0.459, CNN-LC under dropout 0.559 and 0.548,
and APC/RH 0.654 and 0.643. Only two of the twenty distributional arms come
within 0.05 of nominal on $\annuity$ in the stable control---the sparse
VAR's native law at 0.980, on 297 of the 520 stable units, and CBD's
Poisson bootstrap at 0.934 on its truncated 55--99 support---and thirteen
of the twenty sit below 0.700.

The actuarial reading is blunt. An annuity factor whose nominal 95\%
interval contains the realised value in a quarter of population--sex
origins is not a usable reserving input: at 0.253 for SVD Lee--Carter and
0.157 for the LSTM ensemble, the interval an actuary would quote for
$\annuity$ says almost nothing about where the realised value will fall,
and it says it in the quiet decades 1990--2014 exactly as it says it across
2020. A literature that reports rate-level coverage---including this
paper's own \Htwo\ table---therefore understates by a wide margin what a
practitioner sees on the quantity actually reserved.

The registered comparison is an ordering of departures, and there the
result is partial; we state it as such. The counts above are on the
two-sided departure $|\text{coverage}-0.95|$ per arm, and the exceptions
are informative in themselves. The six exceptions on $e_{65}$ are the two
SVD Lee--Carter arms, the two LSTM arms, CNN-LC under dropout and
Poisson-LC under the Poisson bootstrap; on $\annuity$ the same six plus
CNN-LC's ensemble and Poisson-LC's native law. They are not well-behaved
intervals that the break spared: they are predominantly the arms whose
stable departure was already the largest---the LSTM ensemble covers 0.157
of realised annuity factors in the control, its dropout arm 0.191, SVD
Lee--Carter 0.253---and the break moved them \emph{towards} nominal, in
places by a wide margin, SVD Lee--Carter's Poisson bootstrap closing from a
departure of 0.645 to one of 0.400 on $\annuity$. The rule does not
separate cleanly, and we do not claim that it does: Poisson-LC's native law
and the neural-LC ensemble start further from nominal on $e_{65}$ (0.516
and 0.482) than CNN-LC under dropout (0.391) and still move away. What does
not operate here is the sorting \Htwo\ shows on the rate scale
(Section~\ref{sec:results}), where over-covering arms are drawn towards
nominal and under-covering arms pushed off it. On the liability scale there
is almost nothing above nominal to sort: nineteen of the twenty
distributional arms already under-cover $\annuity$ in the stable control,
and the single arm that does not---the sparse VAR under its native law at
0.980---is the arm the break damages most, from a departure of 0.030 to one
of 0.364. What operates instead is a floor. The eight arms that moved
towards nominal are among the deepest under-coverers of the twenty,
spanning 0.157 to 0.558 in the control, and an interval that already misses
four cells in five has little room left to miss more. \Hfive's registered
clause therefore holds for a majority of arms and not for all; the
substantive claim it stood proxy for---that miscalibration of the rate
surface reaches the reserve---holds in every regime, and holds worst where
the pandemic is absent.

The attenuation alternative is answered, and answered the same way in both
regimes. Reading Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} beside
Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}, every one of the eighteen full-age
distributional arms covers the realised $\annuity$ less often than it
covers a single log rate, in the stable control and in the shift regime
alike. The two sides are not scored on identical horizons: the marginal
rate coverage is pooled over $h=1\ldots5$ while the derived quantity is
computed on the first test year alone, so this is an $h$-aggregated rate
figure set against an $h=1$ functional. The 18-of-18 direction does not
turn on that---the annuity is scored at the shortest horizon, the one most
favourable to it---but the sizes below should be read with the mismatch in
view. In the stable control the gap between an arm's marginal rate
coverage and its $\annuity$ coverage runs from 0.008 (the sparse VAR under
its native law) to 0.481 (the LSTM ensemble) and averages 0.252; in the
shift regime it averages 0.289. Integration does not average the failure
away; it concentrates it, and it concentrates it in quiet periods too. The
instructive cells are the arms that look calibrated on rates and are not
calibrated on liabilities: CNN-LC under dropout covers 0.954 of single log
rates in the stable control and 0.548 of realised annuity factors, and the
NB head's ensemble covers 0.988 and 0.814. Rate-level coverage near nominal
is no evidence of liability-level coverage near nominal, which is the
practical reason an audit of this kind must score the functional the
actuary uses.

The mechanism is the one \Hthree\ measures on the horizon axis, here acting
on the age axis: a period functional integrates the whole scored age range
of one test year, so a common-factor miss that leaves many single-age
intervals near but inside their bounds still displaces the functional, and
one displaced functional is one uncovered cell. A derived quantity behaves
as a joint coverage statement across ages, not a marginal one. Priced from
the same marginal baseline on the same arms, the age axis is the dearer of
the two. Restricted to the eighteen full-age distributional cells, the mean
gap from an arm's marginal rate coverage to its joint five-horizon path
coverage is 0.153 in the stable control and 0.208 in the shift regime,
against marginal-to-annuity gaps of 0.252 and 0.289 on those same arms; the
0.127 and 0.168 quoted for \Hthree\ in Section~\ref{sec:results} are means
over the full fifty-cell grid, thirty cells of which are conformal and
carry no derived quantity, and are not the like-for-like comparator. The
horizon gap is also the weaker quantity before any comparison is made,
because most of it is arithmetic: independence alone, at nominal coverage
over five horizons, produces a gap of $1-0.95^5=0.176$, larger than either
grid mean. Every one of the fifty stable and fifty shift cells in fact
clears the independence benchmark set at its \emph{own} marginal rate, by
as much as 0.34, so horizons are strongly positively dependent rather than
independent. That dependence is measured against the independence
benchmark and not against each model's own implied path law: the
re-specified comparator of Addendum~3 (\S8), a cell's model-implied joint
coverage, is not computable from the columns the runner emitted, and we
state the point as a limitation rather than work around it. The annuity gap
admits no such arithmetic discount in any case: $\annuity$ is one event on
one test year, not a conjunction of five, and nothing about dependence
across horizons accounts for any part of the 0.252.

Two families qualify the mechanism rather than contradicting it. On the two
registered functionals CBD is the only family whose derived coverage
exceeds its own rate-scale coverage: in the stable control its native law
covers 0.930 of realised $e_{65}$ against a marginal 0.901, and its Poisson
bootstrap 0.950 on $e_{65}$ and 0.934 on $\annuity$ against a marginal
0.921---the native arm's 0.889 on $\annuity$ being the one figure of the
four that falls just short of its own marginal. The claim is scoped to
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$; on the unregistered $e_0$ column, below, two
classical families run the same way. CBD is also the only family fit and
integrated on ages 55--99 alone; whether that behaviour is a property of
the family or of its truncated support is not separable in this design, and
its rows are read with that caveat. The sparse VAR is the one full-age arm
whose derived intervals are calibrated when nothing breaks, at 0.983 and
0.980, and it is the arm the break damages most in derived terms, at 0.621
and 0.586 on 29 admissible shift units. Its two scales disagree about the
same model: on rates the break moves the sparse VAR's native law
\emph{towards} nominal, from 0.988 to 0.937, while on the liability it
moves from 0.980 to 0.586. Reporting only one of the two scales would
describe this family as improved or as destroyed, at the analyst's choice.

On $e_0$, which \Hfive\ does not register, two classical families run the
other way: Poisson-LC covers 0.744 and APC/RH 0.808 in the stable control,
above their own rate-level marginals of 0.682 and 0.731, while SVD
Lee--Carter reaches 0.369 and the LSTM ensemble 0.267. We report the column
descriptively and read no mechanism into it.

\subsection{Reading a coverage shortfall in balance-sheet terms}

For an annuity-due of one per annum at age 65, the interval on $\annuity$
translates directly into an interval on the best-estimate liability of a
unit annuitant. A nominal 95\% interval that covers the realised $\annuity$
in materially fewer than 95\% of population--sex cells is, in Solvency~II
language, an internal model whose longevity distribution is
miscalibrated---too narrow for most of these arms, mislocated for the few
that overstate---and Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} puts every distributional
arm in that position in the shift regime and all but two of them in the
stable control. Under COVID the best figure is 0.850 (CBD, on its truncated
table) and no full-age arm exceeds 0.750. The two the control affords do
not survive the break: the sparse VAR's native law, at 0.980 on 297 units,
is if anything too wide rather than too narrow there, and CBD's Poisson
bootstrap sits at 0.934. Thirteen of the twenty arms are below 0.700 even
in the quiet control.

The direction of the central miss carries its own message. In the stable
control the mean error on $\annuity$ is negative for 19 of the 20
distributional arms---the model's central annuity factor below the realised
one, understating the liability---the single exception being the NB-head
ensemble at $+0.02$ annuity units, and the understatement reaching $-0.63$
for the LSTM ensemble and $-0.58$ for SVD Lee--Carter. Across the pandemic
the signs split, twelve positive against eight negative: the two SVD
Lee--Carter arms, both CNN-LC arms and both LSTM arms still sit below the
realised $\annuity$ (mean errors $-0.24$ to $-0.45$), and CBD's two arms
sit marginally below on its truncated table ($-0.03$ and $-0.04$), while
APC/RH, neural-LC, the sparse VAR under its native law and the NB-head
ensemble sit above it ($+0.24$ to $+0.39$), the sparse VAR's bootstrap arm
above it by less at $+0.14$. This is not the single-signed pandemic effect
that a uniform mortality margin would absorb,
and for the arms that were already understating it is not a pandemic effect
at all: the excess mortality of 2020 moved the realised annuity factor
towards their too-low central estimates, so the break masked a pre-existing
bias rather than creating one.

The shortfall of the model's 97.5\% quantile of $\annuity$ below the
realised value, where it occurs, is the amount by which the implied capital
at that level is understated; we report this quantity as a proportion of
the central $\annuity$ so that it is portable across populations and
interest-rate assumptions.

Computed that way, the understating arms breach their own upper quantile at
many times its nominal 2.5\% frequency---and they breach it more often in
the quiet control than under COVID. In the stable regime the realised
$\annuity$ exceeds the 97.5\% quantile in 0.804 of the LSTM ensemble's 434
admissible cells, 0.779 of LSTM dropout's, 0.701 of SVD Lee--Carter's 442
under its native law, 0.529 of Poisson-LC's and 0.441 of CNN-LC dropout's,
with mean shortfalls over the breached cells of 4.2\%, 4.0\%, 4.2\%, 2.0\%
and 2.4\% of the central $\annuity$. In the shift regime the same five arms
breach in 0.605, 0.474, 0.425, 0.150 and 0.325 of cells, with mean
shortfalls of 3.4\%, 3.8\%, 4.3\%, 1.5\% and 2.5\%. Capital set at the
model's 97.5\% quantile is therefore understated by two to four per cent of
the best-estimate liability in a large minority of population--sex
origins---and in the stable control, for four of these five arms, in an
outright majority: 0.529 for Poisson-LC, 0.701 for SVD Lee--Carter and
0.779 and 0.804 for the two LSTM arms---against a nominal one in forty.

The overstating arms fail on the other side. In the shift regime the
NB-head ensemble and its dropout arm never breach their upper quantile, but
the realised value falls below their 2.5\% quantile in 0.550 and 0.250 of
cells (mean excess 2.0\% and 1.6\% of the central $\annuity$), and the
neural-LC ensemble in 0.625 (2.3\%); in the stable control the same three
arms are milder and two-sided, with upper breaches in 0.081, 0.106 and
0.292 of cells against lower breaches in 0.104, 0.090 and 0.249---reserves
set too high rather than capital set too low. CBD is again the outlier in
magnitude: upper breaches in 0.050--0.075 of shift cells and 0.066--0.109
of stable cells, with a mean shortfall at or below 1\% of the central
$\annuity$ throughout. Because the quantity is reported relative to the
central $\annuity$, a worked example on a hypothetical portfolio would only
rescale these figures, and we do not add one.

\subsection{Twin crises in economic terms}

The placebo rows of Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} supply the same
functionals for 1914--1922. A side-by-side of the $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$
interval coverage across the two events asks whether any break-induced
coverage shortfall is of similar economic size at the two breaks a century
apart, or whether the pandemic years are distinctive; the comparison is
descriptive, and the placebo's first test year is 1914, before the
influenza wave, which the reading must respect. With the stable control in
place the question sharpens, because a shortfall the quiet decades already
show cannot be credited to either break.

For the full-age classical cores under their native laws the realised
$e_{65}$ falls inside the nominal 95\% band in 0.425--0.500 of
population--sex cells under COVID and 0.455--0.636 across 1914--1922, with
$\annuity$ at 0.450--0.475 and 0.455--0.636 respectively---and at
0.256--0.654 and 0.253--0.643 in the stable control, on 442 units against
the shift regime's 40 and the placebo's 22. The shortfall is economically
material at both breaks and of the same order at each. For two of the three
cores it is no larger than the one the control already carries: SVD
Lee--Carter's annuity coverage is \emph{lowest} in the quiet control at
0.253, highest at the placebo at 0.636, with COVID in between at 0.450, and
Poisson-LC runs the same way on $\annuity$, 0.430 in the control against
0.475 and 0.591. APC/RH is the exception and is worse at both breaks than
in the control, its $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ coverage falling from 0.654 and
0.643 to 0.500 and 0.475 under COVID and to 0.455 at the placebo. CBD tells
the same story as Section~\ref{sec:results}---0.850 on $\annuity$ and 0.875 on $e_{65}$ under
COVID with its native law, 1.000 on both at the placebo, 0.889 and 0.930 in
the stable control---and its age-55 support lies above the excess mortality
of 1918. The $e_0$ column carries the timing caveat in numbers: at 0.909
for Lee--Carter at the placebo against 0.500 under the shift and 0.369 in
the control, the 1914 period table predates the influenza wave while 2020
sits inside the COVID shock. Outside the sparse VAR's nine-unit placebo
bootstrap row, which covers 1.000 on both functionals on the residue of a
row whose other thirteen units failed, neither break leaves the derived
intervals of a full-age family anywhere near nominal---but neither does the
control, and on the evidence of three regimes the pandemic is not
distinctive in economic kind, nor, for the worst-behaved families, even in
degree.
