\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\noindent\textit{Every table in this section is a generated fragment
(\texttt{paper/tables/<name>.tex}, one \texttt{booktabs} body per hook)
produced by the analysis stage from the per-cell results files; every
figure is a generated \texttt{figures/<name>.pdf}. No number in those
fragments is typed by hand; every figure quoted in the running prose below
is transcribed from them and was checked back against them. All three
regimes are final: every column is
read from its regime's two results files
(\texttt{results/<regime>.parquet} and
\texttt{results/<regime>\_gp.parquet}) after that regime's post-sweep QA
gate passed. Each table's note states the regime, the number of
populations, and---where a contrast is involved---the number of cells
dropped by the common-cell restriction of Addendum~3 (\S11).}

The study is complete. The three registered regimes were swept on the
same grid, through the same predictive-sample interface and the same
evaluation code path: the stable control (13 expanding origins,
1990--2014 in steps of two, $h=1,\dots,5$), the shift regime (train to
2019, test 2020--2024, $h=1,\dots,5$) and the placebo break (train to
1913, test 1914--1922, $h=1,\dots,9$). The stable sweep produced 26{,}000
cell rows of which 6{,}480 are error rows (24.9\%), the shift sweep 2{,}000
of which 210 (10.5\%), and the placebo sweep 1{,}100 of which 136
(12.4\%). No error row of any regime is a machine failure: the post-sweep
gate that refuses to generate a table while a broken-run row exists passed
on all three sets of final files. Every error row is classified in
Section~\ref{sec:results-infeasible} and excluded from every mean below.

The single sentence that organises the rest of this section is that the
stable control is not a quiet backdrop against which the break stands out.
Most of the miscalibration this paper measures is already present with
nothing broken, and the pandemic mainly re-orders the grid rather than
degrading it uniformly.

\subsection{Validation gates}

Gates 1--3 (Section~\ref{sec:design-gates}) passed before any real-data
result was read. The one substantive finding from gate~1---the mild
over-dispersion of two-stage SVD Lee--Carter on Poisson data---is carried
into the reading of the SVD-LC rows below. A fourth, post-sweep gate
(\texttt{scripts/final\_qa.py}) classifies every cell that produced no
valid row before any table is generated; it passed on all three regimes'
final files, with the machine class empty in each. Its output is the first
table of this section.

\subsection{What was scored: design-floor and method-failure cells}
\label{sec:results-infeasible}

% scripts/make_tables.py; its caption and label live in the fragment.

Table~\ref{tab:infeasible} lists every (population, family, mechanism) cell
of the primary grid that produced no valid row, by regime, in the classes
of the QA gate; the unabridged ledger, one row per affected cell with the
full recorded reason, is Appendix~\ref{app:infeasible}. Of the shift
regime's 210 error rows, 126 are structural, 56 method, 28 in the gate's
residual class and none machine. Of the stable regime's 6{,}480, 3{,}376
are structural, 1{,}106 method, 1{,}998 residual and none machine; the
share is larger than in the shift regime because thirteen expanding
origins reach back to 1990, and a design floor that binds at one origin
binds at every earlier one. Of the placebo regime's 136, 98 are method and
38 residual, with no structural row at all and none machine: it is the one
regime in which the registered design floor never binds, because the
eleven placebo populations were registered with continuous 1908--1922
coverage and a start year no later than 1900
(	exttt{PREREGISTRATION.md}), and---with the stable regime---one of the two in which
the method class is not the sparse VAR alone.

\emph{Structural} cells are design-floor cells: the panel is shorter than
the mechanism's calibration or ensemble-member requirement, or falls under
the registered admissibility floor of $n_{\text{train}}\ge 15$ complete
training years (Addendum~3, \S4). The 126 structural rows of the shift
regime trace the training lengths of Table~\ref{tab:populations} exactly:
KOR, the shortest shift panel, appears under every mechanism with a
calibration demand; HRV joins it already in the split arms of the three
families with the widest windows (the sparse VAR, CNN-LC and the LSTM),
and then in every family's EnbPI and copula arms, where the split
wrapper's single calibration block becomes ten staggered refits; CHL
enters under the largest windowed demands (the EnbPI and copula arms of
the sparse VAR, CNN-LC and LSTM), and the LSTM's construction alone adds
HKG. The same four populations, joined by TWN wherever the ten staggered
refits are required, account for most of the stable regime's structural
rows at the early origins, where their panels are one to five years long.
Four further short-panel populations---EST, LTU, LUX and LVA---enter the
structural class nowhere but the EnbPI and copula arms of CNN-LC and the
LSTM, the four heaviest cells in the design, contributing 56 of the 420
structural rows those cells carry between them; all four appear again in
the method class, under every arm of the sparse VAR. These cells are a property of the design, not a failure of
any method.

\emph{Method} cells are those in which a family's own sampler declines to
produce a predictive law on that panel under a registered rule, and two
registered rules can fire: the sparse VAR's explosive-coefficient-draw
rejection (Addendum~3, \S7---rejection and never clipping) and the
Poisson-composition overflow guard. In the shift regime the class belongs
to the sparse VAR alone. Its rejection rule fires on 11 rows over six
populations under the native law (CHL, HKG, HRV, JPN, KOR, TWN) and on
8--11 rows in each conformal arm, and its Poisson-bootstrap arm loses 15
rows over nine populations, 8 of them to that rejection and 7 to the
overflow guard on the longest panels (CHE, FIN, ISL, LUX, SWE). In the
stable regime the sparse VAR accounts for 1{,}105 of the 1{,}106 method
rows---1{,}027 rejected draws, over as many as fifteen populations in the
split-conformal arm, and 78 overflow rows in its bootstrap arm---and the
remaining row is a single CNN-LC ensemble failure in ISL, the overflow
guard again. The placebo divides the same way: 92 rejected-draw rows and 4
overflow rows, all sparse VAR, plus 2 CNN-LC rows in ITA under EnbPI and
the copula wrapper. The overflow guard therefore fires in all three
regimes---79, 7 and 6 rows---and always on the longest exposure panels,
where a bootstrap refit can push a fitted Poisson rate past the
composition sampler's representable range. Every one of these rows is read
below as a property of the family under its own registered rule, never as
a machine failure.

The residual class (\emph{other}) collects admissibility failures whose
floor is a family's own rather than the registered mechanism floor. All 28
shift-regime rows belong to the multi-output GP: cells whose trailing
block of complete years is shorter than the family's minimum training
block. In the stable regime the class has three sources: 1{,}416 rows in
which the origin precedes a population's first observed year, so no
training panel exists to fit (1{,}288 across the nine non-GP families and
128 in the GP); 574 GP rows under the family's trailing-block floor; and 8
rows in LUX where the banded VAR's own regression-row minimum binds.

That GP floor is worth stating in the open, because it changes what the GP
row of every table means. The multi-output GP needs forty training years:
its separable age$\times$time kernel is fitted by exact marginal
likelihood, and a shorter panel leaves the time-kernel length-scale
unidentified against the nugget (Section~\ref{sec:design-families}; the
sixty-year cap of Addendum~4 is Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}). The
forty-year floor---not the fifteen-year admissibility rule---is what
removes CHL, HKG, HRV and KOR from every GP cell. The GP family is
therefore read on sixteen populations in the shift regime where the other
nine families are read on twenty, and on sixteen in the stable regime
where the other nine are read on eighteen. Its means are not taken over
the same panel set as anyone else's, and no contrast in this paper crosses
that boundary without the common-cell restriction.

Error rows of every class are excluded from every mean in this paper, and
every contrast between arms below is computed on the intersection of cells
in which each compared arm produced a valid row, with the intersection
size reported (Addendum~3, \S11).

\subsection{Calibration where nothing breaks: the stable control}
\label{sec:results-stable}

The stable regime is the control the registration demanded, and it carries
the paper's first finding. Over 13 expanding origins from 1990 to 2014, on
panels with no structural break in them, with the horizons, the grid and
the code path of the shift regime, the classical intervals already cover
far below nominal. Under their own predictive law, SVD Lee--Carter covers
0.702 at the nominal 95\% level, Poisson Lee--Carter 0.682 and APC/RH
0.731; CBD reaches 0.901 on its 45-age support. The shortfall is not an
artefact of the tail level: SVD Lee--Carter covers 0.365 at a nominal 0.50
and 0.562 at 0.80, Poisson Lee--Carter 0.324 and 0.522. The point-forecast
neural mechanisms are worse still---the LSTM-$\kappa_t$ ensemble covers
0.638 and its dropout arm 0.667, the neural-LC ensemble 0.758---while the
arms that carry a count likelihood or a full posterior do not under-cover
in the quiet regime at all: the NB head covers 0.975 natively and 0.988
under the deep ensemble, the sparse VAR 0.988, CNN-LC under dropout 0.954
and the multi-output GP 0.945. The first three over-cover rather than sit
at nominal, and that slack is precisely what the break later consumes in
the \Htwo\ reading below.

The other registered failures are present in the control too. Joint path
coverage is below marginal in all fifty stable cells, with a mean gap of
0.127---a descriptive fact that is largely arithmetic, as
Section~\ref{sec:results-joint} insists, but whose level is not: 21 of the
50 control cells hold the whole five-year path less often than 0.774, the
rate a perfectly calibrated forecaster with independent horizons would
manage. The age profile of the Lee--Carter failure is already the profile
the break is supposed to produce: 0.888 at ages 0--24, 0.760 at 25--64 and
0.501 at 65--99 (Section~\ref{sec:results-age}). And for two of the arms that
break, the derived-quantity intervals of \Hfive---the quantities an
actuary actually quotes---are worse in the quiet regime than any
rate-level number in this section: SVD Lee--Carter under its native
law covers 0.253 for $\annuity$ at 2\% and 0.256 for $e_{65}$, and the
LSTM ensemble 0.157 for $\annuity$, against a nominal 0.95
(Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}). The propagation is not universal---in the
same control the sparse VAR's native law holds 0.980 for $\annuity$ and
0.983 for $e_{65}$, and CBD's bootstrap arm 0.934 for $\annuity$---but for
the index-extrapolating families an annuity factor computed from these
models carries essentially no reliable interval even when nothing breaks.

The consequence for the rest of the paper is a change of baseline. The
shift regime is not to be read against the nominal level, which most of
these arms never attained, but against this control. What the break does
to a family is the difference between its two columns, and that difference
turns out to depend on which side of nominal the family started.

\subsection{H1 as a harness-validity check: ranking by RMSE versus ranking by proper scores}

% tab-h1-rankings is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; its caption and label live in the fragment.
\inputtable{tab-h1-rankings}

Table~\ref{tab:h1-rankings} ranks the distributional arms---the
family--mechanism cells whose samples constitute a predictive
distribution---three times within each regime: by mean RMSE on log rates,
by mean CRPS on log rates and by mean Poisson log score. Two exclusions
are by construction. The conformal arms do not appear, because their CRPS,
log score and PIT are placeholder scores from uniform-in-interval samples
and are never ranked against a distributional mechanism. CBD is ranked in
a separate block: it is fit on ages 55--99 and therefore scored on 45
ages, and a mean over a different age support is not the same quantity as
a mean over ages 0--99. Means are taken within population--sex first and
then across populations, never exposure-weighted, and the ranking is
computed on the common cells of the block (22 of 40 units kept in the
shift regime; the dropped 18 trace the sparse VAR, GP and LSTM failure
cells of Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}).

\Hone\ as registered---rankings by RMSE and by CRPS disagree, with rank
correlation below one and at least one top-three inversion, in the shift
regime---is supported, in its mild form against CRPS and in a much
stronger form against the log score. The Spearman correlation between the
RMSE and CRPS orderings is $0.95$: below one, with the registered
top-three inversion present---the RMSE top three (LSTM-$\kappa_t$ under
dropout and ensemble, then SVD-LC native) share only one member with the
CRPS top three (the two sparse VAR arms, then LSTM dropout). Against the
Poisson log score the point-accuracy ordering nearly dissolves:
$\rho=0.12$. The arms with the best RMSE are among the worst by log
score---the LSTM ensemble is 2nd by RMSE and 16th of 18 by log score,
SVD-LC native 3rd and 12th---while the NB head, mid-pack by RMSE (ranks
10--12), takes the top three log-score positions together with CNN-LC
dropout, and neural-LC dropout, last by RMSE, is 5th by log score. A
point-accuracy league table and a density-forecast league table of the
same arms over the same break are close to unrelated, which is the
inversion the published literature reports
\citep{barigou2023bayesian,goes2025bayesian,schnurch2022point} recurring
under pre-registration.
% Intervals: results/h5_extras.json (scripts/h5_extras.py) -- cluster
% bootstrap resampling populations with replacement on the tab-h1
% common-cell block, B = 2000, seed 20260830.
Both correlations carry the cluster-bootstrap interval of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference}, obtained by resampling with
replacement the thirteen populations that survive the common-cell
restriction and recomputing the block's means, ranks and correlations in
each of 2{,}000 replicates: $[0.77, 0.96]$ for $\rho(\text{RMSE},
\text{CRPS})$ and $[-0.16, 0.21]$ for $\rho(\text{RMSE}, \text{log
score})$. The first interval excludes one, so the registered
below-one clause does not depend on which populations entered the
block; the second contains zero, so at the resolution thirteen
correlated clusters afford, the log-score ordering is indistinguishable
from one unrelated to the RMSE ordering.

The other two regimes say that this is a property of the metrics, not of
the break. In the stable control, on the 217 of 520 units the common-cell
restriction keeps, the two correlations are $0.81$ and $-0.18$; in the
placebo, on 9 of 22 units, $0.85$ and $0.16$. The RMSE-versus-log-score
divergence is if anything wider with nothing broken than through COVID.
\Hone\ is retained as what it was registered to be: a validity check on
the harness, confirming that the evaluation is measuring the
distributional object and not a point forecast in disguise, in every
regime.

\subsection{H2: marginal coverage across the break}

% tab-h2-coverage is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; its caption and label live in the fragment.
\inputtable{tab-h2-coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} reports, for every admissible family--mechanism
cell and every regime, the mean empirical coverage at 50, 80 and 95\%, the
mean 95\% interval score, and the number of cells behind each mean;
conformal rows carry the 95\% columns only, because a conformal wrapper
constructs one interval at one level and its coverage and interval score
are read from that interval's bounds. The interval score is the
width-penalised companion that keeps a vague interval from passing as a
calibrated one.

\Htwo\ as registered has a one-sided form and a two-sided restatement, and
the two verdicts differ. \textbf{The one-sided form---nominal 95\%
intervals under-cover in the shift regime \emph{for every model
family}---is not supported.} The classical cores under their own
predictive law do under-cover, severely: Poisson Lee--Carter 0.624,
APC/RH 0.654, SVD-LC 0.692, CBD 0.799 on its 45-age support. But the
distributional NB head covers 0.933 natively, 0.956 under the deep
ensemble and 0.972 under dropout; CNN-LC under dropout covers 0.962; the
multi-output GP 0.905; the sparse VAR 0.937--0.938 on the cells its
rejection rule admits. Several of those arms sit at or above nominal
through the break, so the direction of the miscalibration is not even
uniformly downward, and the registered universal fails on its own terms.

The two-sided quantity registered in Addendum~3 (\S8),
$|\text{coverage}-0.95|$, hypothesised to grow from the stable control to
the shift regime, gives the sharper verdict. It grows in 27 of the 50
admissible cells---a bare majority, not a general degradation. Raw
coverage falls in 34 of 50, which is the number a paper without a control
would have reported as damage; but a fall is a degradation only for a cell
that began below nominal, and 16 of the 50 cells began above it.

What the break does is sort the grid by which side of nominal each arm
started on. Of the sixteen arms that over-covered in the stable control,
eleven move \emph{towards} nominal under COVID: the sparse VAR's native
law from 0.988 to 0.937, its Poisson-bootstrap arm from 0.996 to 0.938,
the NB head from 0.988 to 0.956 under the deep ensemble, from 0.988 to
0.972 under dropout and from 0.975 to 0.933 natively. For these families
the pandemic is not a stress test that they fail; it consumes the slack
their own predictive law had built in. Of the thirty-four arms at or below
nominal in the control, twenty-two move further away: Poisson Lee--Carter
from 0.682 to 0.624, APC/RH from 0.731 to 0.654, CBD from 0.901 to 0.799,
SVD Lee--Carter from 0.702 to 0.692. The registered expectation was that
the break would push the whole grid off nominal; what it does is spread
the grid apart from both sides.

Read against the control, then, the honest statement of the shift-regime
result is not that COVID broke these intervals. Poisson Lee--Carter's
native band missed 0.318 of observations before the pandemic existed and
0.376 during it. The pandemic contributes the last six percentage points
of a thirty-three-point shortfall from nominal.

The registration deliberately declined a directional claim on
neural-versus-classical ordering, and the working conjecture that
motivated the audit---coverage degradation larger for the neural
families---is refuted in its strong form, in both regimes. The split does
not run along the architecture class at all: LSTM-$\kappa_t$ (0.638
ensemble and 0.667 dropout in the control, 0.647 and 0.685 under the
break) and the neural-LC ensemble (0.758 and 0.694) break exactly like the
classical cores, while their sibling neural arms with a count likelihood
or dropout smoothing (NB head, CNN-LC dropout) are the best-covered arms
in the study in every regime. What separates the broken arms from the
calibrated ones is the family--mechanism pair, not the presence of a
neural network---the finding the crossed design exists to expose, taken up
in Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}.

The interval score orders the same failure and, again, orders it the same
way in both regimes: the broken natives pay twice, in missed observations
and in width---Poisson Lee--Carter native 3.056 in the control and 4.456
under the break, neural-LC ensemble 3.925 and 4.321, APC/RH 2.398 and
3.401, against NB native 1.455 and 1.684 and CNN-LC dropout 1.653 and
1.677. The largest interval score in the table, neural-LC dropout at 5.559
under the break with coverage 0.811 (5.068 and 0.816 in the control), is
an arm buying part of its coverage with indiscriminate width. Wrapping any
family in a conformal band moves its 95\% coverage into a narrow range
regardless of how broken the native law is: in the control 0.890--0.948
for split and EnbPI and 0.951--0.985 for the copula arm, under the break
0.848--0.944 and 0.939--0.981---the repair whose cost is priced in
Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} and, crucially, a repair whose sign is the same in
both regimes.

\subsection{H3: joint path coverage}
\label{sec:results-joint}

% tab-h3-joint is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; its caption and label live in the fragment.
\inputtable{tab-h3-joint}

Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint} sets, for each family--mechanism cell, mean
marginal coverage at 95\% beside mean joint path coverage at the same
nominal level and the gap between the two: the difference between the
coverage a mortality paper reports and the coverage a liability depends
on. Joint coverage lies below marginal in 100 of 100 (regime, cell)
pairs---all fifty admissible arms in the stable control and all fifty
under the break, without a single exception---with a mean gap of 0.127 in
the control and 0.168 under the break. That count is descriptive and
largely arithmetic, and it is reported as such rather than as a result.
Joint coverage is bounded above by the smallest marginal coverage on the
path, and at nominal 0.95 over five horizons independence alone would give
$0.95^5 = 0.774$, a gap of 0.176 that is \emph{larger} than either
observed mean. Section~\ref{sec:design-status} and
Section~\ref{sec:metrics-coverage} call the registered marginal comparison
near-tautological; nothing in this table contradicts them, and the
universality of the sign is not evidence of miscalibration.

The informative benchmark is not the marginal rate but independence
\emph{at each arm's own marginal rate}: an arm covering $c$ marginally
would hold $c^{H}$ of its paths if its horizons were independent. Measured
against that, almost every arm does better than independence---in 50 of 50
stable cells and 50 of 50 shift cells, by between $+0.007$ and $+0.344$ in
the control and between $+0.014$ and $+0.328$ under the break, and in 45 of
the 46 placebo cells over nine horizons, the exception being the sparse
VAR's bootstrap arm at 0.748 against a benchmark of 0.756. Mortality forecast
errors are strongly positively dependent down the path: a trajectory
inside its band at $h=1$ is far likelier than chance to be inside it at
$h=5$, because the same drift error persists across every horizon of the
same path. That dependence is what \citet{dowd2010backtesting} named in a
footnote and did not measure, and measuring it across ten families, seven
mechanisms and three regimes is what \Hthree\ contributes here. It also
means the marginal-to-joint gap cannot be read as miscalibration on its
own: most of it is arithmetic, and the dependence works in the
forecaster's favour, not against it.

What is supportable as a practical statement is the level of joint
coverage, not the size of the gap. Joint path coverage falls below 0.774
in 21 of the 50 stable cells and 30 of the 50 shift cells. For those arms
a reader of the marginal column is being sold a band that fails to hold
the whole five-year path more often than a genuinely nominal pointwise
construction would fail even with no horizon dependence helping it, and
nine more cells sit below that line under the break than in the control.

For the broken classical natives the collapse is near-total in both
regimes: Poisson Lee--Carter falls from 0.682 marginal to 0.422 joint in
the control and from 0.624 to 0.298 under the break, SVD-LC from 0.702 to
0.495 and from 0.692 to 0.417, APC/RH from 0.731 to 0.465 and from 0.654
to 0.330, CBD from 0.901 to 0.781 and from 0.799 to 0.504. The gap is not
a symptom of bad marginal calibration that good calibration removes: the
best-covered marginal arms lose it too---the NB dropout arm falls from
0.988 to 0.956 in the control and from 0.972 to 0.897 under the break,
CNN-LC dropout from 0.954 to 0.855 and from 0.962 to 0.870, the sparse VAR
natively from 0.988 to 0.951 and from 0.937 to 0.819---so an arm whose
reported marginal coverage is nominal can still miss whole trajectories at
a rate no reader of its marginal column would expect---from roughly one in
twenty for the best-covered arms to one in five for the worst of them, the
NB head's native law under the break, whose 0.933 marginal coverage sits
against 0.795 joint.

The one mechanism constructed to be joint over $h=1,\dots,H$ behaves as
constructed, and does so in both regimes: the copula-conformal arm holds
joint path coverage between 0.894 and 0.942 across the ten families in the
control, with gaps of $-0.043$ to $-0.064$, and between 0.805 (CBD) and
0.927 (SVD-LC) under the break, with gaps of $-0.049$ to $-0.136$---the
smallest in its family block in every family but the NB head, whose
dropout arm's gap under the break, $-0.075$, is narrower still.

The registered comparator itself is not available, and the verdict on
\Hthree\ has to be stated with that absence in it. Addendum~3 (\S8)
re-specified the \Hthree\ comparator as each cell's own
simulated-path-implied joint coverage, computed from the same predictive
paths, so that the realised-minus-implied gap could take either sign; the
runner's column contract carries realised joint coverage but does not emit
the implied quantity, as Section~\ref{sec:metrics-coverage} records rather
than carries silently. That quantity is not recoverable from the columns
the runner wrote, so the registered paired test is not run and \Hthree\ is
not adjudicated in the form it was registered in. What stands in its place
is two measurements with different standing. The marginal-to-joint gap is
descriptive, near-tautological in sign, and labelled as such. The
comparison against each arm's own independence benchmark is neither, and
it is the measurement of horizon dependence the prior literature left
unmade. The implied comparator is recomputable from the recorded per-row
seeds and would split the residual part of the gap into horizon dependence
and miscalibration; it is declared unfinished business rather than an
omission discovered after the fact.

\subsection{H4: where in age the intervals fail}
\label{sec:results-age}

% tab-h4-age is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; its caption and label live in the fragment.
\inputtable{tab-h4-age}

\Hfour\ as registered---shift-regime coverage at ages 65--99 lower than at
25--64 \emph{for every family}---is not supported as a universal; the age
profile of the failure is family-specific. It is supported, sharply and
consistently, for the full-age families whose extrapolated period index
\emph{also} broke in Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}. SVD Lee--Carter falls
from 0.882 at ages 0--24 to 0.725 at 25--64 and 0.518 at 65--99 under the
break; APC/RH from 0.732 to 0.661 to 0.589; and the LSTM ensemble most
steeply of all, 0.893 to 0.687 to 0.426. For these arms the old ages are
where the interval fails, and they fail there by a wide margin: an
interval sold as 95\% contains the realised old-age rate in about half of
the cells.

The control settles what the break contributed. The same gradient is
already there with nothing broken---SVD Lee--Carter runs 0.888 / 0.760 /
0.501 across the three bands in the stable regime and the LSTM ensemble
0.858 / 0.700 / 0.409---so the old-age concentration is a standing
property of index extrapolation rather than a pandemic effect. What
2020--2024 adds is small: Lee--Carter's old-age coverage moves from 0.501
to 0.518, and the LSTM ensemble's from 0.409 to 0.426. Both move
\emph{towards} nominal, because a band that already misses half its old-age
outcomes has little room left to lose.

This is the one hypothesis whose registered direction the prior literature
disputed. \citet{dowd2010backtesting}, backtesting six classical models on
one population, find the age-65 forecasts biased upward with realised
values at or below the lower bound while the age-84 forecasts show ``only
a very slight upward bias'' (Section~\ref{sec:related})---the higher age
behaving better, which is the opposite of the registered \Hfour\ direction.
Across three regimes, ten families and up to twenty populations---twenty in
the shift regime and sixteen there for the GP, eighteen in the stable
control for the nine non-GP families and again sixteen for the GP, eleven
in the placebo---we find the reverse for the full-age index-extrapolating
cores, and we find it in quiet years as well as across the break.

The reversal is nonetheless common enough to be worth stating plainly:
under the break, coverage at 65--99 exceeds coverage at 25--64 in 34 of the
50 cells. Most of those are conformal arms, whose Mondrian strata are
calibrated on these bands separately and which therefore flatten or invert
the profile by construction; but eight of the twenty arms carrying a
family's own uncertainty reverse it too---CBD natively (0.849 at 65--99
against 0.626 at its 55--64 stub) and under the bootstrap (0.876 against
0.668), CNN-LC under dropout (0.985 against 0.944) and as an ensemble
(0.880 against 0.847), the multi-output GP (0.909 against 0.900),
neural-LC under dropout (0.934 against 0.874), and Poisson Lee--Carter
natively (0.639 against 0.602) and under the bootstrap (0.664 against
0.619). Five of those eight reverse only in the arithmetic: Poisson
Lee--Carter is uniformly poor across all three bands under the break
(0.639 / 0.602 / 0.639) and in the control (0.721 / 0.664 / 0.676), its
bootstrap arm likewise, the GP is flat and near-nominal (0.908 / 0.900 /
0.909), and CNN-LC's two arms reverse by 0.041 and 0.033. A margin of five
hundredths or less is no age profile at all, in either direction.

CBD is excluded from the supported class for a different reason. It
extrapolates two period indices by a bivariate random walk with drift and
its native interval broke (0.901 to 0.799), so on the stated criterion it
belongs there; but it is fitted on ages 55--99, so its 25--64 column is a
55--64 stub and its bands are not comparable with the full-age families'.
Its reversal is also not a break effect: it is already present in the
control, 0.913 at 65--99 against 0.861 at the stub and 0.930 against 0.888
under the bootstrap. What COVID does to CBD is deepen an old-age advantage
its restricted support already gave it rather than create one. The
neural-LC ensemble is the remaining exception in the other direction: it
covers only 0.619 at ages 0--24 against 0.793 and 0.633 above, so its
failure is concentrated at the young ages, where the exposure is largest
and the deaths fewest.

% tab-murphy is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by

\inputtable{tab-murphy}

Table~\ref{tab:pit} reports, for the distributional arms, the mean KS
distance of the randomised PIT from the uniform and the share of cells
whose nominal KS $p$-value is below $0.05$; the $p$-value is descriptive
only, because PIT values across ages and horizons within a cell are
dependent, and the shape of each departure is read from
Figure~\ref{fig:pit-hist}. The ordering matches
Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} in the shift regime---the smallest KS
distances belong to the GP (0.172), the NB head (0.182--0.189) and the
sparse VAR (0.188), the largest to Poisson Lee--Carter (0.374) and APC/RH
(0.325)---but the levels carry the sharper message: no arm is
distributionally calibrated through the break, and no arm is
distributionally calibrated without one either. The calibrated end of the
ordering is the same in the control---sparse VAR 0.166, the NB head
0.172--0.194, the GP 0.192---but the poorly-calibrated end re-orders
between the regimes. In the control the three largest distances belong to
the neural arms (LSTM ensemble 0.335, LSTM dropout 0.322,
CNN-LC ensemble 0.291), with SVD-LC at 0.279, Poisson Lee--Carter at 0.278
and APC/RH mid-pack at 0.227; under the break the Poisson-likelihood
classicals take the worst places (Poisson Lee--Carter 0.374 and 0.365,
APC/RH 0.325 and 0.319) and the LSTM arms fall back to 0.287 and 0.264.
Which arms are worst calibrated distributionally is therefore not a fixed
property: a PIT ranking read off one regime does not transfer to the
other, even though the well-calibrated end does. The share of cells whose
nominal KS $p$-value falls
below 0.05 never drops beneath 0.905 in the control or 0.844 under the
break, sitting at 0.925--1.000 for most arms in both. Coverage at a single
level is a much weaker property than PIT uniformity: the NB head's 95\%
interval can be essentially nominal while its predictive distribution,
read over all levels at once, is still rejected as uniform in nearly every
cell, in a quiet decade as much as through a pandemic.

Table~\ref{tab:murphy} decomposes the Brier score of the 95\% hit
indicator: with a constant forecast probability there is one bin, so
reliability is the squared coverage gap, resolution is zero by
construction, and uncertainty is the base-rate term. The reliability
column spans two orders of magnitude across arms in both regimes---under
the break 0.1515 for Poisson Lee--Carter native, 0.1311 for APC/RH and
0.1226 for the LSTM ensemble against 0.0015 for CNN-LC dropout, 0.0022 for
NB dropout and 0.0012--0.0223 for the conformal rows; in the control
0.1324 for the LSTM ensemble, 0.1142 for Poisson Lee--Carter and 0.0921
for SVD-LC against 0.0013 for the NB head natively, 0.0016 for the sparse
VAR and 0.0021 for CNN-LC dropout---which restates the coverage table as a
proper-score share and shows the same spread with no break present. The
PIT-scale reliability beside it, the chi-square-type distance from
uniformity that a single level's coverage cannot see, orders the arms the
same way (under the break Poisson Lee--Carter 0.1904 down to NB dropout
0.0302; in the control the LSTM ensemble 0.1427 and Poisson Lee--Carter
0.1197 down to NB head 0.0232) but never approaches zero, in agreement
with the KS column of Table~\ref{tab:pit}: the calibrated arms are
calibrated at 95\%, not everywhere. The two decompositions reported are
the two the harness emits, and they are the two Section~\ref{sec:metrics}
specifies: Murphy's one-bin partition of the interval-hit Brier score and
the divergence form on the PIT scale. The isotonic (CORP) estimator is not
emitted and no hypothesis rests on it.

\subsection{Forecast comparison: Diebold--Mariano and the model confidence set}

% tab-dm-mcs is a page-spanning longtable fragment generated by
% scripts/make_tables.py; it carries its own \caption and \label{tab:dm-mcs}.
\inputtable{tab-dm-mcs}

Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} shows the two registered inference procedures in
all three regimes, and its loss column is the reason it is one table: a
contrast among distributional arms is decided on CRPS on log rates, while
any contrast that includes a conformal arm is decided on the per-horizon
95\% interval score, because a conformal cell's CRPS is a placeholder.
Every entry is computed on the intersection of cells in which each
compared arm produced a valid row, and the kept and dropped counts are
part of the table.

The model confidence set over the classical families under their own
predictive law is formed over the four full-age families only: the
five-family set including CBD is skipped in every regime, with its reason
recorded in the table, because a mean CRPS over 45 ages is not comparable
with one over 100 and the registration forbids working around an
incomparable loss. In the control, on 297 common cells, the 90\% set
retains the sparse VAR's native arm alone, eliminating SVD-LC, Poisson
Lee--Carter and APC/RH at $p=0.03$ each. Under the break, on 29 common
cells, it retains \{SVD-LC native, sparse VAR native\} (elimination
$p$-values 0.13 and 1.00), eliminating Poisson Lee--Carter and APC/RH at
$p<0.0005$---with the caveat, carried from Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}, that
the sparse VAR is scored only on the cells its own rejection rule admits.
The set widens under the break because thirteen origins of evidence are
replaced by one, not because the eliminated families improved.

The within-family conformal sets separate the wrappers far more sharply
under the break than in the control, and the pattern is informative. In
the control, seven of the ten families retain all three wrappers---the
quiet regime cannot tell a split band, an EnbPI band and a copula band
apart on the single-level interval score---with only CBD and the sparse
VAR reduced to EnbPI alone and the NB head to \{copula, EnbPI\}. Under the
break the sets collapse: in five families---SVD-LC, LSTM-$\kappa_t$, NB
head, neural-LC and sparse VAR---EnbPI is the sole survivor, eliminating
the split and copula arms at $p$-values between 0.00 and 0.08; in Poisson
Lee--Carter the copula arm is the sole survivor ($p=0.01$ against both
others); and in CBD, CNN-LC, the GP and APC/RH all three wrappers are
retained. Read with Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint}, the EnbPI wins are a width
result, not a calibration result: the interval score at a single level
rewards the narrowest band that roughly covers, which EnbPI's residual
recycling produces, while the copula arm's wider joint band is what
actually holds path coverage. The two registered procedures answer
different questions and disagree accordingly.

The Diebold--Mariano block prices the conformal repair of
Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} and is the attribution finding of the crossed
design. The contrast is defined for the seven families with a native arm,
and its most important property is that its sign is the same in the
control as under the break, family by family, without exception. Under
the break, split conformal is significantly \emph{better} than the native
law for exactly the four classical cores whose native intervals broke:
Poisson Lee--Carter ($\Delta=1.895$, $p=0.004$), APC/RH ($\Delta=1.039$,
$p=0.013$), SVD-LC ($\Delta=0.700$, $p=0.018$) and CBD ($\Delta=0.318$,
$p=0.017$), with $G=19$ population clusters each. It is significantly
\emph{worse} for exactly the three families whose native law was already
calibrated: the NB head ($\Delta=-1.024$, $p<0.0002$, $G=19$), the
multi-output GP ($\Delta=-0.413$, $p=0.009$, $G=16$) and the sparse VAR
($\Delta=-0.318$, $p<0.0002$, $G=14$). In the control the same seven signs
appear: $\Delta=+1.038$ for Poisson Lee--Carter ($p=0.032$), $+0.694$ for
SVD-LC ($p=0.006$), $+0.451$ for APC/RH ($p=0.086$) and $+0.108$ for CBD
($p=0.198$), against $-0.873$ for the NB head, $-0.772$ for the GP and
$-0.236$ for the sparse VAR, all three at $p\le0.0002$. No family is left
where the wrapper is free: conformalisation is insurance that pays out
precisely when the model's own uncertainty was wrong and costs a premium
precisely when it was right. Which side of the line a family falls on is a
property of the family--mechanism pair, not of the wrapper and not of the
pandemic---the sharpest form of the study's central claim that
architecture and uncertainty mechanism are separate factors.

\subsection{Twin crises: 1914--1922 beside 2020--2024}

placebo strata of Addendum~1 as a further column. Joint coverage spans
$H=9$ placebo and $H=5$ shift horizons---not one scale. The sparse

\label{tab:twin-crises}
\inputtable{tab-twin-crises}
\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:twin-crises} sets the 1914--1922 placebo beside the
2020--2024 shift. Read within regime (the joint columns span $H=9$
against $H=5$), the placebo reproduces the shift regime's family split a
century earlier: the full-age classical cores under-cover (Lee--Carter
0.787, Poisson Lee--Carter 0.780, APC/RH 0.761 marginal against a
nominal 0.95), the point-forecast neural mechanisms are worst
(neural-LC ensemble 0.661, LSTM 0.636), and the families that came
through COVID calibrated sit near the top of the placebo ordering too
(NB head 0.849, multi-output GP 0.820). Joint path coverage collapses
in the same order---0.146 (neural-LC ensemble), 0.172 (LSTM),
0.293--0.335 (the classical cores) over nine horizons---so the
marginal-to-joint gap of \Hthree\ is not a COVID artefact either, though
over nine horizons its descriptive part is more nearly arithmetic still
($0.95^9 = 0.630$), and the informative reading is the one
Section~\ref{sec:results-joint} gives: 45 of the 46 placebo arms beat
independence at their own marginal rate, and 37 of them nonetheless hold
the nine-year path less often than 0.630. Sparse VAR is the one family
with no placebo verdict under its native law, and none under any conformal
wrapper: on all but one cell per arm the pre-1914 native draws tripped the
registered explosive-path rejection, and the remaining cell fails the
admissibility rule, leaving zero valid rows in four of its five arms
(Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}). Only its Poisson-bootstrap arm survives, on
nine rows, where it covers 0.969 marginally against 0.748 jointly---the
closest to nominal of any placebo arm, though on the thinnest evidence in
the table, and closer than CBD's own-law arms only because those
over-cover (0.986--0.988). The four empty arms also empty the common-cell intersection
(Addendum~3, \S11) of the registered classical model confidence set in
this regime.

Against the stable control the placebo makes the sorting mechanism of
\Htwo\ visible from the other side, with the caveat that the two regimes
share neither populations, era nor horizon count. The classical cores
cover \emph{better} through the 1914--1922 break than in the quiet
1990--2014 decades---Lee--Carter 0.787 against 0.702, Poisson Lee--Carter
0.780 against 0.682, APC/RH 0.761 against 0.731, CBD 0.986 against
0.901---because a model estimated on the volatile pre-1914 panels
produces much wider bands, which is a form of accidental calibration
rather than a better law. The arms that were calibrated in the control
lose the most: the NB head falls from 0.975 to 0.849 and the GP from
0.945 to 0.820. Coverage moves towards the middle from both directions
here as it does under COVID; the crisis re-orders the grid rather than
degrading it uniformly, which is the same finding in a different century.

The exception is CBD, and it is the most instructive cell in the table:
near-nominal through the placebo break (0.986 marginal, 0.918 joint)
and broken through the COVID one (0.799, 0.504). The 1918 influenza
concentrated its excess mortality at young-adult ages, below CBD's
age-55 support, while COVID's excess fell at the old ages CBD models.
Whether an interval survives a crisis therefore depends not only on the
family's dynamics but on where in the age range the crisis lands---the
age-localisation of miscalibration that \Hfour\ establishes \emph{within}
the shift regime, operating here \emph{between} regimes.

The Addendum~1 strata sharpen the reading. In the neutral
register-based populations the classical cores hold 0.86--0.89---the
1918 flu alone bends them without breaking them---while the
belligerent-total stratum, where war losses compound the flu, drops the
same families to 0.55--0.59, with the civilian-only stratum in between.
Within one family and one regime, coverage failure scales with the size
of the break.

The paired mechanism contrasts, however, do not transfer. The
split-conformal rescue that is decisive under COVID---and, as
Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts} shows, already decisive in the
quiet control---is absent here: on the interval score, native versus
split is insignificant for every full-age classical core (Lee--Carter
$\Delta=-0.091$, $p=0.689$; Poisson Lee--Carter $p=0.127$; APC/RH
$p=0.563$), and the NB head's native law significantly beats its own
wrapper ($\Delta=-0.461$, $p=0.008$)---a calibration window drawn from
the pre-1914 years knows as little about the coming shock as the model it
corrects. The ensemble-versus-dropout verdicts flip family by family
between the two crises: CNN-LC, indistinguishable under COVID
($p=0.739$), favours dropout here ($\Delta=+0.531$, $p=0.0002$); LSTM,
which favours dropout under COVID ($p=0.0006$), is indistinguishable
here ($p=0.478$). A family's fragility is predictable from its past
crisis; which uncertainty mechanism patches it best is not.

\subsection{Architecture or mechanism? Contrasts within sub-grids}
\label{sec:results-contrasts}

The payoff of the crossed design. Each contrast below is pre-registered and
computed as a paired difference within the admissible sub-grid named, on
the common cells of that sub-grid, and each is now read in the stable
control as well as under the break.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Conformal versus native.} Does wrapping a fit in a conformal
  band change its interval score and coverage? The native versus
  split-conformal block of Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} carries this contrast,
  per family, on the interval score, and its answer is signed by the
  family and \emph{identical in sign in both regimes}: the wrapper
  improves the four classical cores whose native law was broken
  (Lee--Carter $\Delta=+0.694$ in the control against $+0.700$ under the
  break; Poisson Lee--Carter $+1.038$ against $+1.895$; APC/RH $+0.451$
  against $+1.039$; CBD $+0.108$ against $+0.318$) and worsens the NB
  head, the GP and the sparse VAR, whose native law was not ($-0.873$
  against $-1.024$; $-0.772$ against $-0.413$; $-0.236$ against
  $-0.318$). The break changes the significance of two of the seven---CBD
  from $p=0.198$ to $p=0.017$ and APC/RH from $p=0.086$ to $p=0.013$---and
  the magnitude of the repair for the Poisson families, but it changes no
  family's side. Conformalisation is therefore not a pandemic remedy: it
  helps families whose native law was wrong and costs families whose law
  was right, in quiet decades exactly as through a break. The width side
  of the question is read from the interval-score column of
  Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}, and again does not depend on the regime:
  for the broken families the wrapper buys its coverage while
  \emph{shrinking} the score (Poisson Lee--Carter 3.056 native against
  2.056 split in the control, 4.456 against 2.674 under the break), for
  the calibrated ones it pays in width (NB head 1.455 native against 2.335
  split in the control, 1.684 against 2.717 under the break).
\item \emph{Ensemble versus Monte Carlo dropout, neural families only.} Do
  two mechanisms on the same architecture give the same coverage?
  Descriptively, Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} answers no---on the same
  CNN-LC architecture, dropout covers 0.962 against the ensemble's 0.882
  under the break and 0.954 against 0.863 in the control, and on neural-LC
  0.811 against 0.694 and 0.816 against 0.758. The registered paired test
  agrees and adds a sign that the descriptive reading does not supply: on
  the interval score the two mechanisms differ significantly, \emph{in
  opposite directions}, on two of the four neural families under the
  break. Dropout is favoured on LSTM-on-$\kappa_t$ ($\Delta = +0.224$,
  $t = 3.78$, $p = 0.0006$) and the ensemble on neural-LC
  ($\Delta = -1.238$, $t = -4.18$, $p < 0.0002$), while CNN-LC
  ($p = 0.739$) and the NB head ($p = 0.139$) are indistinguishable. All
  four signs are the same in the control, on 434--442 common cells rather
  than 38--40 (LSTM $+0.156$, $p=0.005$; neural-LC $-1.142$, $p=0.0002$;
  NB head $+0.049$, $p=0.019$; CNN-LC $+0.001$, $p=0.991$), so the
  opposing directions are a property of the family, not of the break. The
  mechanism is not separable from the architecture it wraps: no single
  ordering of the two survives a change of family, which is the crossed
  design's reason for existing.
\item \emph{Bootstrap versus native, classical families only.} Does adding
  parameter uncertainty move a classical interval toward nominal?
  Descriptively the movement is positive and small in both regimes
  (SVD-LC 0.702 to 0.726 in the control and 0.692 to 0.727 under the
  break, CBD 0.901 to 0.921 and 0.799 to 0.830 in
  Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}), nowhere near the repair a conformal
  wrapper delivers. The registered paired test makes the smallness
  precise: under the break the semiparametric bootstrap improves the
  interval score significantly on three of the five classical families---
  SVD-LC ($\Delta = -0.112$, $p < 0.0002$), Poisson Lee--Carter
  ($\Delta = -0.115$, $p < 0.0002$) and Renshaw--Haberman
  ($\Delta = -0.101$, $p = 0.0002$)---leaves CBD unchanged ($p = 0.681$),
  and significantly \emph{worsens} sparse VAR ($\Delta = +0.168$,
  $p = 0.0008$, on the 22 cells where its native arm survives the
  explosive-draw rejection of Addendum~3~\S7). The control gives the same
  three improvements at the same order of magnitude ($-0.100$, $-0.073$
  and $-0.075$, each at $p \le 0.0002$, on 442 cells) and the same sparse
  VAR penalty ($+0.237$, $p < 0.0002$, on 223 cells), with CBD favouring
  its native arm there ($+0.096$, $p = 0.016$). The three improvements are
  an order of magnitude smaller than the conformal repair of the same
  families in the contrast above (for Poisson Lee--Carter, $-0.115$ against
  $+1.895$ in favour of the wrapper): resampling the estimator moves a
  broken interval, but it does not fix it, and it does not fix it in quiet
  periods either.
\item \emph{Joint-path conformal versus split conformal, all families.} The
  \Hthree\ mechanism contrast: the within-family conformal confidence sets
  of Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} on the interval score, read with the joint
  column of Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint}. The two tables pull in opposite
  directions by construction: the copula arm holds joint path coverage of
  0.894--0.942 in the control and 0.805--0.927 under the break, where the
  split arm holds 0.760--0.842 and 0.692--0.862, yet on the single-level
  interval score the copula arm survives as sole set member only for
  Poisson Lee--Carter under the break and is eliminated in five families
  where EnbPI's narrower band wins. A practitioner who needs the path pays
  for it at the level; the study reports the price rather than
  adjudicating it.
\end{enumerate}
