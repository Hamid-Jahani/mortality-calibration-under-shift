\subsection{Pre-registration and its addenda}
\label{sec:design-prereg}

The design was written down, committed and hash-stamped on 2026-08-25,
before any model was fit to real HMD data; its SHA-256 digest is
\begin{center}
\small\texttt{bbbe3860a5446d063e186e56555afa893b07c36cf15a990d07567471affe50be}
\end{center}
The registration fixes the analytic choices: regimes and their exact years,
population lists, the model families and mechanisms, the admissible grid,
every metric and its convention, the inference procedure, the validation
gates, and the five hypotheses. It does not, and could not, hide the outcome
of 2020--2024 from us; pre-registration here protects against analytic
flexibility, not against hypothesis surprise.

Three addenda were registered on 2026-08-26, each hash-stamped in its own
commit and each still before any real-data fit. None changes a population
list, a split year, a horizon, a test-window observation or the metric
list. \emph{Addendum~1} (commit \texttt{5f2e3cb}) adds the placebo strata
and the denominator sensitivities of Section~\ref{sec:data}.
\emph{Addendum~2} (commit \texttt{577ce52}) fixes the scoring target: every
rate-scale score evaluates the predictive law of the \emph{observed} crude
rate, conformal cells are scored from their own interval bounds at their
construction level, and the PIT uniformity $p$-value is descriptive
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). \emph{Addendum~3} (commit \texttt{01f154a})
specifies the computation on cases the original text covers but does not
spell out---structural zeros, training-window contiguity, an admissibility
floor, the rounding of the rate-scale truth, rejection of explosive VAR
draws, the conformal calibration horizon, zero-death reporting and the
common-cell rule for contrasts---and records, with the prior-art map of
Section~\ref{sec:related} as dated evidence, the hypothesis-status
clarifications of Section~\ref{sec:design-status}. Each clause is cited
where it applies. Every subsequent deviation is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}.

The \emph{stable} regime measures calibration where nothing breaks; it is
the reference level against which \Htwo\ and \Hfive\ are stated, and,
because its half of \Htwo\ is already settled in the literature
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), it is labelled a replication arm. The
\emph{shift} regime is the audit. The \emph{placebo} regime is a second
event---the First World War and the 1918 influenza pandemic---with a longer
horizon, used descriptively: a side-by-side of 1914--1922 and 2020--2024
coverage answers ``is this just COVID?'' without a transfer regression.
Every window is expanding-origin; training always uses the maximal
contiguous block of complete years ending at the origin, from the
population's own first year, and a triple (origin, population, sex) is
admissible only with at least fifteen such years (Addendum~3~\S2, \S4).
Inadmissible triples are recorded as such and not scored. In the stable
regime that floor is not a marginal trim: HRV's series begins in 2001 and
KOR's in 2003, so even their best origin ($T=2014$) yields fourteen and
twelve training years---below the floor at every origin. The stable
control therefore covers eighteen of the twenty registered populations,
while both populations remain present throughout the shift regime. CHL
(first year 1992) is admissible at five of the thirteen origins and HKG
(1986) at eight; 221 of the 260 (origin, population) pairs---442 of the
520 triples---clear the floor, and the stable panel is unbalanced across
origins: sixteen populations at origins 1990--1998, eighteen from 2006.
Every stable-regime summary is therefore read beside its per-origin
population count (Addendum~3~\S4), because the wild cluster bootstrap's
behaviour depends on the number of clusters actually present, and any
stable-versus-shift contrast is computed on the eighteen-population
intersection (Addendum~3~\S11), never a twenty-population mean against an
eighteen-population one. A random split
is never used anywhere, because mortality panels leak trivially under
random splitting: adjacent ages and years are near-duplicates.

\subsection{Hypothesis status after the prior-art map}
\label{sec:design-status}

The five hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:intro} are reproduced verbatim from
the registration and are tested as registered. The prior-art map, however,
found that the literature already answers or contradicts parts of them, and
Addendum~3~\S8 records, before any result existed, how each is to be read.
\Hone\ is demoted from headline finding to \emph{harness-validity check}:
the point-versus-proper-score inversion is published three times over
(Section~\ref{sec:related}); what remains ours is its magnitude under
pre-registration and per population, unweighted by exposure, so that it
cannot be the population-size artefact \citet{schnurch2022point} describe.
\Htwo\ is restated \emph{two-sided}: the quantity tested is
$|\text{empirical coverage}-\text{nominal}|$, which is hypothesised to grow
from the stable to the shift regime, with the direction free to differ by
family and mechanism; the registered one-sided ``under-cover for every
family'' is already false in the stable regime for bootstrap-ensemble
neural intervals. \Hthree's comparator is re-specified: realised joint path
coverage is compared with each cell's \emph{own simulated-path-implied}
joint coverage, computable from the same predictive sample paths, rather
than with the marginal rate at the same nominal level, because joint
coverage is bounded above by the minimum marginal coverage and equals
$0.95^5=0.774$ under independence, so the registered comparison was close
to arithmetically guaranteed. The realised-minus-implied gap can be of
either sign for any family. \Hfour's registered direction (worse at 65--99
than at 25--64) is contradicted by \citet{dowd2010backtesting}; we
pre-commit to reporting a reversal as an informative result, not to
re-testing until confirmation, and note that the 0--24 band, infant
mortality and the 90+ tail are unmeasured in prior work. \Hfive\ is
restricted to the \emph{ex-post coverage} of derived-quantity intervals
($e_0$, $e_{65}$, $\annuity$), the construction of which is prior art; the
named alternative is the attenuation finding of \citet{cairns2011mortality},
that model differences shrink in applications, and the test is of
calibration error propagating, not of point applications dispersing.

\subsection{Model families}
\label{sec:design-families}

Ten families, chosen to span the classical, statistical-learning and neural
lineages of Section~\ref{sec:related}. Each is specified by its reference
paper; the repository documents every deviation, and the neural and GP
families are specified in \texttt{docs/NEURAL-SPEC.md}, committed before
their first real-data fit. Every family is fit per population and sex on a
matrix of deaths and exposures over ages $0$--$99$ (CBD: $55$--$99$) and
years up to the origin, with the cell weights $W=\ind\{E>0\}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:data}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Lee--Carter (SVD).} $\log m_{x,t}=\alpha_x+\beta_x\kappa_t$,
  rank-one fit to centred log rates with $\sum_x\beta_x=1$,
  $\sum_t\kappa_t=0$ (exact SVD when no cell is weighted out, an
  expectation--maximisation SVD otherwise, the two agreeing to machine
  precision at unit weights); random walk with drift on $\kappa_t$
  including drift-estimation uncertainty \citep{lee1992modeling}.
\item \textbf{Poisson Lee--Carter.} Same structure, Poisson likelihood with
  log-exposure offset, fit by Newton's method \citep{brouhns2002poisson}.
\item \textbf{CBD (M5).} $\operatorname{logit} q_{x,t} = \kappa^{(1)}_t +
  \kappa^{(2)}_t (x-\bar x)$, fit by per-year weighted least squares on
  ages 55--99, where the logit is near-linear in age; bivariate random walk
  with drift \citep{cairns2006two}. CBD is undefined below age 55 and its
  samples are masked there: it is scored on 45 ages, and throughout the
  paper it sits in its own row block with that age support stated, never
  averaged into a ranking with families scored on 100 ages.
\item \textbf{APC / Renshaw--Haberman (M2-A).} Lee--Carter with a cohort term
  $\gamma_{t-x}$ under the identifiability constraints of
  \citet{cairns2009quantitative}; ARIMA projection of the cohort index
  \citep{renshaw2006cohort}.
\item \textbf{Sparse VAR.} A VAR(1) on age-specific mortality improvements
  $\Delta\log m_{x,t}$ in which age $x$'s equation regresses on the lagged
  improvements of ages within a band of half-width~3, estimated by
  ordinary least squares; the hard band is the sparsity structure, a
  documented simplification of the penalised estimator of
  \citet{li2017coherent}. Innovations are drawn from the cross-age residual
  covariance shrunk toward its diagonal by a fixed weight of $0.2$, and
  coefficient draws carry the OLS coefficient covariance. Regression rows
  use only year triples in which every age of the band is observed
  (Addendum~3~\S1). A coefficient draw whose companion matrix has spectral
  radius $\ge1$ is \emph{rejected and redrawn}, up to one hundred times per
  path; a path that cannot produce a stable draw makes the cell an error
  row. Predictive rates are never clipped to rescue a divergent draw, since
  clipping would convert divergence into a bounded interval and flatter the
  family's coverage (Addendum~3~\S7).
\item \textbf{Multi-output Gaussian process.} The multi-output GP of
  \citet{huynh2021multi} reduced to one population: an exact multitask GP
  with the scaled calendar year as input and ages as tasks, kernel
  $\mathrm{RBF}(\text{year})\otimes\mathrm{ICM}(\text{rank }5)$, Gaussian
  likelihood on the half-count log rate with a Poisson-level noise floor,
  trained by marginal likelihood on the trailing block of complete years
  (the Kronecker structure needs a complete year-by-age block), at least
  forty and---by Addendum~4, registered during the production sweeps---at
  most sixty: the family trains on the trailing sixty complete years of
  every panel under every mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}).
  Its native predictive is the joint posterior over the $h$ future years.
\item \textbf{Neural-LC.} The embedding network of \citet{richman2021neural}
  in per-panel form: an age embedding of dimension~5 concatenated with the
  scaled year, two hidden layers of 64 tanh units with dropout $0.05$, and a
  log-rate output added to the log-exposure offset under the Poisson
  deviance. The forecast evaluates the network at future years; the
  extrapolation of the year feature is the audited fragility, not a defect
  to engineer away.
\item \textbf{Shallow CNN-LC.} The convolutional generalisation of
  Lee--Carter \citep{perla2021time}: a trailing window of ten years of
  half-count log rates over all ages, one convolutional layer of eight
  $3\times3$ filters, and a linear map to next year's log rates under the
  Poisson deviance; multi-step forecasts are recursive.
\item \textbf{LSTM-on-$\kappa_t$.} The SVD Lee--Carter age structure with a
  one-layer LSTM (16 hidden units, input window 10) in place of the random
  walk on $\kappa_t$ \citep{nigri2019deep}; the innovation variance is
  estimated from one-step training residuals, and forecast paths add a
  fresh Gaussian innovation per horizon, exactly as the random walk does.
\item \textbf{Distributional NB head.} The neural-LC features with two
  outputs per cell, $\log\mu$ (plus the offset) and a log-dispersion,
  trained by the negative-binomial (NB2) log-likelihood; the only neural
  family with a model-native predictive distribution. Because the negative
  binomial is a Poisson--gamma mixture, its rate samples are gamma draws,
  so the Poisson composition of Section~\ref{sec:design-interface} yields
  the NB count law exactly, with no double counting.
\end{enumerate}

Hyperparameters of the neural and GP families (learning rate and epochs,
from grids of two by two values fixed in the specification) are tuned on an
inner \emph{time} split---the final five pre-cutoff years of each training
window---by the family's own loss, never on a test year, never on a random
subset and never against coverage. One grid was corrected before any
real-data run: the CNN's learning rates were lowered by an order of
magnitude because the original grid diverged at every point on synthetic
data; the correction is dated in the specification.

\subsection{Uncertainty mechanisms}
\label{sec:design-mechanisms}

Seven mechanisms, each a way of turning a fitted family into predictive
sample paths on the $m_x$ scale.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Model-native.} The predictive distribution the reference paper
  itself supplies: random-walk innovation and drift uncertainty for
  Lee--Carter-type models, the VAR's coefficient and innovation draws, the
  GP posterior, the count distribution of the distributional head.
\item \textbf{Semiparametric Poisson bootstrap.} Refit the family $B=200$
  times to pseudo-deaths $D^{(b)}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(E\hat m)$ and pool the
  refits' native samples \citep{brouhns2005bootstrapping}; adds parameter
  uncertainty the native mechanism conditions away.
\item \textbf{Deep ensemble.} $M=10$ independently seeded trainings with
  identical hyperparameters, paths distributed over members with equal
  weight and drawn from each member's own predictive
  \citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}: a mixture of near-deltas for the
  point families, a mixture of NB laws for the distributional head. Stated
  in advance: such mixtures are narrow, and if they under-cover that is a
  finding about the mechanism, not a defect to widen away.
\item \textbf{Monte Carlo dropout.} One training; dropout active at
  prediction time, stochastic forward passes as paths \citep{gal2016dropout},
  plus the family's own innovation noise where it has any.
\item \textbf{Split conformal.} Absolute-residual conformal intervals on the
  log-rate scale \citep{lei2018distribution}, Mondrian-stratified by age
  band ($0$--$24$, $25$--$64$, $65$--$99$) and by horizon
  \citep{vovk2005algorithmic}. The last nine training years are held out;
  the base family is fit on the years before them and its median forecast
  at horizons $1,\dots,9$ is scored against the held-out years; the
  finite-sample quantile of the residuals gives a radius per (horizon,
  band), which is applied around the median forecast of a refit on the
  full training panel.
\item \textbf{EnbPI.} Ensemble batch prediction intervals \citep{xu2021conformal}
  adapted to annual multi-step forecasting with four documented deviations:
  $K=10$ members are fit on nested trailing-block splits with origins one
  year apart (interior leave-block-out would put holes in a panel that
  random-walk forecasters cannot fit); residuals are horizon-matched rather
  than pooled across steps; the interval is centred on a full-panel refit
  rather than an aggregation of the stale members; and there is no online
  residual update, because the test window is scored once. The member
  residuals are what split conformal lacks: ten realisations of the latent
  period-effect path per horizon.
\item \textbf{Copula / joint-path conformal.} A simultaneous band over
  horizons from a scaled supremum-norm nonconformity score on the same
  member residuals \citep{diquigiovanni2022conformal}, in the spirit of
  \citet{sun2024copula} and \citet{messoudi2021copula}; the only mechanism
  whose band is calibrated to be joint over $h=1,\dots,H$.
\end{enumerate}

Four disciplines apply to every conformal arm. The calibration residuals
are drawn only from years at or before the training cutoff, on an inner
time split in which the calibration years are later than the years the
base fit is trained on; no mechanism sees a test year. The calibration
horizon is nine in every regime, at least the longest scored horizon, so no
placebo horizon reuses a radius calibrated for a shorter one
(Addendum~3~\S9). Calibration residuals use the half-count convention of
Section~\ref{sec:data-rounding}. And a conformal wrapper yields an
interval, not a distribution. Coverage, width, interval score and joint
path coverage for a conformal cell are read from the wrapper's own bounds
at its own construction level, 95\%, with no Poisson composition (the
radius was calibrated on observed residuals and already contains
observation noise; composing it again more than doubles the width at
small expected counts) and no quantile-of-samples step (Addendum~2~\S3,
Addendum~3~\S6). Coverage and interval scores at 50\% and 80\% are
\emph{not applicable} to conformal cells and are reported as such. To
preserve the single sample interface for everything else, the harness
draws uniformly inside each cell's interval, independently across cells;
CRPS, log score and PIT computed from those draws describe a placeholder
density, are flagged \texttt{secondary}, are reported only in
Appendix~\ref{app:secondary}, and are never ranked against distributional
mechanisms. Any contrast that includes a conformal arm is decided on the
95\% interval score (Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference}).

\subsection{The crossed grid}
\label{sec:design-grid}

Not every family admits every mechanism. Deterministic classical fits have
no seed variance, so ensembles and dropout are undefined for them; the GP
posterior already integrates parameter uncertainty, so a bootstrap would be
redundant; and neural networks have no closed-form native predictive except
through the distributional head. Table~\ref{tab:grid} enumerates the
admissible cells: 50 primary and four secondary (the GP under a deep
ensemble, and the three point-forecast networks under the Poisson
bootstrap), the secondary ones run and reported in the appendix only. The
three conformal columns do \emph{not} reuse the native fit: every wrapper
refits the base family---twice for split conformal, eleven times for EnbPI
and for the copula band---so the honest cost of a classical family at one
origin is roughly $1+201+2+11+11$ fits, trivial for the classical families
and the same multiplier on a neural base, where the member refits rather
than the $M=10$ ensemble dominate. The expensive axis is neural families
$\times$ members $\times$ expanding origins.

\paragraph{Design-floor cells.} A cell's construction implies a minimum
panel length, and the floor is a property of the family--mechanism pair,
because a wrapper's inner fits inherit the base family's own minimum.
Every family needs at least ten training years; the banded VAR needs more
than ten, the CNN more than fifteen (its ten-year window plus the five
inner-validation years) and the LSTM more than seventeen (window, inner
validation and two more), under every mechanism. Split conformal fits the
base family on the $T-9$ years beneath its nine calibration years; EnbPI
and the copula band fit their earliest staggered member on $T-18$ years.
Applied to the registered shift panels, whose lengths at the 2019 origin
range from seventeen years upward, the rule leaves the following cells
unbuildable: in KOR (seventeen years) every conformal cell of every family
and the LSTM under every mechanism; in HRV (nineteen) EnbPI and the copula
band for every family, and split conformal for the VAR, the CNN and the
LSTM; in CHL (twenty-eight) EnbPI and the copula band for the VAR, the CNN
and the LSTM; and in HKG (thirty-four) EnbPI and the copula band for the
LSTM. These are properties of the design---the registered population list
crossed with the registered mechanisms and the families' reference
architectures---not failures of a fit; they are tabulated as such, and
every other shift panel clears every floor. Applied to the thirteen stable
origins the same rule produces the longer list summarised above, up to the
wholesale exclusion of HRV and KOR; the stable rows enter the same ledger
(Section~\ref{sec:results-infeasible}) when that sweep lands.

\paragraph{Contrasts on common cells; three classes of missing row.}
Because the grid is ragged, no claim in this paper is a full-factorial main
effect. Every comparison is a \emph{contrast within an admissible
sub-grid}, and the pre-registration names the contrasts that carry the
hypotheses: conformal versus native within the classical families;
ensemble versus dropout within the neural families; bootstrap versus native
within the classical families; and joint-path conformal versus split
conformal across all families. Arms fail on different cells---conformal
arms on short panels, native arms where a fit diverges---so every contrast
is computed on the \emph{intersection} of (origin, population, sex) cells
in which each compared arm produced a valid row, with the intersection
size printed beside it; uncensored per-arm tables go to the appendix, and
no table's denominator silently omits an error row (Addendum~3~\S11). A
quality gate sorts every missing row into one of three classes before any
number is read: \emph{machine} failures (memory, environment), which block
analysis until re-run; \emph{design-floor} cells as above; and
\emph{method} failures, where the registered procedure refuses to produce a
forecast---a VAR path with no stable coefficient draw, or a Poisson
composition overflowing on a divergent rate---which are reported as a
property of the family and shrink that family's arms under the common-cell
rule. Error rows enter no mean.

\subsection{One interface, one scoring path}
\label{sec:design-interface}

Every family--mechanism cell implements the same two calls,
\[
  \texttt{fit}(D, E) \quad\text{and}\quad
  \texttt{sample\_mx}(h, n, \text{rng}) \;\to\; [n, h, n_{\text{ages}}],
\]
returning $n$ pathwise trajectories of $m_{x,T+1},\dots,m_{x,T+h}$. The
scoring module then makes every distributional cell \emph{Poisson-inclusive}:
on each path it draws $D^{\ast(j)}_{x,h}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(E_{x,T+h}\,
m^{(j)}_{x,T+h})$ and forms the half-count log crude rate, so that the
object scored is the predictive law of the rate that is actually observed
(Addendum~2~\S1). Scoring a latent-rate sample against an observed crude
rate would count Poisson observation noise as miscalibration, most severely
at young ages and in small populations; on the harness's synthetic truth a
correctly specified Poisson Lee--Carter covered a fraction of nominal at
every level before this was fixed, and mechanisms calibrated on observed
residuals ranked better for a reason unrelated to any shift. Point
forecasts, intervals, proper scores, PIT values, joint path coverage and
the life-table functionals are all computed from these samples by one
evaluation module. No model has a bespoke metric implementation, so no
model can be accidentally flattered by one. One point functional serves
every point metric: the pointwise median of the predictive samples---of
the log rate per (horizon, age) cell for RMSE and MAE, and of the
per-sample life-table functionals for $e_0$, $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$. The
median is invariant under monotone transforms, lies inside the reported
interval by construction, is the centring convention of the conformal
wrappers, and is unaffected by the heavy right tail of exponentiated
Gaussian paths that would drag a sample mean; no column uses the mean.

\subsection{Seeds and provenance}

The global seed is 20260825. Each cell derives its own generator from a seed
sequence over (global seed, origin, population, sex, family, mechanism);
ensemble member $j$ is seeded from the $j$th spawn of the cell's sequence
(the registration's ``global seed plus member index'' in substance:
distinct, deterministic and recorded per member). Every seed, the device the cell ran on, the number of
ages and cells scored, the training window and the exception string of any
failed cell are written to the results file alongside the metrics.

\subsection{Validation gates}
\label{sec:design-gates}

Three gates had to pass before any real-data result could be read. All three
have passed, and gates~1 and~2 were re-run after the weighted-fit rewrite of
Addendum~3~\S1.

\paragraph{Gate 1: synthetic truth.} Data are simulated from a known Poisson
Lee--Carter process (40 ages, 60 training years, horizons $1$--$10$,
exposures $10^5$, thirty independent worlds, 1{,}500 predictive samples
each) and pushed through the real-data code path. The correctly specified
Poisson Lee--Carter must attain nominal 95\% coverage within Monte Carlo
tolerance and a near-uniform PIT histogram; the harness must also detect an
injected break as under-coverage. It does both. If this gate had failed,
every coverage number on real data would have been measuring a bug. Each
neural and GP family passed its own gates on the same synthetic worlds
(finite, bounded point error over $h=1,\dots,5$; distinct recorded member
seeds; for the NB head, reproduction of an NB-simulated world's coverage
through the Poisson composition). One documented finding from the gate: the
two-stage SVD Lee--Carter is mildly \emph{over}-dispersed on Poisson data,
because the SVD $\kappa_t$ carries observation noise into the random-walk
innovation variance; its intervals are slightly wide and its PIT
centre-heavy. This is a property of the method, not of the harness, and it
is carried into the interpretation of the SVD-LC rows.

\paragraph{Gate 2: oracle parity.} The Python Poisson Lee--Carter must match
R \pkg{StMoMo} (version 0.4.1 under R~4.6.1) on a shared subset. On Swedish
males, 1950--2000, ages $0$--$99$, the two fits have identical Poisson
log-likelihood and maximum relative differences of $3.1\times10^{-15}$ in
$\alpha_x$, $2.4\times10^{-14}$ in $\beta_x$ and $1.5\times10^{-13}$ in
$\kappa_t$ when the Newton iteration is run to its cap. The \pkg{StMoMo}
parameter dump is tracked in the repository so the check can be re-run
without R.

\paragraph{Gate 3: life-table parity.} Our period life table on HMD's
published $m_x$ reproduces HMD's published $e_0$ and $e_{65}$ within
0.15~years at spot checks (Section~\ref{sec:data}).

\subsection{Inference plan}

The shift regime is one event. Twenty populations experienced one common
shock, so the effective number of independent observations of ``what a
pandemic does to an interval'' is nearer one than twenty, and nearer one
than the thousands of cells in the panel. Inference is therefore framed as
an event study: population is the cluster, every test resamples clusters
rather than cells and imposes the null before resampling, PIT is pooled
within cluster before it is compared across clusters, every contrast is
restricted to common cells, and the placebo regime is treated as a second
event rather than as more draws from the same one. The procedures are
defined in Section~\ref{sec:metrics-inference}.

\subsection{Deviations from the pre-registration}
\label{sec:design-deviations}

\begin{itemize}
\item Ordering: \citet{dowd2010backtesting} and \citet{schnurch2022point}
  were read on 2026-08-26, after the registration was frozen and before
  any real-data fit; the consequences for how the hypotheses are read are
  registered in Addendum~3~\S8 and stated in
  Section~\ref{sec:design-status}. Nothing in the frozen design depends on
  either paper's content.
\item Addenda~1--3 (2026-08-26): pre-specified strata and sensitivities,
  scoring-target clarifications, and defect-handling rules. Not
  deviations---no population, split, horizon, observation or metric
  changed---but recorded here so the ledger is complete.
\item CNN learning-rate grid lowered before the first real-data run
  (divergence on synthetic data); neural gate~G-N1 recalibrated from
  ``within twice the Poisson Lee--Carter error'' to ``finite and below one
  nat'', because the cell-feature networks' extrapolation plateau is the
  audited fragility rather than an implementation defect. Both dated in
  \texttt{docs/NEURAL-SPEC.md}.
\item Seed derivation for ensemble members. Registered: member seed $=$
  global seed $+$ member index. Implemented: the $j$-th member's seed is the
  $j$-th child of the cell's \texttt{SeedSequence}, whose entropy is the
  global seed plus stable hashes of the cell key (origin, population, sex,
  family, mechanism), so that every cell can be re-run in isolation and
  reproduce its row. The implemented rule keeps every property the
  registration wanted from the additive one---member seeds are distinct,
  deterministic given the global seed, and recorded per row in the results
  metadata---while avoiding the seed collisions that an additive offset
  produces between neighbouring cells. No result depends on the particular
  integers; the change is recorded because the registered sentence is not
  literally what the code does (\texttt{docs/NEURAL-SPEC.md}, rule~4).
\item Training window of the multi-output GP (Addendum~4, 2026-08-29,
  registered during the production sweeps before any GP cell on a panel
  longer than sixty years had produced a result). Registered: the GP trains
  on the whole trailing block of complete years. Implemented: the trailing
