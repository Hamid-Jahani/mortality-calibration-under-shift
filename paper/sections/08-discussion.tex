\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The break sorts the families; it does not create the miscalibration}
\label{sec:discussion-standing}

The audit was built to ask whether mortality prediction intervals survive
a pandemic. With all three regimes scored the answer is not the yes or no
the question invites, and the more interesting answer is the one the
stable control supplies: most of the interval failure measured in this
paper is already present in a quiet period, before any break, and what
the break chiefly does is re-order the families according to how far
their intervals stood from nominal beforehand and on which side.

The stable control is the evidence. Over thirteen expanding origins from
1990 to 2014, horizons $h=1,\dots,5$, with no structural event in any
test year, the classical cores under their own predictive law cover
0.702 (SVD Lee--Carter), 0.682 (Poisson Lee--Carter), 0.731 (APC/RH) and
0.901 (CBD) against a nominal 0.95, and LSTM-$\kappa_t$ covers 0.638
under deep ensembling and 0.667 under MC dropout
(Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}). The shortfall is not an artefact of the
tail of the predictive law: the three full-age cores among them cover
0.365, 0.324 and 0.353 at a nominal 0.50, and 0.562, 0.522 and 0.566 at
a nominal 0.80. These are the standard implementations of
the standard families on the standard data source, scored against the
years they were fitted to forecast, with nothing breaking. A reader who
saw only the shift regime would attribute SVD Lee--Carter's 0.692 in
2020--2024 to the pandemic; 0.702 of it was there in 1990--2014.

\Htwo\ as registered---``nominal 95\% intervals under-cover in the shift
regime for every model family''---is therefore not supported, and the
two-sided restatement of Addendum~3~\S8, under which the quantity tested
is $|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ and the direction is free to differ
by arm, is supported in only about half the grid. The departure grows
from the stable control to the shift regime in 27 of the 50 admissible
cells. It is important not to report that number alone. Raw coverage
\emph{falls} from stable to shift in 34 of the 50, so the modal movement
at the break is downward, as the registered one-sided form anticipated;
what the one-sided form did not anticipate is that a fall is degradation
only for an arm that began below nominal.

The two counts are the same movement read through an absolute value. A
structural break widens realised forecast errors relative to the
dispersion the intervals were built from, and realised coverage duly
falls in roughly two thirds of the grid---34 of the 50 cells, against 16
in which it rose. Whether that fall is a departure from nominal or a
return to it depends entirely on the starting side. An arm at 0.702 that
moves to 0.692 has moved away from 0.95; an arm at 0.988 that moves to
0.937 has moved towards it. Because the registered quantity is folded
about the nominal point, a predominantly downward movement produces
increases in the registered departure on one side of nominal and
decreases on the other, and the grid divides accordingly. Four
behaviours are visible, and only the second and third are the failure the
hypothesis was written to catch.

Some arms are standing failures that the break barely moves: SVD
Lee--Carter native goes 0.702 to 0.692, its Poisson-bootstrap companion
0.726 to 0.727, LSTM-$\kappa_t$ 0.638 to 0.647 under the ensemble and
0.667 to 0.685 under dropout---four arms whose intervals were wrong
before 2020 and are wrong by about the same amount afterwards. Some are
standing failures that deepen: Poisson Lee--Carter 0.682 to 0.624 and
APC/RH 0.731 to 0.654. Some began the quiet control close to nominal and
were genuinely damaged: the multi-output GP's native law goes 0.945 to
0.905, and the sparse VAR's split-conformal band---0.948 in the control,
a registered departure of 0.002 and among the smallest in the stable
grid---goes to 0.910. CBD belongs beside them only with a qualification,
because it was 0.049 from nominal before anything broke: its departure
grows 0.049 to 0.151 as coverage falls 0.901 to 0.799, the largest growth
anywhere in the grid, but from a starting point that was not sound. And
some were over-wide and were pulled towards nominal by the shock: the
sparse VAR's native law goes 0.988 to 0.937 and the NB head's deep
ensemble 0.988 to 0.956, each posting a smaller registered departure at
the break than in the quiet period.

\Hthree\ needs more care than its headline count invites, and the care
is what turns it into a result. Joint path coverage over $h=1,\dots,5$
does lie below marginal coverage in all 100 of the 100 (regime, cell)
pairs formed by the fifty admissible arms in the stable and shift
regimes, with a mean gap of 0.127 in the quiet control and 0.168 at the
break (Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint}). That count is descriptive and close to
arithmetic: five independent nominal-95\% statements already give
$0.95^5=0.774$, a gap of 0.176 that is larger than either observed mean,
so a negative gap is what a well-calibrated arm should produce and is on
its own evidence of nothing. Section~\ref{sec:design-status} recorded
that before any result existed, which is why the comparator was
re-specified to each cell's own simulated-path-implied joint coverage;
that quantity is not among the columns the runner emitted
(Section~\ref{sec:results-joint}), so \Hthree\ returns no registered
verdict here and the marginal-versus-joint gap is reported as a
measurement rather than as a test.

The measurement that carries information compares each arm's joint rate
with independence \emph{at that arm's own realised marginal rate},
$\text{marginal}^H$, and on that benchmark the sign reverses. Realised
joint coverage \emph{exceeds} what independence at the same marginal
level would deliver in 50 of the 50 stable cells, 50 of the 50 shift
cells and 45 of the 46 placebo cells, by as much as $+0.34$. Whether a
path is missed at successive horizons is therefore strongly positively
dependent: a miss at $h=1$ tends to persist, so trajectories fail in
clumps rather than independently. That is the dependence
\citet{dowd2010backtesting} name in a footnote and then set aside,
measuring marginal positions instead, and quantifying it across ten
families, seven mechanisms and three regimes is what this paper
contributes on \Hthree.

The practical reading follows, and it is less comfortable than the
dependence result sounds. Positive dependence helps, but not enough:
joint coverage still falls below the nominal independence value of 0.774
in 21 of the 50 stable cells and in 30 of the 50 at the break. Four arms
hold a joint rate at or above 0.95 in the quiet control---the NB head
under dropout and under ensembling (0.956 and 0.953) and the sparse VAR
under its native and bootstrap laws (0.951 and 0.985)---and every one of
them does so from a marginal rate of 0.988 or higher, that is, by being
over-wide at the single level; at the break no arm in the grid reaches
0.95 jointly. The copula band, the one mechanism constructed for the
trajectory rather than the level, carries the highest joint rate in eight
of the ten family blocks in the quiet control and in nine of ten at the
break (0.894--0.942 and 0.805--0.927), but it is not alone at the top of
the column---the NB head's dropout arm outscores its own family's copula
band in both regimes, 0.956 against 0.922 and 0.897 against 0.868---and
its weakest cell of the two, CBD under the break at 0.805, sits far from
its nominal 0.95. The literature reports marginal coverage; the quantity
a multi-year reserve depends on is the path, and outside a handful of
over-wide arms the path is not covered at the advertised rate in either
regime.

The practical consequence is the strongest recommendation this paper is
willing to make, and it is a recommendation about audit design rather
than about models. \emph{A calibration audit must include a quiet control
period.} A shift-only audit cannot distinguish a model that broke from
one that was never calibrated, because both present as under-coverage at
the break, and it cannot distinguish either from a model that was
over-wide and got lucky. The sparse VAR is the cautionary case: read on
the shift regime alone, its native law is the best-calibrated native arm
in the grid---on the 29 of 40 population--sex units its own rejection
rule admits---at 0.937 against nominal 0.95, a registered departure of
0.013. Read against its own quiet control it earns that rank by having
been over-wide at 0.988 by roughly the amount the shock happened to
consume, and there is nothing in the shift regime that says the next
shock will be sized to consume it again. Reporting the departure at a
break without the departure in quiet years is not a conservative
simplification; it is a measurement that can rank a broken model above a
sound one.

\subsection{What the family-level split is evidence of}
\label{sec:discussion-split}

The registration deliberately declined a directional claim on
neural-versus-classical ordering (Section~\ref{sec:intro}), and
the working conjecture that motivated the audit---that coverage
degradation across the break would be larger for the neural
families---is refuted in its strong form by Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}.
The split the grid actually produced runs through the middle of the
neural group, and it is visible in the stable control as well as at the
break. In the quiet regime the arms holding near-nominal marginal
coverage are the NB head under all three of its distributional
mechanisms (0.975--0.988), the multi-output GP (0.945), CNN-LC under dropout
(0.954) and---on the cells its own rejection rule admits, a selection
caveat carried from Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}---the sparse VAR (0.988).
The arms far from nominal are the three full-age classical cores
(0.682--0.731), LSTM-$\kappa_t$ under both its mechanisms (0.638--0.667)
and the neural-LC ensemble (0.758). At the break the membership of the
two groups is unchanged---0.905--0.972 for the first, 0.624--0.694 for
the second---with CBD, at 0.901 in the quiet control and 0.799 at the
break, the one family that sits between the two groups and moves towards
the second.

The two groups differ along a line the design did not manipulate. Every
arm in the second group forecasts through an extrapolated
low-dimensional period index---$\kappa_t$ and its cohort or CBD
companions, the LSTM's learned continuation of $\kappa_t$, the year
feature that neural-LC evaluates at future dates---and equips the
extrapolation with intervals that propagate the \emph{process risk} of
that index: an innovation variance estimated from in-sample residuals,
drift-estimation uncertainty, and, for the ensembles, variance across
training seeds. Every arm in the first group carries its uncertainty
somewhere else: a count likelihood with a fitted dispersion (the NB
head), a posterior over functions of calendar time (the GP), stochastic
perturbation of the fitted map itself (dropout), a cross-age innovation
covariance with coefficient draws (the sparse VAR). The intervals that
are calibrated, in quiet years and across the break alike, are the ones
whose width comes from a distributional head or from integrating over
the model's own parameters rather than from the historical smoothness of
an index.

This is an observed association, not an established mechanism. The grid
crossed family with mechanism; it did not cross ``index extrapolation''
with ``distributional head'' as manipulated factors, and the families on
the two sides of the line differ in architecture, likelihood, feature set
and width at once. Four readings within the grid nevertheless discipline
the association, and each now has a stable-regime value beside its
shift-regime one. First, the mechanism moves coverage with the
architecture held fixed: on the same CNN-LC architecture dropout covers
0.954 against the ensemble's 0.863 in the quiet control and 0.962
against 0.882 at the break, and on neural-LC 0.816 against 0.758 and
0.811 against 0.694 (Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}). Second, the
family moves coverage with the mechanism held fixed: under the same
deep-ensemble mechanism the NB head covers 0.988 where neural-LC covers
0.758 and the LSTM 0.638 in the stable regime, and 0.956, 0.694 and
0.647 at the break. Third, the calibrated arms are not simply vaguer.
Among the full-age distributional arms of the quiet control the two
smallest 95\% interval scores belong to the NB head's native law and the
sparse VAR's native law ($\IS_{95}$ of 1.455 and 1.474, at coverages of
0.975 and 0.988), against 2.330 for SVD Lee--Carter at 0.702 coverage and
3.056 for Poisson Lee--Carter at 0.682; the same holds at the break, where
NB native and CNN-LC dropout carry the two smallest scores among the
distributional arms (1.684 and 1.677; only the interval-constructing
EnbPI wrappers of SVD-LC and the LSTM, scored from their bounds, sit
lower at 1.615 and 1.631). The one arm that visibly buys coverage with
width---neural-LC dropout, 0.811 at the break for an interval score of
5.559---is identified by the width penalty exactly as it should be. CBD's
scores are excluded from these comparisons because its 55--99 age support
is not the full-age families'. Fourth, adding parameter uncertainty to
the classical cores barely moves them, in either regime: the Poisson
bootstrap takes SVD-LC from 0.702 to 0.726 in the quiet control and from
0.692 to 0.727 at the break, and CBD from 0.901 to 0.921 and 0.799 to
0.830. What these intervals lack is not parameter risk, and they lack it
whether or not anything is breaking---which is consistent with the
missing risk being in the process the index extrapolates rather than in
the estimation of the model that extrapolates it. Agreement is not proof.
Establishing the mechanism would need designs this grid does not
contain---a distributional head grafted onto a classical core, or the NB
head's likelihood replaced while its features are kept---and that is a
follow-on study, not a reading of this one.

\subsection{The age profile is standing, and it settles a disagreement with the prior literature}
\label{sec:discussion-age}

\Hfour\ registered a concentration of coverage failure at the old ages.
The result is family-specific rather than universal
(Section~\ref{sec:results}), and for the families in which it holds it is
again a property of the quiet control rather than of the break. SVD
Lee--Carter's native intervals cover 0.888, 0.760 and 0.501 across ages
0--24, 25--64 and 65--99 in the stable regime, and 0.882, 0.725 and
0.518 at the break (Table~\ref{tab:h4-age}); LSTM-$\kappa_t$ under deep
ensembling covers 0.858, 0.700 and 0.409 in the quiet control against
0.893, 0.687 and 0.426 at the break. For these arms the age gradient is
essentially unmoved by the pandemic: it is a standing feature of an
interval built by extrapolating one index whose loadings are largest
where the death counts are. APC/RH is the counter-case in which the
gradient is genuinely break-induced---0.751, 0.729, 0.720, flat in age in
the quiet control, against 0.732, 0.661, 0.589 at the break---and
Poisson Lee--Carter is uniformly poor in both regimes with only a shallow
age profile that runs the other way, 0.676 at 65--99 against 0.664 at
25--64 in the control and 0.639 against 0.602 at the break. The neural-LC
dropout arm's collapse at the young ages is standing too, at 0.529 in the
quiet control and 0.538 at the break.

This resolves a disagreement in the published record, and we state the
resolution as a contribution rather than as an incidental finding. The
prior-art map recorded before any result existed that \Hfour's registered
direction was contradicted by \citet{dowd2010backtesting}, whose
published backtest reports that ``the age 65 forecasts have a notable
upward bias with the realized values often close to or below the lower
bounds, whereas the age 84 forecast show only a very slight upward
bias''---the higher age behaving better, on two ages of one family group.
On thirteen origins, eighteen populations and the full 0--99 range, with
no break in the test years, the ordering for the index-extrapolating
families runs the other way and runs steeply: 0.888 at 0--24 against
0.501 at 65--99 for SVD Lee--Carter. The disagreement is not about the
pandemic, since our stable window contains no pandemic; it is about age
coverage of an extrapolated index measured over a wider age range, more
origins and more populations than the two-age comparison the earlier
finding rested on. What survives from that literature is the narrower
point that the ordering is not universal, and our own table makes that
point far more strongly than the earlier one did: in the shift regime the
registered direction is reversed in 34 of the 50 cells---in 26 of the 30
conformal arms, and in every arm of CBD, CNN-LC, Poisson Lee--Carter and
the multi-output GP. It survives chiefly where the interval comes from
extrapolating an index under its own law: SVD Lee--Carter and APC/RH
natively and under the Poisson bootstrap, LSTM-$\kappa_t$ under both its
mechanisms, and the neural-LC ensemble. The NB head and the sparse VAR
preserve it in four of their five arms each, but by margins of 0.044 and
below, mostly inside bands that sit at or above nominal---0.932 at 25--64
against 0.901 at 65--99 for the NB head's native law, 0.935 against 0.927
for the sparse VAR's, the widest being the NB head's EnbPI arm at 0.898
against 0.854---which is a different phenomenon from the steep gradient
\Hfour\ was written about. The old-age concentration is thus a property
of index extrapolation rather than of mortality forecasting at large, and
a conformal wrapper does not merely flatten it: on 26 of 30 wrapped arms
it tips the gradient the other way, so that the old ages end up the
better covered.

\subsection{Derived quantities carry the least reliable intervals in the study, and already do so in quiet years}
\label{sec:discussion-derived}

The result an actuarial reader should take from this study is not on the
rate surface at all. Intervals on the derived quantities---period life
expectancy and the annuity-due factor---are worse calibrated than the
rate-level intervals of the same arms in every full-age family, and for
the index-extrapolating families the deterioration is already present in
the quiet control at close to its full size. In the stable regime SVD
Lee--Carter's native law covers 0.256 for $e_{65}$ and 0.253 for
$\annuity$ at a nominal 0.95, against 0.702 for the same arm's marginal
coverage on log rates; the LSTM ensemble covers 0.182 and 0.157
(Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial}). An annuity factor produced by these
models carries, one year ahead of thirteen quiet origins spanning three
decades, an interval that covers its own realised value about a quarter
of the time when it advertises nineteen times in twenty. That is a
statement about ordinary valuation practice, not about crisis
resilience.

Two qualifications keep the claim honest. CBD is the exception: on its
truncated 55--99 table the integration does not hurt it, and its native
law covers 0.930 on $e_{65}$ and 0.889 on $\annuity$ against 0.901 on
rates, with its Poisson-bootstrap companion at 0.950 and 0.934 against
0.921---derived-quantity coverage at or above the rate-level figure in
three of those four comparisons. And for the arms that were well
calibrated on rates the derived-quantity failure is largely a break
effect rather than a standing one: the sparse VAR's native law loses
0.008 between rates and $\annuity$ in the quiet control (0.988 against
0.980) and 0.351 at the break (0.937 against 0.586), the GP 0.082 against
0.311, the NB head's ensemble 0.174 against 0.506. It is the
index-extrapolating arms---SVD and Poisson Lee--Carter, APC/RH,
LSTM-$\kappa_t$, neural-LC---whose derived quantities were already
uncovered before anything broke.

The ordering across arms is the same one the rate surface shows, which is
what makes the finding coherent rather than anecdotal: in the quiet
control the sparse VAR's native law covers 0.980 on $\annuity$, CBD's
0.889 on its truncated 55--99 table, the GP's 0.863 and the NB head's
deep ensemble 0.814, while SVD Lee--Carter, Poisson Lee--Carter and
LSTM-$\kappa_t$ sit between 0.157 and 0.468 under either of their two
mechanisms. The mechanism is the one Section~\ref{sec:actuarial} sets out:
a period functional integrates a whole age range of one test year, so it
behaves as a joint coverage statement across ages rather than a marginal
one, and the marginal-to-joint gap \Hthree\ measures along the horizon
axis has an age-axis analogue that the life-table integration cannot
average away. The attenuation
alternative of \citet{cairns2011mortality} is answered in the quiet
control as well as at the break: integration concentrates the failure.

The sorting logic of Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing} recurs here in
a form worth stating because it is easy to misread. Several
badly-under-covering arms cover \emph{better} on the derived quantities
at the break than in the quiet control---SVD Lee--Carter's native
$\annuity$ coverage goes 0.253 to 0.450---while several of the arms that
covered well in quiet years collapse: the sparse VAR from 0.980 to 0.586,
the GP from 0.863 to 0.594, the NB head's ensemble from 0.814 to 0.450.
CBD is the exception among the well-covering arms and is worth naming as
such: its stable $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ coverages fall only to 0.875 and
0.850 natively, and to 0.925 and 0.850 under the bootstrap---a
deterioration, not a collapse. The first movement is not a repair. SVD
Lee--Carter under-states $e_{65}$ systematically: its median path sits a
mean of 0.84 years below the
realised value in the quiet control and 0.57 years below it at the break
(the error column of Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial}, $-0.84$ and $-0.57$),
and coverage rose because a standing under-projection of longevity was
partly cancelled by a mortality shock, not because the intervals became
trustworthy. The two error means are descriptive and rest on different
support---thirteen origins over eighteen populations against one origin
over twenty, two of which the stable regime cannot score
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations})---so the narrowing is a
statement about the two columns as they stand and not an estimate of a
period effect. A reserving basis that appears to improve under a pandemic
for that reason has not been validated by the pandemic.

\subsection{Reading the conformal attribution}
\label{sec:discussion-conformal}

The Diebold--Mariano block of Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} is the crossed
design's attribution finding, and its sign pattern admits a one-sentence
practitioner reading: \emph{wrap what is broken; do not wrap what is
calibrated}. A conformal band is not a free safety layer laid over a
model's own uncertainty; it replaces that uncertainty with a
residual-quantile geometry, and the replacement is signed. It is better
on the 95\% interval score for exactly the four classical cores whose
native law is wrong and worse for exactly the three families whose native
law is sound---a premium paid in width by a model whose own dispersion
was already right---and at the break every one of those seven contrasts
is significant.

The contrast is not a pandemic effect either, which is a further place
where the quiet control changes the reading. Every one of
the seven families that admits the native-versus-split contrast carries
the \emph{same sign} in the stable regime as at the break, with mean loss
differentials of comparable size: SVD Lee--Carter $+0.694$ in the quiet
control against $+0.700$ at the break, Poisson Lee--Carter $+1.038$
against $+1.895$, APC/RH $+0.451$ against $+1.039$ and CBD $+0.108$
against $+0.318$, all favouring the wrapper; the NB head $-0.873$ against
$-1.024$, the GP $-0.772$ against $-0.413$ and the sparse VAR $-0.236$
against $-0.318$, all favouring the native law. The pattern is already
significant at the 5\% level in five of the seven families in the quiet
control (SVD-LC $p=0.006$, Poisson-LC $p=0.032$, the NB head and the GP
$p<0.0002$, the sparse VAR $p=0.0002$), with APC/RH marginal ($p=0.086$)
and CBD not distinguishable from zero ($p=0.198$); at the break all seven
reach $p\le0.019$. The bootstrap resolution is 4999 replicates, so
$p<0.0002$ is the smallest statement any of these tests can support.
Conformalisation therefore behaves as insurance in ordinary
years and not only across breaks: it pays out precisely when the model's
own uncertainty is wrong and costs a premium precisely when it is right,
and which side of the line a family falls on is a property of the
family--mechanism pair rather than of the wrapper or of the regime.

Four cautions temper the reading. First, the pattern does not reproduce
at the second break. In the placebo regime every one of the six families
that admits the contrast there returns a point estimate favouring the
native law, and only the NB head's reaches significance ($-0.461$,
$p=0.008$, keeping the native-favouring sign it carries in the other two
regimes) with CBD's reversal marginal ($-0.476$, $p=0.071$); the remaining
contrasts are indistinguishable from zero ($p\ge0.127$). Over a nine-year
horizon, on the placebo regime's eleven populations---seven of them for
the GP, whose own admissibility rule binds hardest there---with
calibration residuals drawn from pre-1914 data, the wrapper's advantage
for the classical cores simply is not there. Second, the repair is
empirical, not guaranteed:
exchangeability between calibration and test years is violated by design
in the shift regime, so the finite-sample conformal guarantee does not
apply across the break; the wrappers held 95\% coverage of 0.848--0.981
anyway, which the coverage-gap bounds of \citet{barber2023conformal}
permit but do not promise, and a practitioner who adopts the wrapper for
the next break inherits the same asterisk. Third, the wrapper equalises
the marginal level, not everything an actuary needs: split and EnbPI
bands that cover marginally still lose the path (joint coverage
0.760--0.842 in the quiet control and 0.692--0.862 at the break for the
split arm, against the 0.774 that independence at the nominal level
would already give), and the copula band that holds the path pays for it at the
single level, where the interval-score confidence sets of
Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} eliminate it in five families at the break and in
two---CBD and the sparse VAR---in the quiet control. The choice among
wrappers is a choice of which quantity is to be
calibrated---the level or the trajectory---and
Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts} prices that choice without
adjudicating it. Fourth, a conformal cell has no distributional paths, so
it contributes nothing to the derived-quantity audit of
Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}: a practitioner who needs an interval on
$e_{65}$ or $\annuity$ from a wrapped model must first decide what a band
on log rates means for a functional across ages, a construction this
study did not register.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}

The limitations in this subsection, through the itemised list below, were
written and committed before any regime result was read, so that none
could be selected after the fact to excuse a finding or to inflate one.
They are retained in substance; where a pre-committed limitation now
bears on a specific result, the cross-reference is added and nothing is
removed. Three further limitations arose during the production sweeps and
the analysis and are stated, dated, at the end.

\subsubsection{The effective sample size is one}

Every shift-regime number in this paper is a statement about one pandemic
observed in twenty places, sixteen of them for the multi-output GP.
Clustering by population and imposing the null
in the bootstrap make the standard errors honest \emph{given the event};
they do not make the event a sample of events. The stable control changes
what the shift numbers mean---it separates a break from a standing
failure, which is the substance of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing}---but it does not change this. The
audit answers ``did these intervals survive this break, and were they
calibrated before it?'' and, through the placebo, ``does the pattern
resemble the previous break?''---not ``will these intervals survive the
next break?''. A reader who wants the last question answered wants a
different study, one that we cannot run until there is a next break.

\subsubsection{The grid is ragged, so claims are contrasts}

Ten families by seven mechanisms is seventy cells on paper and fifty in
fact. Main effects over a ragged grid confound the factor with the
pattern of admissibility, so no main effect is reported; every claim is a
contrast within a sub-grid whose composition is stated. This costs
generality: a finding that conformal wrapping changes coverage for
classical families says nothing about neural families unless the neural
sub-grid shows it too, and we will not average across the two. The counts
of Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing}---27 of 50 and 34 of 50---are
descriptive tallies over the admissible cells and carry no inference;
they are not a test of \Htwo\ over a balanced design, because the design
is not balanced.

\subsubsection{Conformal cells carry secondary proper scores}

A conformal band is an interval, not a distribution. Drawing uniformly
inside it keeps the single interface honest for coverage, width and
interval score, but the CRPS, log score and PIT of those draws describe a
placeholder density. They are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:secondary},
flagged, and not ranked. Any reader who compares a conformal cell's CRPS
with a native cell's CRPS is comparing a convention with a forecast.
Conformal predictive systems that output a full calibrated distribution
would remove this limitation and are noted as future work.

\subsubsection{2024 may be revised}

HMD does not flag provisional years; it re-issues whole series at each
release, so the 2024 values are final only in the sense that this release
contains them. The shift regime's $h=5$ results rest on 2024, and two of
that regime's twenty populations (USA, CHL) carry census-based exposures
that HMD documents as subject to later revision. The pre-registered robustness run
drops 2024; Addendum~1 adds runs that drop USA and CHL and that contrast
register-based with census-based populations. If the $h=5$ and $h\le4$
results disagree, the $h\le4$ result is the one to trust and the $h=5$
result is reported as provisional; if a later vintage appears before
submission, the shift regime is re-run on it and any change in the
headline coverage is reported as a vintage-revision effect.

\subsubsection{SVD Lee--Carter is over-dispersed by construction}

Validation gate~1 found that the classical two-stage SVD Lee--Carter
produces intervals slightly wider than nominal on data simulated from a
Poisson Lee--Carter process, because the SVD estimate of $\kappa_t$
absorbs observation noise that then inflates the random-walk innovation
variance. This is a property of the method that
\citet{girosi2007understanding} would predict, not a harness artefact,
and it has a direct consequence for reading the results: an SVD-LC row
that appears better calibrated than Poisson-LC may be so partly because
it is vaguer, and the interval score and Murphy resolution term---not
coverage---are the columns that tell the two apart. This is visible in
both regimes, as 0.702 native coverage against Poisson Lee--Carter's
0.682 in the quiet control and 0.692 against 0.624 at the break; part of
each gap is width, and the interval-score column is the one that prices
it.

\subsubsection{Other pre-committed limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Exchangeability is violated by design.} Conformal calibration
  residuals come from pre-cutoff years, and the test years are precisely
  the years chosen to differ from them. \citet{barber2023conformal} bound
  the resulting coverage gap; we measure it rather than assume it away.
  The conformal arms are audited, not endorsed
  (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal}).
\item \emph{Neural families are budget-limited.} Ensembles of ten over
  the origins, populations and sexes of three regimes fix the
  hyperparameter search to small pre-specified grids; the inner time split
  is small (five years). A better-tuned network might do better on point
  accuracy; whether it would be better calibrated is exactly what is not
  known, and the audited fragility of the year-feature extrapolation
  (Section~\ref{sec:design-families}) is a property of the specification,
  not a tuning accident.
\item \emph{Data quality in the placebo window.} Military deaths are
  reconstructed in the belligerent series and excluded in the
  civilian-only one. Placebo results are descriptive, are reported pooled
  and by the pre-specified strata, and the neutral register-based stratum
  carries the 1918-versus-2020 comparison.
\item \emph{Single-age granularity.} Modelling ages $0$--$99$ singly keeps
  the old-age cells noisy in small populations; the age-cap-90 robustness
  run tests whether \Hfour\ is an artefact of that noise.
\item \emph{Rounding of deaths.} The log-score convention rounds fractional
  deaths; the CRPS-on-counts sensitivity exists to show that nothing turns
  on it.
\item \emph{One code path, one author.} A single evaluation module is a
  strength for consistency and a weakness for independent verification;
  the synthetic-truth gate and the \pkg{StMoMo} oracle are the checks, and
  the code is public.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Limitations recorded during the sweeps and analysis}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The GP rows audit a sixty-year-window variant on a reduced
  population set} (Addendum~4, registered 2026-08-29, during the production
  sweeps and before any GP cell on a panel longer than sixty years had
  produced a result). The exact multitask GP was infeasible on the long
  panels at the available memory, so the family trains on the trailing
  sixty complete years under every mechanism; the cause, the memory
  figures and the disclosure of an earlier forty-year diagnostic are in
  Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}. Every GP number in this paper is
  therefore a statement about the sixty-year-window GP, not about the
  registered full-panel specification. The family's own minimum
  training-block rule additionally removes populations from every regime,
  and by a different amount in each: the GP rows rest on sixteen
  populations in the stable control and sixteen in the shift regime,
  against eighteen and twenty respectively for the other nine families,
  and on eight of the placebo regime's eleven, where its native rows come
  from sixteen of the twenty-two population--sex units
  (Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial}). Every GP comparison therefore stays
  inside its own sub-grid.
\item \emph{The stable control is scored on eighteen populations, not
  twenty} (recorded 2026-09-02, at the stable QA gate). HRV and KOR enter
  HMD too late to supply the registered admissibility floor of fifteen
  contiguous training years at any of the thirteen stable origins---at the
  earliest origins their panels have no training years at all---so no
  family produces a valid stable row for either, and the stable columns of
  every regime-indexed table are means over eighteen populations for the
  nine non-GP families and sixteen for the multi-output GP. The sparse
  VAR is thinned further, but by its own explosive-draw rejection rule
  rather than by an admissibility floor, so its stable support is a set of
  cells rather than a population count: 297 of the 520 (population, sex,
  origin) units for its native law (Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}). The
  stable-versus-shift comparisons of
  Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing} are therefore not
  taken on an identical population set: the two regimes' descriptive means
  differ in support as well as in period, and the shift regime's twenty
  populations include two that the stable regime cannot score. Every
  contrast reported with a test statistic is computed on the intersection
  of cells in which both compared arms produced a valid row
  (Addendum~3~\S11), and the cluster counts in Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs}
  state that intersection for each.
\item \emph{The 99.5\% longevity tail was not emitted by the swept passes}
  (recorded 2026-08-30 at analysis; closed as future work 2026-09-04). The
  runner persists the 2.5\% and 97.5\% sample quantiles of the derived
  quantities, not the 99.5\% quantile, and the three production sweeps
  completed without it. The empirical frequency with which a nominally
  once-in-200-year $\annuity$ exceedance is realised---the quantity an
  internal model would carry as its one-year longevity tail---therefore
  cannot be evaluated from the results files, and no tail-frequency claim
  is made anywhere in this paper. It was descriptive and not registered.
  Per-cell seeds are recorded, so a re-score with a persisted 99.5\%
  column would reproduce the original predictive samples exactly; that
  re-score is left to future work, and the 97.5\% breach frequencies of
  Section~\ref{sec:actuarial} are what this study reports at the top of
  the distribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the stable and placebo regimes settle, and what they cannot}
\label{sec:discussion-regimes}

The three regimes were registered with different jobs, and it is worth
separating what each has now done from what no amount of data of that
kind could do.

The stable regime supplied the reference level, and it turned out to
carry the paper's reframing rather than merely to anchor it. Both
comparative clauses depend on it: \Htwo's registered growth in
$|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ from stable to shift, per family and
mechanism, and \Hfive's registered excess of the shift-regime departure
on $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ over the stable-regime departure, per arm.
Both are now computed, and both return a split verdict rather than the
registered universal one. The stable half of \Htwo\ was labelled a
replication arm (Section~\ref{sec:design-regimes}) because stable-window
miscalibration of particular arms is already in the literature; what the
replication delivers here is not novelty but the discriminating power
that Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing} rests on. What the stable
regime cannot do is convert an event study into a sample of events: a
shift-minus-stable difference is a statement about calibration at one
break relative to calibration at none, with the first limitation above
intact.

The placebo regime is a second event, used descriptively by registration.
Its side-by-side (Table~\ref{tab:twin-crises}) shows whether the patterns
found at the pandemic recur at a break of different cause and different
age profile, over a nine-year horizon, in data a century older, and the
answer is mixed in an informative way. The horizon dependence measured
under \Hthree\ recurs: joint coverage beats independence at each arm's
own marginal rate in 45 of the 46 admissible placebo cells even over nine
horizons, though its level falls much further than at the pandemic, below
the nominal independence value of $0.95^9=0.630$ in 37 of those 46 cells
and to 0.335 for SVD Lee--Carter's native law. The full-age classical
cores' native coverage sits at 0.761--0.787 rather than below it, and the
conformal sign pattern does not recur at all
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal}). What the placebo cannot do is
unchanged by that. It supports no transfer regression and no formal test
of COVID-specificity; its eleven populations overlap only partly with the
shift regime's twenty, and the sparse VAR's rejection rule leaves that
family a single admissible mechanism there; the
pre-specified strata carry the data-quality reading, and its first test
year, 1914, precedes the influenza wave, so the $h=1$ column is a
pre-shock year in a way the shift regime's is not. Recurrence or
non-recurrence at two events leaves the effective-sample-size limitation
exactly where it is.

\subsection{What this paper does not do}

It does not propose a model, a repair, or a recommendation about which
family to use. The audit did find a mechanism that improves the interval
score of the families whose native law is wrong; it is stated as a signed
contrast within the registered sub-grids (Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs},
Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}), with its cost to the calibrated
families stated in the same table and its failure to reproduce at the
placebo stated beside it, and it is not presented as a method
contribution. The practitioner reading of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal} is the content of that contrast,
not an endorsement of any family or wrapper. The one recommendation the
paper does make---that a calibration audit include a quiet control
period---is a recommendation about measurement, and it follows from the
fact that this study would have reported a different and wrong headline
without one. Explainability of the neural families---which features drive
their forecasts and whether the explanations are stable across the
break---is a separate question deferred to a companion paper that reuses
these fits.
