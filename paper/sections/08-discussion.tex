\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the family-level split is evidence of}
\label{sec:discussion-split}

The registration deliberately declined a directional claim on
neural-versus-classical ordering (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}), and the
working conjecture that motivated the audit---that coverage degradation
across the break would be larger for the neural families---is refuted in
its strong form by Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}. The split the shift regime
actually produced runs through the middle of the neural group. The arms
that held near-nominal marginal coverage through the break are the NB head
under all three of its mechanisms (0.933--0.972), CNN-LC under dropout
(0.962), the multi-output GP (0.905) and---on the cells its own rejection
rule admits, a selection caveat carried from
Table~\ref{tab:infeasible}---the sparse VAR (0.937--0.938). The arms that
broke are the four classical cores (0.624--0.799), LSTM-$\kappa_t$ under
both its mechanisms (0.647--0.685) and the neural-LC ensemble (0.694).

The two groups differ along a line the design did not manipulate. Every
broken arm forecasts through an extrapolated low-dimensional period
index---$\kappa_t$ and its cohort or CBD companions, the LSTM's learned
continuation of $\kappa_t$, the year feature that neural-LC evaluates at
future dates---and equips the extrapolation with intervals that propagate
the \emph{process risk} of that index: an innovation variance estimated
from pre-2020 residuals, drift-estimation uncertainty, and, for the
ensembles, variance across training seeds. Every arm that held carries its
uncertainty somewhere else: a count likelihood with a fitted dispersion
(the NB head), a posterior over functions of calendar time (the GP),
stochastic perturbation of the fitted map itself (dropout), a cross-age
innovation covariance with coefficient draws (the sparse VAR). When the
surface broke, the intervals that survived were the ones whose width came
from a distributional head or from integrating over the model's own
parameters, not from the historical smoothness of an index.

This is an observed association, not an established mechanism. The grid
crossed family with mechanism; it did not cross ``index extrapolation''
with ``distributional head'' as manipulated factors, and the families on
the two sides of the line differ in architecture, likelihood, feature set
and width at once. Four readings within the grid nevertheless discipline
the association. First, the mechanism moves coverage with the architecture
held fixed: on the same CNN-LC architecture, dropout covers 0.962 against
the ensemble's 0.882, and on neural-LC, 0.811 against 0.694
(Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}). Second, the family moves coverage
with the mechanism held fixed: under the same deep-ensemble mechanism the
NB head covers 0.956 where neural-LC covers 0.694 and the LSTM 0.647.
Third, the held arms are not simply vaguer: NB native and CNN-LC dropout
carry the two smallest 95\% interval scores among the distributional arms
(1.684 and 1.677; only the interval-constructing EnbPI wrappers of SVD-LC
and the LSTM, scored from their bounds, sit lower at 1.615 and 1.631),
while the one arm that visibly buys coverage with width---neural-LC
dropout, 0.811 at an interval score of 5.559---is identified by the width
penalty exactly as it should be. Fourth, adding parameter uncertainty to
the classical cores barely moves them (SVD-LC 0.692 to 0.727 and CBD
0.799 to 0.830 under the Poisson bootstrap): what the broken intervals
lacked at this break was not parameter risk, which is consistent with the
missing risk being a break in the process the index extrapolates. The age
resolution agrees: the families whose miss concentrates at ages
65--99---where the pandemic's excess mortality was---are the index
extrapolators (Table~\ref{tab:h4-age}), the pattern a level-and-drift miss
of one common factor loaded onto all ages would produce, while the
calibrated arms are near-flat in age. Agreement is not proof. Establishing
the mechanism would need designs this grid does not contain---a
distributional head grafted onto a classical core, or the NB head's
likelihood replaced while its features are kept---and that is a follow-on
study, not a reading of this one.

\subsection{Reading the conformal attribution}
\label{sec:discussion-conformal}

The Diebold--Mariano block of Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} is the crossed
design's attribution finding, and its sign pattern admits a one-sentence
practitioner reading: \emph{wrap what is broken; do not wrap what is
calibrated}. A conformal band is not a free safety layer laid over a
model's own uncertainty; it replaces that uncertainty with a
residual-quantile geometry, and the replacement is signed. It is
significantly better on the 95\% interval score for exactly the four
classical cores whose native law broke, and significantly worse for
exactly the three families whose native law had held---a premium paid in
width by a model whose own dispersion was already right. Conformalisation
here behaves as insurance: it pays out precisely when the model's own
uncertainty was wrong and costs a premium precisely when it was right,
and which side of the line a family falls on is a property of the
family--mechanism pair, not of the wrapper.

Three cautions temper the reading. First, the repair is empirical, not
guaranteed. Exchangeability between calibration and test years is violated
by design in the shift regime, so the finite-sample conformal guarantee
does not apply across the break; the wrappers held 95\% coverage of
0.848--0.981 anyway, which the coverage-gap bounds of
\citet{barber2023conformal} permit but do not promise. A practitioner who
adopts the wrapper for the next break inherits the same asterisk. Second,
the wrapper equalises the marginal level, not everything an actuary needs:
split and EnbPI bands that cover marginally still lose the path (joint
coverage 0.692--0.862 for the split arm), and the copula band that holds
the path (0.805--0.927) pays for it at the single level, where the
interval-score confidence sets of Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} eliminate it in
five families. The choice among wrappers is a choice of which quantity is
to be calibrated---the level or the trajectory---and
Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts} prices that choice without
adjudicating it. Third, a conformal cell has no distributional paths, so
it contributes nothing to the derived-quantity audit of
Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}: a practitioner who needs an interval on
$e_{65}$ or $\annuity$ from a wrapped model must first decide what a band
on log rates means for a functional across ages, a construction this
study did not register.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limitations}

The limitations in this subsection, through the itemised list below, were
written and committed before any shift-regime result was read, so that
none could be selected after the fact to excuse a finding or to inflate
one. They are retained in substance; where a pre-committed limitation now
bears on a specific result, the cross-reference is added and nothing is
removed. Two further limitations arose during the production sweeps and
the analysis and are stated, dated, at the end.

\subsubsection{The effective sample size is one}

Every shift-regime number in this paper is a statement about one pandemic
observed in twenty places. Clustering by population and imposing the null
in the bootstrap make the standard errors honest \emph{given the event}; they
do not make the event a sample of events. The audit therefore answers
``did these intervals survive this break?'' and, through the placebo,
``does the pattern resemble the previous break?''---not ``will these
intervals survive the next break?''. A reader who wants the last question
answered wants a different study, one that we cannot run until there is a
next break.

\subsubsection{The grid is ragged, so claims are contrasts}

Ten families by seven mechanisms is seventy cells on paper and fifty in
fact. Main effects over a ragged grid confound the factor with the pattern
of admissibility, so no main effect is reported; every claim is a contrast
within a sub-grid whose composition is stated. This costs generality: a
finding that conformal wrapping changes coverage for classical families
says nothing about neural families unless the neural sub-grid shows it
too, and we will not average across the two.

\subsubsection{Conformal cells carry secondary proper scores}

A conformal band is an interval, not a distribution. Drawing uniformly
inside it keeps the single interface honest for coverage, width and
interval score, but the CRPS, log score and PIT of those draws describe a
placeholder density. They are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:secondary},
flagged, and not ranked. Any reader who compares a conformal cell's CRPS
with a native cell's CRPS is comparing a convention with a forecast.
Conformal predictive systems that output a full calibrated distribution
would remove this limitation and are noted as future work.

\subsubsection{2024 may be revised}

HMD does not flag provisional years; it re-issues whole series at each
release, so the 2024 values are final only in the sense that this release
contains them. The shift regime's $h=5$ results rest on 2024, and two of the
twenty populations (USA, CHL) carry census-based exposures that HMD
documents as pending revision. The pre-registered robustness run drops 2024;
Addendum~1 adds runs that drop USA and CHL and that contrast register-based
with census-based populations. If the $h=5$ and $h\le4$ results disagree,
the $h\le4$ result is the one to trust and the $h=5$ result is reported as
provisional; if a later vintage appears before submission, the shift regime
is re-run on it and any change in the headline coverage is reported as a
vintage-revision effect.

\subsubsection{SVD Lee--Carter is over-dispersed by construction}

Validation gate~1 found that the classical two-stage SVD Lee--Carter
produces intervals slightly wider than nominal on data simulated from a
Poisson Lee--Carter process, because the SVD estimate of $\kappa_t$ absorbs
observation noise that then inflates the random-walk innovation variance.
This is a property of the method that \citet{girosi2007understanding}
would predict, not a harness artefact, and it has a direct consequence for
reading the results: an SVD-LC row that appears better calibrated than
Poisson-LC in the stable regime may be so partly because it is vaguer, and
the interval score and Murphy resolution term---not coverage---are the
columns that tell the two apart. In the shift tables this is visible as
SVD-LC's 0.692 native coverage against Poisson Lee--Carter's 0.624 on the
same panels: part of that gap is width, and the interval-score column is
the one that prices it.

\subsubsection{Other pre-committed limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Exchangeability is violated by design.} Conformal calibration
  residuals come from pre-cutoff years, and the test years are precisely
  the years chosen to differ from them. \citet{barber2023conformal} bound the
  resulting coverage gap; we measure it rather than assume it away. The
  conformal arms are audited, not endorsed
  (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal}).
\item \emph{Neural families are budget-limited.} Ensembles of ten over
  the origins, populations and sexes of three regimes fix the
  hyperparameter search to small pre-specified grids; the inner time split
  is small (five years). A better-tuned network might do better on point
  accuracy; whether it would be better calibrated is exactly what is not
  known, and the audited fragility of the year-feature extrapolation
  (Section~\ref{sec:design-families}) is a property of the specification,
  not a tuning accident.
\item \emph{Data quality in the placebo window.} Military deaths are
  reconstructed in the belligerent series and excluded in the civilian-only
  one. Placebo results are descriptive, are reported pooled and by the
  pre-specified strata, and the neutral register-based stratum carries the
  1918-versus-2020 comparison.
\item \emph{Single-age granularity.} Modelling ages $0$--$99$ singly keeps
  the old-age cells noisy in small populations; the age-cap-90 robustness
  run tests whether \Hfour\ is an artefact of that noise.
\item \emph{Rounding of deaths.} The log-score convention rounds fractional
  deaths; the CRPS-on-counts sensitivity exists to show that nothing turns
  on it.
\item \emph{One code path, one author.} A single evaluation module is a
  strength for consistency and a weakness for independent verification; the
  synthetic-truth gate and the \pkg{StMoMo} oracle are the checks, and the
  code is public.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Limitations recorded during the sweeps and analysis}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The GP rows audit a sixty-year-window variant} (Addendum~4,
  registered 2026-08-29, during the production sweeps and before any GP
  cell on a panel longer than sixty years had produced a result). The
  exact multitask GP was infeasible on the long panels at the available
  memory, so the family trains on the trailing sixty complete years under
  every mechanism; the cause, the memory figures and the disclosure of an
  earlier forty-year diagnostic are in
  Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}. Every GP number in this paper is
  therefore a statement about the sixty-year-window GP, not about the
  registered full-panel specification; the four populations whose panels
  were already within the cap are unchanged, and the GP's contrasts stay
  inside its own sub-grid.
\item \emph{The 99.5\% longevity tail is not computable from the persisted
  results} (recorded 2026-08-30, at analysis). The runner persists the
  2.5\% and 97.5\% sample quantiles of the derived quantities, not the
  99.5\% quantile, so the empirical frequency of a nominally
  once-in-200-year $\annuity$ exceedance described in
  Section~\ref{sec:actuarial} cannot be evaluated from the results files
  as they stand. It was descriptive, not registered; filling it requires
  re-scoring the cells with a persisted 99.5\% column, and until that run
  exists the paper makes no tail-frequency claim.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the stable and placebo regimes will and will not settle}
\label{sec:discussion-pending}

Two of the three registered regimes are still running, and this draft
asserts nothing derived from them. What their arrival can and cannot
change is fixed by the registration, and is stated here as design, not as
prediction.

The stable regime supplies the reference level. The comparative clause of
\Htwo---the departure $|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ grows from the
stable to the shift regime, per family and mechanism---and the registered
verdict on \Hfive---the shift-regime departure on $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$
exceeds the stable-regime departure, per arm---are computable only when
the stable files exist; the shift-side quantities in
Tables~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} and~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} are already in the
form those comparisons need. The stable half of \Htwo\ is labelled a
replication arm (Section~\ref{sec:design-regimes}) because stable-window
miscalibration of particular arms is already in the literature. What the
stable regime cannot do is alter any shift-regime number, and it cannot
convert the event study into a sample of events: a shift-minus-stable
difference is a statement about calibration at one break relative to
calibration at none, with the first limitation above intact.

The placebo regime is a second event, used descriptively by registration.
Its side-by-side (Table~\ref{tab:twin-crises}) can show whether the
patterns found here---the family-level split, the joint-path collapse, the
old-age concentration of the index extrapolators' miss---recur at a break
of different cause and different age profile, over a nine-year horizon, in
data a century older; the pre-specified strata carry the data-quality
reading, and its first test year, 1914, precedes the influenza wave. What
it cannot do: it supports no transfer regression and no formal test of
COVID-specificity, its eleven populations overlap only partly with the
shift regime's twenty, and recurrence or non-recurrence at two events
leaves the effective-sample-size limitation exactly where it is.

\subsection{What this paper does not do}

It does not propose a model, a repair, or a recommendation about which
family to use. The audit did find a mechanism that restores marginal
coverage at the break; it is stated as a signed contrast within the
registered sub-grids (Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs},
Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}), with its cost to the calibrated
families stated in the same table, and it is not presented as a method
contribution. The practitioner reading of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal} is the content of that contrast,
not an endorsement of any family or wrapper. Explainability of the neural
families---which features drive their forecasts and whether the
explanations are stable across the break---is a separate question deferred
to a companion paper that reuses these fits.
