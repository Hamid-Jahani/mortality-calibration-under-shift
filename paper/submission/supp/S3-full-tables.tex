\section{Full per-cell tables}
\label{sec:supp-tables}

This section carries the ten unabridged result tables in full. Each is the
complete family $\times$ mechanism $\times$ regime fragment, written by
\pkg{scripts/make\_tables.py} from the committed sweep outputs
(\pkg{results/<regime>.parquet} and \pkg{results/<regime>\_gp.parquet}) and
included here verbatim; the provenance comment at the head of each fragment
names its inputs and its generation date. The manuscript prints abridged views
of five of them (its Tables~3--7) and quotes headline figures from all ten in
its prose. Nothing here is a recomputation: the abridged and the full versions
come from one script run over one set of inputs, so every number the manuscript
states can be located in the corresponding table below.

Three reading conventions hold throughout, and are repeated in the individual
table notes. First, error rows---cells in which an arm produced no valid
row---enter no mean; every table reports $n$, the valid cells behind each mean,
beside $n_{\mathrm{err}}$, the excluded ones, and the reasons are enumerated
cell by cell in Section~S6. Second, the three conformal mechanisms (split
conformal, EnbPI, the copula band) construct a single interval at 95\%: their
50\% and 80\% columns are blank by design, their proper scores and PIT values
are placeholders flagged secondary and never ranked against distributional
arms, and their derived-quantity rows are reported as not applicable because
those functionals require predictive sample paths. Third, CBD (M5) is fitted on
ages 55--99 and scores 45 ages, so its rows never average with a full-age
family: its 25--64 age-band entry is a 55--64 stub and its life-table rows
carry no $e_0$. Every comparison drawn in the manuscript is made within a
sub-grid that respects these three restrictions.

One defect of the generated fragments is recorded here rather than repaired,
because this supplement is published as supplied and each fragment is
reproduced exactly as the table script writes it: the caption of the coverage
table below (Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage}) contains a malformed cross-reference
where it sends the reader to the ledger of excluded rows. The intended
destination is the infeasible-cell ledger of Section~S6, Tables~S11 and~S12.

\subsection{H1: rankings by point accuracy and by proper scores}

Table~\ref{tab:h1-rankings} backs \Hone. It ranks every distributional arm
three times per regime---by mean $\RMSE$ on log rates, by mean $\CRPS$ and by
mean Poisson log score---and reports the rank correlation between the
orderings. It is the evidence behind the manuscript's statement that the RMSE
and CRPS orderings agree at $\rho = 0.95$ while still carrying the registered
top-three inversion, and that against the Poisson log score the point-accuracy
ordering dissolves, $\rho = 0.12$ at the break and $-0.18$ in the quiet
control. The conformal arms are absent by construction, their proper scores
being placeholders, and CBD is ranked in a block of its own with its age
support stated. \Hone\ is carried in this study as a harness-validity check
rather than as a novel finding; this table is what makes the check auditable.

\subsection{H2: interval coverage and interval score}

Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage} is the fifty-cell coverage table in all three
regimes, including the thirty conformal rows that the manuscript's Table~4
omits in favour of the arms carrying their own predictive law. It backs \Htwo\
and the refutation of the one-sided form in which \Htwo\ was registered.
Specifically it carries: the classical cores' native 95\% coverage in the quiet
control (0.702 SVD Lee--Carter, 0.682 Poisson Lee--Carter, 0.731 APC/RH)
against the break (0.692, 0.624, 0.654), which is the evidence that the failure
predates the pandemic rather than being created by it; the split that runs
through the middle of the neural group rather than between neural and
classical, with the negative-binomial head, the multi-output Gaussian process
at 0.905 and the sparse VAR near nominal against the LSTM at 0.647 ensembled
and the ensembled neural Lee--Carter at 0.694; the conformal ranges quoted in
the results (0.890--0.948 for split conformal and EnbPI and 0.951--0.985 for
the copula arm in the control, 0.848--0.944 and 0.939--0.981 under the break);
the 50\% and 80\% columns behind the discussion's finding that the failure is
not a tail artefact; and the mean 95\% interval score $\IS_{95}$, which is what
separates an arm that is calibrated from an arm that is merely vague.

\subsection{H3: joint path coverage against marginal coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint} backs \Hthree. It sets mean marginal 95\% coverage,
pooled over horizons, beside joint coverage of the whole path ($H=5$ in the
shift and stable regimes, $H=9$ in the placebo) and reports their difference,
for all fifty arms in each regime. It carries the 100-of-100 count, the mean
gaps of 0.127 in the control and 0.168 across the break, the copula band's
joint ranges (0.894--0.942 in the control, 0.805--0.927 at the break, and
0.544--0.939 over the nine placebo horizons), and the placebo rows behind the
counter-example the manuscript keeps in its main text---the sparse VAR's
Poisson-bootstrap arm at 0.748 joint against 0.756 marginal, the one pair whose
ordering the rounded columns do not by themselves establish. Two
qualifications stated in the manuscript apply to every row here: the comparator
is the nominal independence value $0.95^{H}$, which is a re-specification of
the comparator as registered, and the model-implied joint comparator
$C^{\mathrm{impl}}$ is not computable from the columns the runner emits, so
these are the descriptive measurement of the marginal-to-joint gap rather than
the registered test of \Hthree.

\subsection{H4: coverage by age band}

Table~\ref{tab:h4-age} backs \Hfour\ and its refutation as a universal claim.
It gives 95\% coverage within the three Mondrian age bands (0--24, 25--64,
65--99) for all fifty arms in all three regimes. It carries the SVD
Lee--Carter profile the manuscript quotes in both regimes (0.888, 0.760, 0.501
in the control against 0.882, 0.725, 0.518 at the break), the LSTM ensemble's
likewise unmoved gradient, the CBD stub pair, and the per-arm values behind the
34-of-50 reversal count---26 of the 30 conformal arms, whose Mondrian strata
are calibrated on these same bands and so flatten or invert the profile by
construction, and the eight arms carrying a family's own uncertainty (both CBD
arms, both CNN-LC arms, the GP, neural-LC dropout and both Poisson Lee--Carter
arms), five of which reverse by five hundredths or less. It also carries the
margins, 0.044 and below, by which the negative-binomial head and the sparse
VAR preserve the registered direction. CBD's 25--64 column is a 55--64 stub,
CBD being fitted on ages 55--99; that row is not comparable with the full-age
families' and is not compared with them anywhere in the manuscript.

\subsection{H5: life expectancy and annuity factors}

Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial} backs \Hfive. It reports coverage of the nominal
95\% interval and the mean error for $e_0$, $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ at 2\% on
the period table of the first test year, computed from each arm's own latent
rate paths and compared with the same functional of the observed rates, for
every family, mechanism and regime. It carries the figures the manuscript
quotes---SVD Lee--Carter's native law covering 0.256 for $e_{65}$ and 0.253 for
$\annuity$ against 0.702 on log rates, the LSTM deep ensemble at 0.157, and the

populations and its sixteen of twenty-two placebo units. Ninety of its rows are
the conformal arms, reported as not applicable: these functionals require
predictive sample paths, and conformal mechanisms yield interval bounds on
$\log m_{x,t}$ only. CBD has no $e_0$.

\subsection{Probability integral transform}

Table~\ref{tab:pit} backs the distributional-calibration reading in the
results. It gives, per distributional arm and regime, the mean
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance of the randomised PIT from uniformity and the
share of population--sex cells whose nominal $p$-value falls below 0.05. It
carries the break-regime KS range the manuscript quotes, from 0.172 for the
multi-output GP to 0.374 for Poisson Lee--Carter, and the rejected-share floors
of 0.905 and 0.844. That $p$-value is descriptive only: PIT values within a
cell are dependent across ages and horizons, and all formal inference in this
study is population-clustered instead. Conformal rows are blank because their
PIT is a placeholder. The shape of each departure---U for over-confidence, hump
for under-confidence, slope for bias---is read from Figures~S10--S12 in
Section~S4.

\inputtable{tab-pit}

\subsection{Murphy decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:murphy} backs the decomposition subsection of the results. It
splits the Brier score of the 95\% interval-hit indicator into reliability,
resolution and uncertainty, and adds the reliability of the PIT against the
uniform reference. With one constant nominal level there is a single bin, so
the resolution term is zero by construction and is emitted as 0.0000 in every
row; the isotonic (CORP) estimator, which would supply a resolution component
with independent content, is not emitted, and no hypothesis in this study rests
on one. The table is what separates an arm that is calibrated from an arm that
looks calibrated by being vague---the comparison of SVD Lee--Carter against
Poisson Lee--Carter that coverage alone cannot decide. Both quantities are
descriptive.

\inputtable{tab-murphy}

\subsection{Diebold--Mariano tests and model confidence sets}

Table~\ref{tab:dm-mcs} carries the two registered forecast-comparison
procedures in all three regimes, and is the evidence behind the study's
conformal attribution finding: wrap what is broken, do not wrap what is not.
Its lower block gives the pairwise Diebold--Mariano comparison of each family's
native law against its split-conformal wrapping, with the mean loss
differential, the wild-cluster-bootstrap $p$-value over populations and the
effective number of clusters---the per-family differentials the manuscript
prints as digits, the break-regime $p$-range of 0.004--0.018, the control
$p$-values including APC/RH at 0.086 and CBD at 0.198, and the placebo range of
0.071--0.689. Its upper block gives each 90\% model confidence set: the arms
retained, the elimination $p$-values behind the five families in which the
copula band is eliminated at the break, and the cells kept and dropped by the
common-cell restriction, which is the intersection every inference statement in
the manuscript is conditioned on. A contrast that cannot be formed is listed
with its recorded reason; Contrasts~2 and~3 have no row here at all, which is
why their statistics are reported in full in the manuscript instead.

\subsection{Twin crises: 1914--1922 beside 2020--2024}

Table~\ref{tab:twin-crises} backs the twin-crises analysis. It sets the placebo
regime beside the shift regime, family by family and mechanism by mechanism,
with marginal 95\% and joint path coverage in each, and adds the three
pre-specified Addendum~1 strata as further columns---the exhibit that the
manuscript's limitation about reporting placebo results by stratum points at.
It carries the placebo family split that reproduces the pandemic's a century
earlier, the joint collapse with its 45-of-46 and 37-of-46 counts, the sparse
VAR's empty placebo cells, which are a finding rather than a gap, and the CBD
cross-crisis contrast. The two joint columns span $H=9$ and $H=5$ and are
therefore read within regime, never across it. The table is descriptive: no
common-cell restriction and no transfer regression is applied to it.
