\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Source, vintage and likelihood}

All data are from the Human Mortality Database \citep{hmd2026}, the
15~June~2026 release (DOI 10.4054/HMD.Countries.20260615, Methods
Protocol~v6 \citep{wilmoth2025methods}): the bulk \texttt{Deaths\_1x1},
\texttt{Exposures\_1x1} and \texttt{Mx\_1x1} files and the period life
tables, pinned byte-for-byte by that DOI, the download date (2026-08-25) and
SHA-256 checksums in \texttt{data/MANIFEST.sha256}. The user agreement bars
redistribution, but every table here reproduces from a matching fresh
download. This is the first vintage with twenty populations at \emph{final}
single-age annual data through 2024, which is what makes the shift regime
(Section~\ref{sec:design}) possible at $h=5$; one (DNK) reaches 2025, unused.
Only deaths and exposures are modelled; \texttt{Mx\_1x1} validates the parser
and the life tables validation gate~3.

Deaths are counts: $D_{x,t}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(E_{x,t}m_{x,t})$ with offset
$\log E_{x,t}$ \citep{brouhns2002poisson}; nothing here models rates as
Gaussian on the raw scale. Two least-squares historical baselines survive
(singular-value Lee--Carter on log rates, CBD on the logit of $q_{x,t}$), and
the LSTM fits $\kappa_t$ by squared error on the SVD stage, as its reference
paper does; losses are fixed in \texttt{docs/NEURAL-SPEC.md}, committed
before the neural sweep (Section~\ref{sec:design-families}).

\subsection{Cells, ages and structural zeros}

A cell is a (population, sex, year, single age) Lexis square with deaths
$D_{x,t}$ and central exposure $E_{x,t}$; sexes are modelled separately and
the \emph{Total} column is never used. We model ages $0$--$99$: above them the
published tables are partly smoothed model output (Kannisto above age~80),
the open group $110{+}$ needs a closure assumption no family shares, and
exposure vanishes---24{,}083 population--year--age rows carry $E_{x,t}=0$ for
at least one sex (15{,}494 for both), almost all at age~90 or above in the
small populations, where the offset is undefined.

A zero-exposure cell inside $0$--$99$ is a \emph{structural} zero, not a
missing value (population zero at both corners, $D_{x,t}=0$), so every family
carries $W_{x,t}=\ind\{E_{x,t}>0\}$, \pkg{StMoMo}'s \texttt{wxt} convention;
this leaves the maximum-likelihood estimate exactly unchanged, and
Addendum~3~\S1 records it as the numerically correct evaluation of the
registered objective, not a deviation. The shift training panel has 99 such
cells (ISL 43, FIN 27, SWE 12, CHE 11, LUX 6) at ages 95--99, in seven
population--sex series, all before 1970. In test windows an age without a
finite observation at every horizon is masked, and life-table functionals use
the maximal contiguous scored range from age~0 on both sides
(Addendum~3~\S3); this binds only in the placebo regime, at ages 98--99.
Zero-\emph{death} cells with positive exposure remain ordinary observations.

\subsection{Fractional deaths and the rounding convention}
\label{sec:data-rounding}

HMD death counts are not integers---deaths are split into Lexis triangles and
re-aggregated and unknown-age deaths redistributed, so a cell may record
$3842.31$---and a Poisson log score is undefined at such a count. We score at
$\tilde D_{x,t} = \lfloor D_{x,t} + \tfrac12 \rfloor$, nearest integer with
halves \emph{up} (round-half-to-even was rejected because its direction would
depend on the parity of the integer part), and report as a registered
sensitivity the $\CRPS$ of the predictive count distribution against the
unrounded $D_{x,t}$. Nine of the twenty shift populations carry non-integer
counts, two of them (JPN, USA) in essentially every cell; the other eleven
are exactly integer.

Zero observed deaths give no finite log rate, so rate-scale
quantities---observed and predictive alike---use the half-count correction
\begin{equation}
  y_{x,t} = \log\frac{\max(\tilde D_{x,t},\,\tfrac12)}{E_{x,t}},
  \label{eq:halfcount}
\end{equation}
which puts both sides of a rate-scale score on one lattice (Addendum~2~\S2,
Addendum~3~\S5); so do the conformal calibration residuals
(Section~\ref{sec:design-mechanisms}). No cell is dropped for having no
deaths; the test cells where the correction binds ($D_{x,t}<\tfrac12$) are
counted per population and sex for the shift window in
Table~\ref{tab:populations}, the full inventory being Table~S1. They sit in
the smallest
populations---ISL 117 female and 68 male of the 500 test cells per sex, LUX
110 and 72, at most 40 (EST female) elsewhere---a material fraction of the
2020--2024 window there, rare outside it. One registered exception
(Addendum~3~\S5): the \emph{observed} life table for \Hfive's realised $e_0$,
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ keeps raw fractional deaths, since $m_x=0$ where
nobody died is the correct life-table statement and half a death would move a
small population's observed $e_0$ past gate~3's tolerance.

One detail bears on the placebo regime: HMD's linear rule for splitting
$1\times1$ death squares into Lexis triangles carries a 1918--1919 influenza
correction extrapolated from Sweden and France (Protocol~v6, Appendix~A). It
changes the split, not the $1\times1$ counts we use, leaving a small
model-based imprint on the 1918--1919 exposures.

\subsection{Populations by regime}

Codes are HMD's; the sets were fixed in the pre-registration from an audit of
the files, not from results.

\paragraph{Shift (primary), 20 populations.} Those whose final data reach
2024: BEL, CHE, CHL, DNK, EST, FIN, HKG, HRV, ISL, JPN, KOR, LTU, LUX, LVA,
NOR, PRT, SVK, SWE, TWN, USA. Each trains on its maximal contiguous block of
complete years ending in 2019 (Addendum~3~\S2). The rule binds once:
Belgium's 1914--1918 data are missing at every age---1{,}000 modelled cells
($100$ ages $\times$ $2$ sexes $\times$ $5$ years), the only genuinely
missing data in the shift training panel---so BEL trains on 1919--2019.
Training lengths are very unequal, from seventeen years (KOR) and nineteen
(HRV) to 269 (SWE), which governs which mechanisms are constructible
(Section~\ref{sec:design-grid}).

\paragraph{Stable (control).} The same twenty at expanding origins
$T=1990,1992,\dots,2014$, an (origin, population, sex) triple admissible only
at fifteen or more complete training years (Addendum~3~\S4). The panel is
therefore unbalanced, and the effective cluster count per origin accompanies
every stable-regime summary.

\paragraph{Placebo break, 11 populations.} Continuous 1908--1922 coverage,
starting no later than 1900: CHE, DNK, FIN, FRATNP, GBRTENW, GBR\_SCO, ISL,
ITA, NLD, NOR, SWE. BEL is excluded for that same gap---555 rows of HMD's
``.'' missing marker in the raw $1\times1$ file, flagged in HMD's own
documentation---and FRACNP and GBRCENW as overlapping civilian variants of
FRATNP and GBRTENW, FRACNP additionally carrying 1919 denominators HMD calls
implausible, so it is not used even as a sensitivity. Addendum~1 (2026-08-26,
still before any real-data fit) stratifies the eleven from HMD's country
documentation: \emph{neutral, register-based} series whose only break in the
window is the 1918 influenza pandemic (CHE, DNK, FIN, ISL, NLD, NOR, SWE);
\emph{belligerent total-population} series with HMD-reconstructed military
deaths at male ages of roughly 18--45 (FRATNP, GBRTENW, ITA); and one
\emph{civilian-only} series excluding deaths abroad (GBR\_SCO). The neutral
stratum is the clean 1918-versus-2020 analogue, and the pooled placebo result
is always reported alongside the strata.

\subsection{Data-quality caveats carried into the analysis}

\emph{Exposures} are estimated from census or register populations and
births under conventions differing by country and era;
\citet{cairns2016phantoms} show cohort-size errors of several percent from
uneven historical births. We take them as given; the error is common to every
model in a cell.

\emph{The placebo window.} Pre-1950 data are patchier and some war years
reconstructed. The public HMD background file of each placebo population, the
Methods Protocol and the release notes were read before any placebo result
(\texttt{docs/DATA-PREREQS.md}); the login-only per-country ``Notes'' were
not. No population carries an explicit 1914--1922 quality flag, and beyond
the strata three sensitivities are pre-specified: drop GBR\_SCO, whose
1912--1938 population is interpolated by a spline HMD itself calls
unreliable; DNK with and without 1921--1922, South Jutland having been gained
in 1921, inside the test window; and the neutral stratum alone as the
cleanest pandemic-only comparison.

\emph{Revisability of 2024.} HMD has no per-year provisional flag and
re-issues whole series, so ``final through 2024'' is final only as of this
release: USA 2020--2025 exposures blend preliminary 2020-census inputs, CHL
exposures rest on the 2017 census pending revision, and TWN 2024 deaths are
provisional, while register-based populations (the Nordic countries, CHE,
EST, LVA, LTU) are effectively final. The pre-registered robustness run drops
2024 and re-scores at $h\le4$; Addendum~1 adds dropping USA and CHL, a
register-versus-census contrast, and a re-run on the next vintage.

\emph{Differing observation windows.} Series start between the
mid-eighteenth century and the 2000s, so the stable panel is unbalanced and
the shortest shift panels cannot support every mechanism; inference is
therefore clustered by population rather than pooled over cells, and every
contrast is computed on the cells common to the arms it compares
(Section~\ref{sec:design-grid}).

\subsection{Life-table conventions}

Life expectancy and annuity factors come from every predictive sample's $m_x$
vector through one period-life-table routine: $q_x = m_x/(1+(1-a_x)m_x)$;
$a_0 = 0.07+1.7\,m_0$ capped to $[0.01,0.35]$, a linear infant-separation
rule after \citet{andreev2015average}; $a_x=\tfrac12$ elsewhere;
constant-hazard closure $e_A = 1/m_A$. HMD's own table differs in its $a_x$
and old-age treatment, so validation gate~3 requires our $e_0$ and $e_{65}$
on HMD's published $m_x$ to reproduce HMD's within 0.15 years, the size of
those simplifications. Annuity factors follow the standard definition
\citep{dickson2020actuarial} on the period table treated as static
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics-actuarial}). A family defined only on part of the
age range---CBD, fitted on ages 55--99---yields a table truncated at its
first age: $e_0$ is then undefined, and $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ are computed
on the truncated table, predictive and observed sides alike, with the age
range recorded.
