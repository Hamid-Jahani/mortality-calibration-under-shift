\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Source, vintage and likelihood}

All data are from the Human Mortality Database \citep{hmd2026}, the
15~June~2026 release (DOI 10.4054/HMD.Countries.20260615, Methods
Protocol~v6 \citep{wilmoth2025methods}): bulk \texttt{Deaths\_1x1},
\texttt{Exposures\_1x1}, \texttt{Mx\_1x1} and the period life tables, pinned
by that DOI, the download date (2026-08-25) and SHA-256 checksums. It is the
first vintage with twenty populations at \emph{final} single-age data through
2024, which makes the shift regime possible at $h=5$; DNK reaches 2025,
unused. Only deaths and exposures are modelled; \texttt{Mx\_1x1} validates
the parser and the life tables gate~3.

Deaths are counts, $D_{x,t}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(E_{x,t}m_{x,t})$ with offset
$\log E_{x,t}$ \citep{brouhns2002poisson}, never Gaussian rates on the raw
scale; three historical baselines keep their reference papers' least-squares
fits (SVD Lee--Carter, CBD, the LSTM's $\kappa_t$ stage). Data construction,
and the loss of every family, is Supplementary Section~S2.

\subsection{Cells, ages and structural zeros}

A cell is a (population, sex, year, single age) Lexis square of deaths
$D_{x,t}$ and central exposure $E_{x,t}$; sexes are modelled separately, the
\emph{Total} column never used. Ages are $0$--$99$: above them the published
tables are partly smoothed model output (Kannisto above age~80), $110{+}$
needs a closure assumption no family shares, and exposure vanishes---24{,}083
rows carry $E_{x,t}=0$ for at least one sex (15{,}494 for both), almost all
at age~90 or above.

Inside $0$--$99$ a zero-exposure cell is a \emph{structural} zero, not missing
data: every family carries $W_{x,t}=\ind\{E_{x,t}>0\}$, \pkg{StMoMo}'s
\texttt{wxt} convention, which leaves the maximum-likelihood estimate
unchanged and which Addendum~3~\S1 records as a clarification, not a
deviation (Section~S2). The shift panel has 99 such cells, at ages 95--99
before 1970; zero-\emph{death} cells with positive exposure are ordinary
observations. Test ages without a finite observation at every horizon are
masked, and life-table functionals use the maximal contiguous scored range
from age~0 on both sides (Addendum~3~\S3), binding only in the placebo
regime, at ages 98--99.

\subsection{Fractional deaths and the rounding convention}
\label{sec:data-rounding}

HMD death counts are not integers---Lexis splitting, re-aggregation and
redistribution of unknown-age deaths give values such as $3842.31$, at which
a Poisson log score is undefined. We score at $\tilde D_{x,t} =
\lfloor D_{x,t} + \tfrac12 \rfloor$, halves \emph{up} (round-half-to-even was
rejected: its direction depends on the parity of the integer part), with the
$\CRPS$ of the predictive counts against the unrounded $D_{x,t}$ as a
registered sensitivity. Nine of the twenty shift populations carry
non-integer counts, two (JPN, USA) in essentially every cell; the other
eleven are exactly integer.

Zero observed deaths give no finite log rate, so rate-scale
quantities---observed, predictive and the conformal calibration residuals
alike---use the half-count correction $y_{x,t} = \log[\max(\tilde
D_{x,t},\,\tfrac12)/E_{x,t}]$, putting both sides on one lattice
(Addendum~2~\S2, Addendum~3~\S5). No cell is dropped for having no deaths;
cells where the correction binds ($D_{x,t}<\tfrac12$) concentrate in the
smallest populations---117 of ISL's 500 female test cells---and are counted
per population and sex in Table~S1. By registered exception
(Addendum~3~\S5) the \emph{observed} life table for \Hfive's realised $e_0$,
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ keeps raw fractional deaths: $m_x=0$ where nobody
died is the correct life-table statement, and half a death would move a small
population's observed $e_0$ past gate~3's 0.15-year tolerance.

\subsection{Populations by regime}

The sets were fixed in the pre-registration by an audit of the files, not
from results; codes are HMD's. The \emph{shift} and \emph{stable} regimes use
the twenty populations whose final data reach 2024 (Table~S1). Belgium's
1914--1918 data are missing at every age---1{,}000 modelled cells, the only
genuinely missing data in the shift training panel---so BEL trains on
1919--2019. Training lengths run from seventeen years (KOR) and nineteen
(HRV) to 269 (SWE), which governs which mechanisms are constructible
(Section~\ref{sec:design-grid}).

The \emph{placebo} regime is the eleven with continuous 1908--1922 coverage
starting no later than 1900: CHE, DNK, FIN, FRATNP, GBRTENW, GBR\_SCO, ISL,
ITA, NLD, NOR, SWE. BEL is excluded for that same gap (555 rows of HMD's
``.'' missing marker), FRACNP and GBRCENW as civilian variants overlapping
FRATNP and GBRTENW, FRACNP also carrying 1919 denominators HMD calls
implausible. Addendum~1 (2026-08-26, pre-fit) stratifies them from HMD's
country documentation: \emph{neutral, register-based}, whose only break in
the window is the 1918 influenza pandemic (CHE, DNK, FIN, ISL, NLD, NOR,
SWE); \emph{belligerent total-population}, carrying HMD-reconstructed
military deaths at male ages of roughly 18--45 (FRATNP, GBRTENW, ITA); and
one \emph{civilian-only} series excluding deaths abroad (GBR\_SCO). The
neutral stratum is the clean 1918-versus-2020 analogue; pooled placebo
results are always reported alongside the strata.

\subsection{Data-quality caveats}

\emph{Exposures} are estimated from census or register populations under
conventions differing by country and era, with cohort-size errors of several
percent \citep{cairns2016phantoms}; they are taken as given, the error being
common to every model in a cell.

\emph{The placebo window.} Pre-1950 data are patchier and some war years
reconstructed. Each placebo population's public HMD background file, the
Methods Protocol and the release notes were read before any placebo result
(\texttt{docs/DATA-PREREQS.md}); the login-only per-country ``Notes'' were
not, and no population carries an explicit 1914--1922 quality flag. Three
sensitivities beyond the strata are pre-specified: dropping GBR\_SCO, whose
1912--1938 population HMD interpolates by a spline it calls unreliable; DNK
with and without 1921--1922, South Jutland having been gained in 1921 inside
the test window; and the neutral stratum alone. HMD's Lexis-triangle split
also carries a 1918--1919 influenza correction extrapolated from Sweden and
France (Protocol~v6, Appendix~A), which changes the split, not the
$1\times1$ counts we use.

\emph{Revisability of 2024.} HMD has no per-year provisional flag and
re-issues whole series, so ``final through 2024'' is final only as of this
release: USA 2020--2025 exposures blend preliminary 2020-census inputs, CHL's
rest on the 2017 census pending revision, TWN 2024 deaths are provisional;
register-based populations (the Nordics, CHE, EST, LVA, LTU) are effectively
final. The registered robustness run drops 2024 and re-scores at $h\le4$;
Addendum~1 adds dropping USA and CHL, a register-versus-census contrast and a
next-vintage re-run.

\emph{Life tables.} One period-life-table routine serves predictive samples
and the observed side alike (Section~S2), gate~3 requiring our $e_0$ and
$e_{65}$ on HMD's published $m_x$ to reproduce HMD's within 0.15~years. CBD,
fitted on ages 55--99, yields a table truncated at its first age: $e_0$ is
undefined, and $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ use the truncated table on both sides,
with the age range recorded.
