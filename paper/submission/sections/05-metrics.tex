\section{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Targets and denominators}
\label{sec:metrics-target}

Two targets are scored at age $x$ and horizon $h$ from origin $T$: the half-up
rounded death count $\tilde D_{x,h}=\lfloor D_{x,T+h}+\tfrac12\rfloor$
(Section~\ref{sec:data-rounding}) and the log rate
$y_{x,h}=\log\{\max(\tilde D_{x,h},\tfrac12)/E_{x,T+h}\}$. A distributional
cell is scored on the Poisson-inclusive samples $z^{(j)}_{x,h}$ of its $n$
latent rate paths (Section~\ref{sec:design-interface}), a conformal cell on
its own 95\% interval $[l_{x,h},u_{x,h}]$ (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
Estimators are in Supplementary Section~S2.

Scores are negatively oriented and averaged over the ages a cell is defined on
(100, or 45 for CBD), never across arms with different age supports; averaging
is within population--sex, then equal- not exposure-weighted across
populations (Section~\ref{sec:related}). Denominators are $n=40$
population--sex units under the shift, 22 in the placebo, up to 442 stable;
errored cells excluded, counted as $n_{\mathrm{err}}$ in every supplementary
table.

\subsection{Point accuracy, CRPS and the Poisson log score}
\label{sec:metrics-logscore}

Point accuracy is $\RMSE$ of the median error $\hat y_{x,h}-y_{x,h}$, and the
error $\hat e_0-e_0$ in period life expectancy at birth. The CRPS
\citep{gneiting2007strictly} is strictly proper and equals MAE for a point
forecast; it is computed on the rate scale, and on the count scale against the
\emph{unrounded} $D_{x,T+h}$ as a registered sensitivity free of any rounding
convention.

The predictive count law is the equal-weight mixture over paths,
$\LS=-\log\{n^{-1}\sum_j\mathrm{Poisson}(\tilde
D_{x,h};E_{x,T+h}m^{(j)}_{x,T+h})\}$. HMD deaths are fractional
(Section~\ref{sec:data}), so the log score is taken at the half-up rounded
count, with count-scale CRPS as its sensitivity; both conventions were fixed
before estimation.

\subsection{Interval score, coverage and joint path coverage}
\label{sec:metrics-interval}
\label{sec:metrics-coverage}

The interval (Winkler) score $\IS_\alpha$ adds to the width a $2/\alpha$
penalty per unit outside the interval
\citep{winkler1972decision,gneiting2007strictly}, and is always reported with
(coverage, mean width), never coverage alone. Bounds are
Poisson-inclusive sample quantiles at $\alpha\in\{0.5,0.2,0.05\}$, or the
conformal band itself.

\emph{Marginal} coverage is the pointwise hit rate, by horizon and pooled.
\emph{Joint path} coverage
$C^{\mathrm{joint}}=\overline{\prod_{h\le H}\ind\{l_{x,h}\le y_{x,h}\le
u_{x,h}\}}$ ($H=5$ under the shift and in the control, $H=9$ in the placebo)
is bounded above by the minimum marginal coverage, which is why \Hthree's
comparator was re-specified (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}). The
re-specified quantity is $C^{\mathrm{impl}}-C^{\mathrm{joint}}$, of either
sign, with the \emph{model-implied} $C^{\mathrm{impl}}$ the mean fraction of a
cell's own paths inside its own pointwise bands at every horizon. The runner
emits realised but not model-implied joint coverage, so $C^{\mathrm{impl}}$ is
not computable from the emitted columns and the registered test of
Addendum~3~\S8 awaits recomputation from the cells' paths; the
descriptive $C^{\mathrm{marg}}-C^{\mathrm{joint}}$ is reported instead
(Section~\ref{sec:results}, Table~S4), and copula conformal reads
$C^{\mathrm{joint}}$ off its simultaneous band with no implied value.

\subsection{PIT, calibration by age and reliability}

A forecaster is calibrated if $F(Y)$ is uniform
\citep{gneiting2007probabilistic,dawid1984present}, with the randomised PIT of
\citet{czado2009predictive} for lattice ties. Per-cell histograms,
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distances, shapes and rejection shares at a nominal $0.05$
are in Table~S7 and Figures~S10--S12; that $p$-value is \emph{descriptive
only}, PIT values being dependent across ages and horizons (Addendum~2~\S4),
and the formal test is a cluster bootstrap of the pooled histogram.

Coverage and PIT are also computed within the age bands $0$--$24$, $25$--$64$,
$65$--$99$ and by single age. \Hfour\ compares $65$--$99$ with
$25$--$64$ under the shift, with a reversal of the registered direction
reported as such (Section~\ref{sec:design-status}). CBD, fitted on ages
55--99, has an empty $0$--$24$ band and a 55--64 stub for $25$--$64$, not
comparable with the full-age families.

The registered Murphy split \citep{murphy1973new,pohle2020murphy} is not
delivered. Against one constant nominal level, Brier resolution is 0.0000 in
every emitted row and reliability restates the coverage gap, exceeding it by
the across-cell coverage variance (SVD Lee--Carter native, control: 0.0921
against $(0.95-0.702)^2=0.0615$). The PIT-scale divergence form
\citep{broecker2009reliability} sees more---miscalibration never below 0.0229,
where Brier reliability reaches 0.0005---but there reliability and resolution
coincide numerically, so no independent resolution component is emitted
\citep{hersbach2000decomposition,dimitriadis2021stable}. Both are descriptive
(Table~S8).

\subsection{Actuarial functionals}
\label{sec:metrics-actuarial}

Each path's rates become a period life table (Section~\ref{sec:data}), from
latent rather than Poisson-inclusive paths and by the same routine as the
realised tables, giving samples of $e_x$ and of the annuity-due
$\annuity=\sum_{t\ge0}v^{t}\,{}_t p_{65}$ at $v=1/1.02$ on the first test
year's table \citep{dickson2020actuarial}. Per regime we report the median
error and the coverage and width of the central 95\% interval, the ex-post
coverage to which Addendum~3~\S8 restricts \Hfive\ against attenuation
(Section~\ref{sec:design-status}); the registered 99.5\% longevity quantile is
replaced by breach frequencies because the runner did not emit it
(Sections~\ref{sec:actuarial} and~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Forecast-comparison inference}
\label{sec:metrics-inference}

The cluster is the population: $G=20$ in the shift and stable regimes, $G=11$
in the placebo, fewer where a design floor or the common-cell rule removes
one, with the effective $G$ beside every statistic in Table~S9. Per-unit
losses are per-horizon means over ages within a population--sex--origin:
rate-scale CRPS among distributional arms, the 95\% interval score wherever a
conformal arm enters. A non-finite loss halts the computation rather than
being imputed---imputation was measured to invert a model confidence set
silently---and CBD never shares a loss matrix with the full-age arms.

Loss differentials enter Diebold--Mariano statistics
\citep{diebold1995comparing} with a population-cluster-robust variance and a
wild cluster bootstrap null with six-point weights
\citep{cameron2008bootstrap,webb2023reworking,mackinnon2017wild}, $B=4999$;
model confidence sets \citep{hansen2011model} at 90\% resample populations as
blocks (2{,}000 replications). \Hone\ checks rank agreement between RMSE and
CRPS, not by bootstrap (Section~\ref{sec:design-status});
\Htwo--\Hfive\ are paired contrasts under that bootstrap: shift minus stable
$|\text{coverage}-0.95|$ for \Htwo, shift minus stable departure from nominal
for $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ for \Hfive, and the differences defined above for
\Hthree\ and \Hfour.
