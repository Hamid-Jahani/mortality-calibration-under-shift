\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The break sorts the families; it does not create the miscalibration}
\label{sec:discussion-standing}

Most of the interval failure measured here predates any break; the break mainly
re-orders the families by how far they already stood from nominal, and on which
side. In the stable control---thirteen origins, 1990--2014, $h=1,\dots,5$, no
structural event in any test year---the classical cores cover 0.702 (SVD
Lee--Carter), 0.682 (Poisson Lee--Carter), 0.731 (APC/RH) and 0.901 (CBD)
against a nominal 0.95, and LSTM-$\kappa_t$ 0.638 and 0.667 under its two
mechanisms (Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main}; all 150 rows in Table~S3). This is not
a tail artefact: those three full-age cores cover 0.324--0.365 at a nominal
0.50 and 0.522--0.566 at 0.80 (Table~S3). A reader seeing only the shift regime
would credit SVD Lee--Carter's 0.692 in 2020--2024 to the pandemic; 0.702 of it
was there in 1990--2014.

\Htwo\ as registered---every family under-covers at 95\% at the break---is
therefore not supported, and the two-sided restatement of Addendum~3~\S8, which
tests $|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ with the direction free by arm, holds
in 27 of the 50 admissible cells. Raw coverage falls in 34 of the 50 and rises
in 16, so the modal movement is downward as the one-sided form anticipated; but
a fall is degradation only for an arm that began below nominal, and folding
about nominal splits one movement into growth on one side and shrinkage on the
other. Four behaviours follow (Table~S3): standing failures barely moved (SVD
Lee--Carter 0.702 to 0.692) or deepened (APC/RH 0.731 to 0.654); near-nominal
arms genuinely damaged (the multi-output GP 0.945 to 0.905; the sparse VAR's
split-conformal band 0.948, a registered departure of 0.002, to 0.910); CBD,
which began 0.049 from nominal and whose departure grows 0.049 to 0.151 as
coverage falls 0.901 to 0.799, the grid's largest growth but from an unsound
start; and over-wide arms pulled towards nominal (the sparse VAR's native law
0.988 to 0.937), posting smaller registered departures at the break than
before it.

\Hthree: joint path coverage lies below marginal coverage in all 100 of the 100
(regime, cell) pairs of the stable and shift regimes, mean gaps 0.127 and 0.168
(Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint-main}; full table S4). But $0.95^5=0.774$ implies a gap of
0.176 under independence, larger than either mean, so a negative gap is
evidence of nothing. Section~\ref{sec:design-status} recorded that before any
result existed, which is why the comparator was re-specified to each cell's own
simulated-path-implied joint coverage; the runner never emitted that column
(Section~\ref{sec:results-joint}), so \Hthree\ returns no registered verdict
here and the gap is a measurement, not a test. Against independence at each
arm's own realised marginal rate, $\text{marginal}^H$, the sign reverses---joint
coverage exceeds it in 50 of the 50 stable cells, 50 of the 50 shift cells and
45 of the 46 placebo cells, by as much as $+0.34$---so path misses are strongly
positively dependent, the dependence \citet{dowd2010backtesting} name in a
footnote and set aside. It does not suffice. Joint coverage still falls below
0.774 in 21 of the 50 stable cells and 30 of the 50 at the break; the four arms
at or above 0.95 jointly in the control (the NB head 0.956 and 0.953, the
sparse VAR 0.951 and 0.985) reach it only from marginal rates of 0.988 or
higher, that is, by being over-wide at the single level, and at the break none
does; and the copula band, the one mechanism built for the trajectory, leads
eight of ten family blocks in the control and nine of ten at the break yet is
beaten inside its own family by the NB head's dropout arm, 0.956 to 0.922 and
0.897 to 0.868. Outside a few over-wide arms the path is not covered at the
advertised rate in either regime.

\emph{A calibration audit must include a quiet control period.} That is the
strongest recommendation here, and it concerns measurement rather than models:
a shift-only audit cannot separate a model that broke from one never
calibrated, nor either from one that was over-wide and got lucky. Read on the
shift regime alone, the sparse VAR's native law is the grid's best-calibrated
native arm---on the 29 of 40 population--sex units its own rejection rule
admits---at 0.937, a registered departure of 0.013; read against its own
control, it earns that rank by having been over-wide at 0.988 by roughly what
the shock consumed, and nothing says the next shock is sized to consume it
again.

\subsection{What the family-level split is evidence of}
\label{sec:discussion-split}

The registration declined a directional claim on neural-versus-classical
ordering (Section~\ref{sec:intro}), and the conjecture that degradation would
be larger for the neural families is refuted in its strong form: the split runs
through the middle of the neural group, in the control as well as at the break,
with the NB head, the GP, CNN-LC under dropout and---on the cells its own
rejection rule admits, a selection caveat carried from Table~S11---the sparse
VAR near nominal, and the classical cores, LSTM-$\kappa_t$ and the neural-LC
ensemble far from it (Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main}). The calibrated arms carry
their uncertainty in a distributional head or in integration over the model's
own parameters, the others in the process risk of an extrapolated period index.
That is an observed association, not an established mechanism: the grid crossed
family with mechanism, not index extrapolation with distributional head, and
the two sides differ in architecture, likelihood, feature set and width at
once. Four readings discipline it, each with a stable value beside its shift
value (Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}): mechanism moves coverage with
architecture fixed; family moves coverage with mechanism fixed; adding
parameter uncertainty barely moves the classical cores in either regime, so
what they lack is not parameter risk; and the calibrated arms are not simply
vaguer---in the quiet control the two smallest 95\% interval scores among
full-age distributional arms belong to the two best-covering native laws
($\IS_{95}$ 1.455 for the NB head at 0.975 coverage and 1.474 for the sparse
VAR at 0.988) against 2.330 for SVD Lee--Carter at 0.702 and 3.056 for Poisson
Lee--Carter at 0.682, while the one arm that visibly buys coverage with width,
neural-LC dropout at 0.811 for an interval score of 5.559, is caught by the
width penalty (Table~S3). CBD is excluded from those comparisons because its
55--99 age support is not the full-age families'. Agreement is not proof: the
mechanism would need designs this grid does not contain, and that is a
follow-on study.

\subsection{The age profile is standing, and it settles a disagreement with the prior literature}
\label{sec:discussion-age}

\Hfour\ registered a concentration of coverage failure at the old ages. The
result is family-specific rather than universal (Section~\ref{sec:results}),
and where it holds it is again a property of the quiet control: SVD
Lee--Carter's native intervals cover 0.888, 0.760 and 0.501 across ages 0--24,
25--64 and 65--99 in the stable regime against 0.882, 0.725 and 0.518 at the
break (Table~\ref{tab:h4-age-main}; all 150 rows in Table~S5), and the LSTM
ensemble's gradient is likewise unmoved---a standing feature of an interval
built by extrapolating one index whose loadings are largest where the death
counts are. APC/RH is the counter-case of a genuinely break-induced gradient
(0.751, 0.729, 0.720, flat in age in the control, against 0.732, 0.661 and
0.589 at the break); Poisson Lee--Carter is uniformly poor in both regimes with
a shallow profile running the other way; the neural-LC dropout arm's collapse
at the young ages is standing too, 0.529 against 0.538.

This settles a disagreement in the published record.
\citet{dowd2010backtesting} report their age-65 forecasts as notably upward
biased, with realised values often at or below the lower bound, and their
age-84 forecasts as only slightly biased---the higher age behaving better, on
two ages of one family group. On thirteen origins, eighteen populations and the
full 0--99 range, with no break in the test years, the ordering for the
index-extrapolating families runs the other way and runs steeply, 0.888 at
0--24 against 0.501 at 65--99. The disagreement is not about the pandemic but
about age coverage of an extrapolated index measured over a wider age range,
more origins and more populations than the two-age comparison the earlier
finding rested on. What survives from that literature is the
narrower point that the ordering is not universal, which our evidence makes
more strongly: in the shift regime the registered direction is reversed in 34
of the 50 cells---in 26 of the 30 conformal arms (SVD Lee--Carter's split arm
covers 0.897 at 25--64 against 0.945 at 65--99, its EnbPI arm 0.884 against
0.938; all thirty in Table~S5) and in every arm of CBD, CNN-LC, Poisson
Lee--Carter and the GP. The NB head and the sparse VAR preserve it in four of
their five arms each, but by margins of 0.044 and below and mostly inside bands
at or above nominal (Table~S5), which is a different phenomenon from the steep
gradient \Hfour\ was written about. The old-age concentration is thus a
property of index extrapolation rather than of mortality forecasting at large,
and a conformal wrapper does not merely flatten it: on 26 of 30 wrapped arms it
tips the gradient the other way.

\subsection{Derived quantities carry the least reliable intervals in the study, and already do so in quiet years}
\label{sec:discussion-derived}

The result an actuarial reader should take from this study is not on the rate
surface. Intervals on period life expectancy and the annuity-due factor are
worse calibrated than the rate-level intervals of the same arms in every
full-age family, and for the index-extrapolating families the deterioration is
already present in the quiet control at close to full size: SVD Lee--Carter's
native law covers 0.256 for $e_{65}$ and 0.253 for $\annuity$ against 0.702 on
log rates (Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial-main}; all rows in Table~S6). An annuity
interval advertising nineteen times in twenty thus covers its realised value
about a quarter of the time one year ahead of thirteen quiet origins---a
statement about ordinary valuation practice, not about crisis resilience. The
mechanism is the age-axis analogue of \Hthree\ that Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}
sets out, and it answers the attenuation alternative of
\citet{cairns2011mortality} in the quiet control as well as at the break:
integration concentrates the failure rather than averaging it away.

Two qualifications keep the claim honest. CBD is the exception: on its
truncated 55--99 table integration does not hurt it, its native law covering
0.930 on $e_{65}$ and 0.889 on $\annuity$ against 0.901 on rates, its
Poisson-bootstrap companion 0.950 and 0.934 against 0.921. And for arms well
calibrated on rates the failure is largely a break effect rather than a
standing one: the sparse VAR's native law loses 0.008 between rates and
$\annuity$ in the control (0.988 against 0.980) and 0.351 at the break (0.937
against 0.586), the GP 0.082 against 0.311, the NB head's ensemble 0.174
against 0.506. The sorting logic of Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing}
recurs with it: several badly under-covering arms cover \emph{better} on the
derived quantities at the break---SVD Lee--Carter's native $\annuity$ coverage
goes 0.253 to 0.450---while arms that covered well in quiet years collapse, the
GP from 0.863 to 0.594 and the NB head's ensemble from 0.814 to 0.450, CBD
again excepted (0.930 and 0.889 falling only to 0.875 and 0.850, a
deterioration, not a collapse). The first movement is not a repair: SVD
Lee--Carter under-states $e_{65}$ systematically, its median path sitting a mean
of 0.84 years below the realised value in the control and 0.57 years below it
at the break (the error column of Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial-main}), so coverage
rose because a standing under-projection of longevity was partly cancelled by a
mortality shock. Those two means rest on different support---thirteen origins
over eighteen populations against one origin over twenty, two of which the
stable regime cannot score (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-limitations})---so the
narrowing describes the two columns as they stand and is not an estimate of a
period effect. A reserving basis that appears to improve under a pandemic for
that reason has not been validated by the pandemic.

\subsection{Reading the conformal attribution}
\label{sec:discussion-conformal}

The Diebold--Mariano block of Table~S9 is the crossed design's attribution
finding, and its sign pattern reads: \emph{wrap what is broken; do not wrap
what is calibrated}. A conformal band does not add to a model's own
uncertainty, it replaces it with a residual-quantile geometry, and the
replacement is signed: better on the 95\% interval score for exactly the four
classical cores whose native law is wrong, worse for exactly the three families
whose native law is sound, with all seven contrasts significant at the break
($p\le0.019$). Nor is this a pandemic effect. Each of the seven carries the
same sign in the stable regime as at the break with a mean loss differential of
comparable size, and five are already significant at the 5\% level in the quiet
control, APC/RH being marginal ($p=0.086$) and CBD indistinguishable from zero
($p=0.198$). Conformalisation thus behaves as insurance in ordinary years and
not only across breaks, and which side of the line a family falls on is a
property of the family--mechanism pair rather than of the wrapper or of the
regime.

Four cautions temper the reading. First, the pattern does not reproduce at the
second break: in the placebo regime all six families admitting the contrast
return point estimates favouring the native law, only the NB head's reaching
significance ($-0.461$, $p=0.008$, keeping its sign from the other two
regimes), CBD's reversal marginal ($-0.476$, $p=0.071$) and the rest
indistinguishable from zero ($p\ge0.127$); over nine horizons, on eleven
populations---seven for the GP---with calibration residuals from pre-1914 data,
the wrapper's advantage for the classical cores is not there. Second, the
repair is empirical, not guaranteed: exchangeability is violated by design in
the shift regime, so the finite-sample conformal guarantee does not apply
across the break; the wrappers held 95\% coverage of 0.848--0.981 anyway, which
the coverage-gap bounds of \citet{barber2023conformal} permit but do not
promise, and whoever adopts the wrapper for the next break inherits the same
asterisk. Third, the wrapper equalises the marginal level, not everything an
actuary needs: split and EnbPI bands that cover marginally still lose the path
(joint coverage 0.760--0.842 in the control and 0.692--0.862 at the break for
the split arm, against the 0.774 independence at the nominal level would
already give), and the copula band that holds the path pays at the single
level, where the interval-score confidence sets (Table~S9) eliminate it in five
families at the break---SVD Lee--Carter ($p=0.08$, marginal at the 90\% level),
LSTM-$\kappa_t$ (0.01), the NB head (0.03), neural-LC (0.04) and the sparse VAR
(0.00)---and in two, CBD and the sparse VAR, in the quiet control. Choosing
among wrappers is choosing which quantity is calibrated, the level or the
trajectory, and Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts} prices that choice without
adjudicating it. Fourth, a conformal cell has no distributional paths and so
contributes nothing to the derived-quantity audit of
Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}: an interval on $e_{65}$ or $\annuity$ from a
wrapped model would first require deciding what a band on log rates means for a
functional across ages, a construction this study did not register.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limits}

All but the last three items were committed before any regime result was read,
so that none could be selected afterwards to excuse or inflate a finding; they
are retained in substance. The last three arose during the sweeps and the
analysis and are dated.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The effective sample size is one.} Every shift-regime number
  describes one pandemic in twenty places, sixteen for the GP. Population
  clustering and imposing the null make the standard errors honest \emph{given
  the event}, not the event a sample of events, and the stable control does not
  change that. The audit answers whether these intervals survived this break,
  whether they were calibrated before it and, through the placebo, whether the
  pattern resembles the previous break---not whether they survive the next.
\item \emph{The grid is ragged, so claims are contrasts.} Seventy cells on
  paper, fifty in fact; main effects would confound the factor with
  admissibility, so none is reported and every claim is a contrast within a
  stated sub-grid, never averaged across the classical and neural halves. The
  27-of-50 and 34-of-50 counts are descriptive tallies carrying no inference:
  the design is not balanced.
\item \emph{Conformal cells carry secondary proper scores.} A band is not a
  distribution: uniform draws inside it keep coverage, width and interval score
  honest, but their CRPS, log score and PIT describe a placeholder density,
  reported flagged and unranked (Section~S5), so comparing a conformal cell's
  CRPS with a native cell's compares a convention with a forecast. Conformal
  predictive systems would remove this limitation.
\item \emph{2024 may be revised.} HMD flags no provisional years and re-issues
  whole series at each release. The shift regime's $h=5$ results rest on 2024,
  and two of its twenty populations (USA, CHL) carry census-based exposures
  documented as subject to later revision. The pre-registered robustness run
  drops 2024; Addendum~1 adds runs dropping USA and CHL and contrasting
  register- with census-based populations. Where $h=5$ and $h\le4$ disagree the
  latter is the one to trust and the former is reported as provisional; a later
  vintage would be re-run and any change in headline coverage reported as a
  vintage-revision effect.
\item \emph{SVD Lee--Carter is over-dispersed by construction.} Validation
  gate~1 found its intervals slightly wider than nominal on data simulated from
  a Poisson Lee--Carter process, because the SVD estimate of $\kappa_t$ absorbs
  observation noise that inflates the random-walk innovation variance---as
  \citet{girosi2007understanding} would predict, not a harness artefact. An
  SVD-LC row that looks better calibrated than Poisson-LC (0.702 against 0.682
  in the control, 0.692 against 0.624 at the break) may be so partly by being
  vaguer; the interval score and the Murphy resolution term (Table~S8), not
  coverage, separate them.
\item \emph{Exchangeability is violated by design.} Conformal calibration
  residuals come from pre-cutoff years and the test years are precisely those
  chosen to differ from them; \citet{barber2023conformal} bound the coverage
  gap, and we measure it rather than assume it away. The conformal arms are
  audited, not endorsed.
\item \emph{Neural families are budget-limited.} Ensembles of ten over the
  origins, populations and sexes of three regimes fix the hyperparameter search
  to small pre-specified grids on a five-year inner time split. Better tuning
  might improve point accuracy; whether it would improve calibration is exactly
  what is not known, and the audited fragility of the year-feature extrapolation
  (Section~\ref{sec:design-families}) is a property of the specification, not a
  tuning accident.
\item \emph{Data quality in the placebo window.} Military deaths are
  reconstructed in the belligerent series and excluded in the civilian-only one;
  placebo results are descriptive, reported pooled and by the pre-specified
  strata (Table~S10), the neutral register-based stratum carrying the
  1918-versus-2020 comparison.
\item \emph{Three further pre-committed items.} Modelling ages 0--99 singly
  keeps old-age cells noisy in small populations, and the age-cap-90 run tests
  whether \Hfour\ is an artefact of that noise; the log-score convention rounds
  fractional deaths, and the CRPS-on-counts sensitivity exists to show nothing
  turns on it; and one evaluation module by one author is a strength for
  consistency and a weakness for independent verification, checked by the
  synthetic-truth gate and the \pkg{StMoMo} oracle, with the code public.
\item \emph{The GP rows audit a sixty-year-window variant on a reduced
  population set} (Addendum~4, registered 2026-08-29 during the sweeps, before
  any GP cell on a longer panel had produced a result). The exact multitask GP
  was infeasible on the long panels at the available memory, so the family
  trains on the trailing sixty complete years under every mechanism; cause,
  memory figures and an earlier forty-year diagnostic are in
  Section~\ref{sec:design-deviations}. Every GP number in this paper describes
  that variant, not the registered full-panel specification. Its own minimum
  training-block rule also removes populations, differently by regime: sixteen
  in the stable control and sixteen in the shift regime against eighteen and
  twenty for the other nine families, and eight of the placebo's eleven, its
  native placebo rows resting on sixteen of the twenty-two population--sex units
  (Table~S6). Every GP comparison stays inside its own sub-grid.
\item \emph{The stable control is scored on eighteen populations, not twenty}
  (recorded 2026-09-02 at the stable QA gate). HRV and KOR enter HMD too late to
  meet the registered floor of fifteen contiguous training years at any of the
  thirteen stable origins, so every stable column is a mean over eighteen
  populations for the nine non-GP families and sixteen for the GP. The sparse
  VAR is thinned further by its own explosive-draw rejection rule rather than by
  an admissibility floor: 297 of the 520 (population, sex, origin) units survive
  for its native law, and of the 223 excluded, 145 are method-class rejections
  against 50 structural and 28 other (Table~S11). Stable-versus-shift
  comparisons therefore do not rest on an identical population set, and the
  shift regime's twenty populations include two the stable regime cannot score.
  Every contrast reported with a test statistic is computed on the intersection
  of cells in which both compared arms produced a valid row (Addendum~3~\S11),
  and the cluster counts in Table~S9 state that intersection.
\item \emph{The 99.5\% longevity tail was not emitted by the swept passes}
  (recorded 2026-08-30 at analysis; closed as future work 2026-09-04). The
  runner persists the 2.5\% and 97.5\% sample quantiles of the derived
  quantities, not the 99.5\%, so the frequency with which a nominally
  once-in-200-year $\annuity$ exceedance is realised---an internal model's
  one-year longevity tail---cannot be evaluated from the results files, and no
  tail-frequency claim is made anywhere in this paper; it was descriptive and
  not registered. Per-cell seeds are recorded, so a re-score with a persisted
  99.5\% column would reproduce the original predictive samples exactly; that is
  left to future work, and the 97.5\% breach frequencies of
  Section~\ref{sec:actuarial} are what this study reports at the top of the
  distribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the stable and placebo regimes settle, and what they cannot}
\label{sec:discussion-regimes}

Both registered comparative clauses depend on the stable regime: \Htwo's growth
in $|\text{coverage}-\text{nominal}|$ from stable to shift, per family and
mechanism, and \Hfive's excess of the shift-regime departure on $e_{65}$ and
$\annuity$ over the stable-regime departure, per arm. Both are now computed and
both return a split verdict rather than the registered universal one. The
stable half of \Htwo\ was registered as a replication arm
(Section~\ref{sec:design-regimes}); what it delivers is not novelty but the
discriminating power Section~\ref{sec:discussion-standing} rests on. What it
cannot do is convert an event study into a sample of events.

The placebo regime is a second event, used descriptively by registration, and
Table~S10 sets it beside the pandemic. The horizon dependence measured under
\Hthree\ recurs: joint coverage beats independence at each arm's own marginal
rate in 45 of the 46 admissible placebo cells even over nine horizons, though
its level falls much further than at the pandemic, below the nominal
independence value of $0.95^9=0.630$ in 37 of those 46 cells and to 0.335 for
SVD Lee--Carter's native law (Table~S4). The full-age classical cores' native
coverage sits at 0.761--0.787 rather than below it, and the conformal sign
pattern does not recur at all. What the placebo cannot do is unchanged by that:
it supports no transfer regression and no formal test of COVID-specificity; its
eleven populations overlap only partly with the shift regime's twenty; the
sparse VAR's rejection rule leaves that family a single admissible mechanism
there; the pre-specified strata carry the data-quality reading; and its first
test year, 1914, precedes the influenza wave, so the $h=1$ column is a
pre-shock year in a way the shift regime's is not. Recurrence or non-recurrence
at two events leaves the effective-sample-size limitation exactly where it is.

\subsection{What this paper does not do}

It does not propose a model, a repair, or a recommendation about which family to
use. The mechanism that improves the interval score of the families whose native
law is wrong is stated as a signed contrast within the registered sub-grids,
with its cost to the calibrated families and its failure to reproduce at the
placebo beside it (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-conformal}), and is not presented
as a method contribution. The one recommendation the paper makes---that a
calibration audit include a quiet control period---is about measurement, and
follows from the fact that this study would have reported a different and wrong
headline without one. Explainability of the neural families is deferred to a
companion paper that reuses these fits.
