\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\noindent\textit{Every table and figure below is generated from the per-cell
results files; no number is typed by hand.}

The three registered regimes of Table~\ref{tab:regimes} were swept on one grid,
one predictive-sample interface and one evaluation code path, each read only
after its post-sweep QA gate passed. The stable sweep produced 26{,}000 cell
rows of which 6{,}480 are error rows (24.9\%), the shift 2{,}000 of which 210
(10.5\%), the placebo 1{,}100 of which 136 (12.4\%); all are classified below and
excluded from every mean. Most miscalibration here is present with nothing
broken, and the pandemic re-orders the grid rather than degrading it uniformly.

\subsection{Validation gates, and what was scored}
\label{sec:results-infeasible}

The three pre-sweep gates passed before any real-data result was read; gate~1's
one substantive finding---mild over-dispersion of two-stage SVD Lee--Carter
(SVD-LC) on Poisson data---is carried into the SVD-LC rows below. A fourth,
post-sweep gate classifies every cell that produced no valid row; it passed on
all three regimes, of the 6{,}480, 210 and 136 error rows exactly 0, 0 and 0
being machine failures. Counts by class (structural / method / residual) are
126 / 56 / 28 under the break, 3{,}376 / 1{,}106 / 1{,}998 in the control and
0 / 98 / 38 in the placebo; ledger Table~S11, reasons Table~S12.

\emph{Structural} cells fall under a design floor---a mechanism's calibration or
ensemble requirement, or the registered floor of $n_{\text{train}}\ge 15$
training years (Addendum~3, \S4); the stable share is largest because a floor
binding at one origin binds at every earlier one, and the placebo, with
continuous 1908--1922 coverage, has none. \emph{Method} cells are those where a
family's sampler declined a predictive law under a registered rule---the sparse
VAR's explosive-draw rejection (Addendum~3, \S7: rejection, never clipping) or
the Poisson-composition overflow guard: the sparse VAR alone under the break;
1{,}105 of the 1{,}106 stable method rows (1{,}027 rejected draws, 78 overflow,
Table~S12), the last a CNN-LC overflow in ISL; and 96 sparse VAR plus 2 CNN-LC
rows in the placebo. The overflow guard fires 79, 7 and 6 times, always on the
longest exposure panels.

The residual class is family-specific admissibility failure: all 28 shift rows
are the multi-output GP's trailing-block floor; the stable 1{,}998 are origins
preceding a population's first observed year (1{,}416), that GP floor (574) and
LUX under the banded VAR's regression-row minimum (8). The GP floor governs
every GP row here: the family needs forty training years, and it, not the
fifteen-year rule, removes CHL, HKG, HRV and KOR; the sixty-year
training-window cap of Addendum~4 is a separate deviation. The GP is read on
sixteen populations against twenty under the break and sixteen against eighteen
in the control; no contrast crosses that boundary, each being computed on cells
where every compared arm produced a valid row, intersection sizes reported
(Addendum~3, \S11).

\subsection{Calibration where nothing breaks: the stable control}
\label{sec:results-stable}

Over 13 expanding origins from 1990 to 2014, on the shift regime's horizons,
grid and code path, the classical intervals already cover far below nominal
under their own law: SVD-LC 0.702 at nominal 95\%, Poisson Lee--Carter
(Poisson-LC) 0.682, APC/RH 0.731, CBD 0.901 on its 45-age support. Nor is this a
tail-level artefact: at nominal 0.50 and 0.80 SVD-LC covers 0.365 and 0.562,
Poisson-LC 0.324 and 0.522. The point-forecast neural mechanisms are worse
still; arms with a count likelihood or a full posterior instead over-cover (NB
head 0.975 natively and 0.988 under the deep ensemble, sparse VAR 0.988, CNN-LC
dropout 0.954, GP 0.945), and that slack is what the break later consumes.

The joint-path collapse and the old-age gradient are present here too
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-joint} and~\ref{sec:results-age}). The \Hfive\
quantities are worse than any rate-level figure: SVD-LC natively covers 0.253
for $\annuity$ at 2\% and 0.256 for $e_{65}$, the LSTM ensemble 0.157 for
$\annuity$, against a nominal 0.95 (Section~\ref{sec:actuarial}). Propagation is
not universal (sparse VAR native 0.980 and 0.983, CBD bootstrap 0.934 for
$\annuity$), but for the index-extrapolating families an annuity factor carries
no reliable interval even when nothing breaks. The shift regime is therefore
read against this control, not against a nominal level most of these arms never
attained.

\subsection{H1: ranking by RMSE versus ranking by proper scores}

\Hone\ ranks the distributional arms three times per regime---by mean $\RMSE$ on
log rates, mean $\CRPS$ and mean Poisson log score (Table~S2)---on the block's
common cells (22 of 40 units under the break), conformal arms excluded
(placeholder scores, Supplementary Section~S2) and CBD ranked separately.

\Hone\ as registered---orderings disagree, rank correlation below one, at least
one top-three inversion---is supported in that mild form and far more strongly
against the log score. Spearman $\rho(\text{RMSE},\text{CRPS}) = 0.95$, with the
registered inversion present: the RMSE top three (LSTM-$\kappa_t$ dropout and
ensemble, then SVD-LC native) share one member with the CRPS top three (the two
sparse VAR arms, then LSTM dropout). Against the log score the ordering nearly
dissolves, $\rho=0.12$: the LSTM ensemble is 2nd by RMSE and 16th of 18 by log
score, SVD-LC native 3rd and 12th, the NB head mid-pack by RMSE (ranks 10--12)
but holding the top three log-score places with CNN-LC dropout, and neural-LC
dropout last by RMSE and 5th by log score. A cluster bootstrap of 2{,}000
replicates over the thirteen populations surviving the common-cell restriction
gives $[0.77, 0.96]$ and $[-0.16, 0.21]$: the first excludes one, so the
below-one clause does not depend on which populations entered; the second
contains zero, so the log-score ordering is indistinguishable from an unrelated
one. This is a property of the metrics, not of the break---$0.81$ and $-0.18$ in
the control on the 217 of 520 units kept, $0.85$ and $0.16$ in the placebo on 9
of 22---so \Hone\ stands as the registered validity check.

\subsection{H2: marginal coverage across the break}

\inputtable{tab-h2-coverage-main}

Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main} covers the arms carrying their own predictive
law; the full 50-cell version, conformal arms included, is Table~S3.

\Htwo\ has a registered one-sided form and a two-sided restatement, and the
verdicts differ. \textbf{The one-sided form---nominal 95\% intervals under-cover
in the shift regime \emph{for every model family}---is not supported.} The
classical cores under their own law do under-cover severely (Poisson-LC 0.624,
APC/RH 0.654, SVD-LC 0.692, CBD 0.799 on its 45-age support), but the NB head
covers 0.933 natively, 0.956 under the deep ensemble and 0.972 under dropout,
CNN-LC dropout 0.962, the GP 0.905 and the sparse VAR 0.937--0.938 on the cells
its rejection rule admits: several arms sit at or above nominal through the
break, so the miscalibration is not even uniformly downward.

The two-sided quantity registered in Addendum~3 (\S8), $|\text{coverage}-0.95|$,
hypothesised to grow from control to shift, gives the sharper verdict: it grows
in 27 of the 50 admissible cells, a bare majority rather than a general
degradation. Raw coverage falls in 34 of 50, but a fall is degradation only for
a cell that began below nominal, and 16 of the 50 began above. The break sorts
the grid by which side of nominal each arm started on: eleven of the sixteen
over-covering arms move \emph{towards} nominal (sparse VAR native 0.988 to 0.937
and bootstrap 0.996 to 0.938; NB head 0.988 to 0.956 as deep ensemble, 0.988 to
0.972 under dropout, 0.975 to 0.933 natively), while twenty-two of the
thirty-four at or below nominal move further away (Poisson-LC 0.682 to 0.624,
APC/RH 0.731 to 0.654, CBD 0.901 to 0.799, SVD-LC 0.702 to 0.692). COVID did not
break these intervals: Poisson-LC's native band missed 0.318 of observations
before the pandemic existed and 0.376 during it, the pandemic contributing the
last six points of a thirty-three-point shortfall.

The registration declined a directional neural-versus-classical claim; the
conjecture that motivated the audit---degradation larger for the neural
families---is refuted in its strong form in both regimes. LSTM-$\kappa_t$ (0.638
ensemble and 0.667 dropout in the control, 0.647 and 0.685 under the break) and
the neural-LC ensemble (0.758 and 0.694) break like the classical cores, while
sibling neural arms with a count likelihood or dropout smoothing are the
best-covered in every regime: what separates broken from calibrated arms is the
family--mechanism pair, not the network
(Section~\ref{sec:results-contrasts}).

The interval score orders the same failure the same way in both
regimes---Poisson-LC 3.056 in the control and 4.456 under the break, APC/RH
2.398 and 3.401, against NB native 1.455 and 1.684---so the broken natives pay
twice, in missed observations and in width; the largest is neural-LC dropout,
5.559 under the break at coverage 0.811. A conformal band moves any family's
95\% coverage into a narrow range however broken the native law: 0.890--0.948
for split and EnbPI and 0.951--0.985 for the copula arm in the control,
0.848--0.944 and 0.939--0.981 under the break (Table~S3).

\subsection{H3: joint path coverage}
\label{sec:results-joint}

\inputtable{tab-h3-joint-main}

Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint-main} sets mean marginal 95\% coverage beside mean joint
path coverage (full version Table~S4; Figure~S8). Joint coverage lies below
marginal in 100 of 100 (regime, cell) pairs---all fifty stable and all fifty
shift arms---mean gap 0.127 in the control, 0.168 under the break. That count is
descriptive and largely arithmetic: at nominal 0.95 over five horizons
independence alone gives $0.95^5 = 0.774$, a gap of 0.176 \emph{larger} than
either observed mean.

The informative benchmark is independence \emph{at each arm's own marginal
rate}, $c^{H}$. Measured so, almost every arm beats independence---50 of 50
stable and 50 of 50 shift cells, by $+0.007$ to $+0.344$ in the control and
$+0.014$ to $+0.328$ under the break, and 45 of the 46 placebo cells over nine
horizons---the exception being the sparse VAR's Poisson-bootstrap placebo arm,
0.748 joint against a benchmark of 0.756 (from unrounded marginal coverage; the
printed 0.969 would give 0.753). Mortality forecast errors are thus strongly
positively dependent down the path: the dependence \citet{dowd2010backtesting}
named in a footnote and did not measure, and measuring it across ten families,
seven mechanisms and three regimes is \Hthree's contribution.

What is supportable is the level of joint coverage, not the gap. Joint path
coverage falls below 0.774 in 21 of the 50 stable cells and 30 of the 50 shift
cells. For the broken classical natives the collapse is near-total in both
regimes (Poisson-LC 0.682 marginal to 0.422 joint in the control, 0.624 to 0.298
under the break), but good marginal calibration does not remove the gap: the
sparse VAR falls natively from 0.988 to 0.951 and from 0.937 to 0.819, the NB
head's native law under the break from 0.933 marginal to 0.795 joint. The
mechanism built to be joint behaves as built: the copula-conformal arm's gaps
run $-0.043$ to $-0.064$ in the control and $-0.049$ to $-0.136$ under the
break, the smallest in its family block everywhere but the NB head.

The registered comparator is not available. Addendum~3 (\S8) re-specified it as
each cell's own simulated-path-implied joint coverage, so that the
realised-minus-implied gap could take either sign; the runner's column contract
carries realised joint coverage but not the implied quantity, which is therefore
\emph{not computable} from the columns the runner wrote. The registered paired
test is not run and \Hthree\ is not adjudicated in its registered form. In its
place stand the marginal-to-joint gap, descriptive and near-tautological in
sign, and the independence comparison, which is neither. The implied comparator
is recomputable from the recorded per-row seeds and would split the residual gap
into horizon dependence and miscalibration: unfinished business, not an omission
discovered after the fact.

\subsection{H4: where in age the intervals fail}
\label{sec:results-age}

\inputtable{tab-h4-age-main}

\Hfour\ as registered---shift-regime coverage at ages 65--99 lower than at
25--64 \emph{for every family}---is not supported as a universal; the age
profile is family-specific (Table~\ref{tab:h4-age-main}; all 50 cells in all
three regimes in Table~S5, Figures~S4--S6). It is supported, sharply, for the
full-age families whose extrapolated period index \emph{also} broke in
Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main}: across ages 0--24, 25--64 and 65--99 under the
break SVD-LC falls 0.882 / 0.725 / 0.518, APC/RH 0.732 / 0.661 / 0.589 and the
LSTM ensemble most steeply 0.893 / 0.687 / 0.426.

The control settles what the break contributed: the gradient is already there
with nothing broken (SVD-LC 0.888 / 0.760 / 0.501, LSTM ensemble
0.858 / 0.700 / 0.409), a standing property of index extrapolation rather than a
pandemic effect, and what 2020--2024 adds is small and in the wrong direction,
old-age coverage moving 0.501 to 0.518 and 0.409 to 0.426, both \emph{towards}
nominal. It is also the one hypothesis whose registered direction the prior
literature disputed: \citet{dowd2010backtesting}, on six classical models and
one population, found the higher age behaving better; we find the reverse for
the full-age index-extrapolating cores, in quiet years as much as across the
break.

The reversal is nonetheless common: under the break, coverage at 65--99 exceeds
coverage at 25--64 in 34 of the 50 cells---26 of the 30 conformal arms, whose
Mondrian strata invert the profile by construction, and 8 of the 20 arms
carrying a family's own uncertainty (both CBD arms, both CNN-LC arms, the GP,
neural-LC dropout and both Poisson-LC arms; Table~S5). Five of those eight
reverse by five hundredths or less, which is no age profile in either direction:
Poisson-LC is uniformly poor across all three bands under the break
(0.639 / 0.602 / 0.639) and in the control (0.721 / 0.664 / 0.676), and the GP
is flat and near-nominal (0.908 / 0.900 / 0.909).

CBD is excluded for a different reason, a caveat governing every CBD column
here: fitted on ages 55--99, its ``25--64'' column is a 55--64 stub, not
comparable with the full-age families' bands. On the stated criterion it would
belong there (it extrapolates two period indices and its native interval broke,
0.901 to 0.799), but its reversal is already present in the control (0.913
against 0.861 natively, 0.930 against 0.888 under the bootstrap). The neural-LC
ensemble is the exception in the other direction, 0.619 at ages 0--24 against
0.793 and 0.633 above, its failure concentrated at the young ages.

Table~S7 gives the mean KS distance of the randomised PIT from the uniform and
the share of cells rejected at a nominal $0.05$---a descriptive $p$-value only
(Supplementary Section~S2); departure shapes are in Figures~S10--S12. Under the
break the ordering matches Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main} (smallest distances
GP 0.172, NB head 0.182--0.189, sparse VAR 0.188; largest Poisson-LC 0.374 and
APC/RH 0.325), but the levels carry the sharper message: no arm is
distributionally calibrated, with or without a break. The calibrated end is the
same in the control while the poorly calibrated end re-orders, the LSTM arms
holding the two largest distances there (0.335 and 0.322), so a PIT ranking read
off one regime does not transfer. The rejected share never drops beneath 0.905
in the control or 0.844 under the break: the NB head's 95\% interval can be
essentially nominal while its predictive law, read over all levels at once, is
rejected as uniform in nearly every cell.

Table~S8 decomposes the Brier score of the 95\% hit indicator. With a constant
forecast probability the partition degenerates (Supplementary Section~S2) and
restates the coverage table as a proper-score share, the same
two-order-of-magnitude spread in both regimes (under the break 0.1515 for
Poisson-LC native against 0.0015 for CNN-LC dropout). The PIT-scale reliability
orders the arms the same way (0.1904 down to 0.0302 under the break; 0.1427 down
to 0.0232 in the control) but never approaches zero: the calibrated arms are
calibrated at 95\%, not everywhere. The isotonic (CORP) estimator is not emitted
and no hypothesis rests on it.

\subsection{Forecast comparison: Diebold--Mariano and the model confidence set}

Table~S9 carries the two registered inference procedures in all three regimes,
on the losses fixed in Supplementary Section~S2. The classical model confidence
set covers the four full-age families only; the five-family set including CBD is
skipped in every regime, a mean CRPS over 45 ages being incomparable with one
over 100. In the control, on 297 common cells, the 90\% set retains the sparse
VAR's native arm alone, eliminating SVD-LC, Poisson-LC and APC/RH at $p=0.03$
each; under the break, on 29 common cells, it retains \{SVD-LC native, sparse
VAR native\} (elimination $p$-values 0.13 and 1.00) and eliminates Poisson-LC
and APC/RH at $p<0.0005$---the sparse VAR scored only on the cells its rejection
rule admits. The set widens under the break because thirteen origins of evidence
are replaced by one, not because the eliminated families improved.

The within-family conformal sets separate the wrappers far more sharply under
the break. In the control seven of the ten families retain all three wrappers,
CBD and the sparse VAR reducing to EnbPI alone and the NB head to
\{copula, EnbPI\}; under the break EnbPI is the sole survivor in five families
(SVD-LC, LSTM-$\kappa_t$, NB head, neural-LC, sparse VAR), eliminating the split
and copula arms at $p$-values between 0.00 and 0.08, the copula arm survives
alone in Poisson-LC ($p=0.01$), and four families retain all three.

The Diebold--Mariano block prices the conformal repair, the attribution finding
of the crossed design. Defined for the seven families with a native arm, its
sign is the same in both regimes without exception. Under the break split
conformal is significantly \emph{better} than the native law for exactly the
four classical cores whose native intervals broke---Poisson-LC ($\Delta=1.895$,
$p=0.004$), APC/RH ($\Delta=1.039$, $p=0.013$), SVD-LC ($\Delta=0.700$,
$p=0.018$), CBD ($\Delta=0.318$, $p=0.017$), $G=19$ clusters each---and
significantly \emph{worse} for exactly the three whose native law was already
calibrated: NB head ($\Delta=-1.024$, $p<0.0002$, $G=19$), GP ($\Delta=-0.413$,
$p=0.009$, $G=16$), sparse VAR ($\Delta=-0.318$, $p<0.0002$, $G=14$). The
control gives the same seven signs: $+1.038$ for Poisson-LC ($p=0.032$),
$+0.694$ for SVD-LC ($p=0.006$), $+0.451$ for APC/RH ($p=0.086$) and $+0.108$
for CBD ($p=0.198$), against $-0.873$ for the NB head, $-0.772$ for the GP and
$-0.236$ for the sparse VAR, all three at $p\le0.0002$.

\subsection{Twin crises: 1914--1922 beside 2020--2024}

Table~S10 sets the placebo beside the shift regime. Read within regime (joint
columns span $H=9$ against $H=5$), it reproduces the shift regime's family split
a century earlier: the classical cores under-cover (SVD-LC 0.787, Poisson-LC
0.780, APC/RH 0.761 marginal against a nominal 0.95), the point-forecast neural
mechanisms are worst (neural-LC ensemble 0.661, LSTM 0.636), the families that
came through COVID calibrated sit near the top (NB head 0.849, GP 0.820), and
joint path coverage collapses in the same order (0.146, 0.172, and 0.293--0.335
for the classical cores). \Hthree's gap is therefore no COVID artefact either,
though over nine horizons its descriptive part is more nearly arithmetic still
($0.95^9 = 0.630$): 45 of the 46 placebo arms beat independence at their own
marginal rate and 37 of the 46 nonetheless hold the nine-year path less often
than 0.630. The sparse VAR has no placebo verdict under its native law or any
conformal wrapper: pre-1914 draws tripped the registered explosive-path
rejection on all but one cell per arm, and that cell fails admissibility,
leaving zero valid rows in four of its five arms and emptying the common-cell
intersection of the registered classical confidence set here; only its
Poisson-bootstrap arm survives, on nine rows, at 0.969 marginal against 0.748
joint.

Against the control the placebo shows \Htwo's sorting from the other side,
though the regimes share neither populations, era nor horizon count: every
classical core covers better through 1914--1922 than in the quiet 1990--2014
decades, the volatile pre-1914 panels producing much wider bands---accidental
calibration, not a better law---while the arms calibrated in the control lose
the most (NB head 0.975 to 0.849, GP 0.945 to 0.820). The exception is CBD,
near-nominal through the placebo break (0.986 marginal, 0.918 joint) and broken
through the COVID one (0.799, 0.504): the 1918 influenza concentrated its excess
at young-adult ages, below CBD's age-55 support, while COVID's fell at the old
ages CBD models---\Hfour's age-localisation operating \emph{between} regimes.
The Addendum~1 strata sharpen this: the neutral register-based populations hold
the classical cores at 0.86--0.89, while the belligerent-total stratum, where
war losses compound the flu, drops the same families to 0.55--0.59, the
civilian-only stratum in between.

The paired mechanism contrasts do not transfer. Native versus split is
insignificant on the interval score for every full-age classical core (SVD-LC
$\Delta=-0.091$, $p=0.689$; Poisson-LC $p=0.127$; APC/RH $p=0.563$), and the NB
head's native law significantly beats its own wrapper ($\Delta=-0.461$,
$p=0.008$): a pre-1914 calibration window knows as little about the coming shock
as the model it corrects. Ensemble-versus-dropout verdicts flip family by family
between the crises---CNN-LC, indistinguishable under COVID ($p=0.739$), favours
dropout here ($\Delta=+0.531$, $p=0.0002$); LSTM, which favours dropout under
COVID ($p=0.0006$), is indistinguishable here ($p=0.478$). A family's fragility
is predictable from its past crisis; which uncertainty mechanism patches it best
is not.

\subsection{Architecture or mechanism? Contrasts within sub-grids}
\label{sec:results-contrasts}

Each contrast is pre-registered and computed as a paired difference within the
sub-grid named, in the control as well as under the break. Contrasts~2 and~3
have no exhibit in either document---Table~S9 has no ensemble-versus-dropout or
bootstrap-versus-native row---so their statistics are given here in full.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Conformal versus native.} The Diebold--Mariano block above is this
  contrast, identical in sign in both regimes; the break changes only the
  significance of two of the seven (CBD and APC/RH) and the size of the Poisson
  repair, not any family's side. The width side (interval-score column of
  Table~\ref{tab:h2-coverage-main}) is likewise regime-independent: for the
  broken families the wrapper buys coverage while \emph{shrinking} the score
  (Poisson-LC's 3.056 and 4.456 native become 2.056 and 2.674 split), for the
  calibrated ones it pays in width (NB head's 1.455 and 1.684 become 2.335 and
  2.717). Conformalisation is not a pandemic remedy.
\item \emph{Ensemble versus Monte Carlo dropout, neural families only.} Two
  mechanisms on one architecture do not give the same coverage (CNN-LC dropout
  0.962 against the ensemble's 0.882 under the break, 0.954 against 0.863 in the
  control). On the interval score the two differ significantly, \emph{in
  opposite directions}, on two of the four neural families under the
  break---dropout favoured on LSTM-on-$\kappa_t$ ($\Delta = +0.224$,
  $t = 3.78$, $p = 0.0006$), the ensemble on neural-LC ($\Delta = -1.238$,
  $t = -4.18$, $p < 0.0002$)---while CNN-LC ($p = 0.739$) and the NB head
  ($p = 0.139$) are indistinguishable. All four signs are the same in the
  control, on 434--442 common cells rather than 38--40 (LSTM $+0.156$,
  $p=0.005$; neural-LC $-1.142$, $p=0.0002$; NB head $+0.049$, $p=0.019$;
  CNN-LC $+0.001$, $p=0.991$): the opposing directions are a property of the
  family, not of the break.
\item \emph{Bootstrap versus native, classical families only.} Descriptively the
  movement toward nominal is positive and small in both regimes (SVD-LC 0.702 to
  0.726 in the control and 0.692 to 0.727 under the break; CBD 0.901 to 0.921
  and 0.799 to 0.830), nowhere near the conformal repair. Under the break the
  semiparametric bootstrap improves the interval score significantly on three of
  the five classical families---SVD-LC ($\Delta = -0.112$, $p < 0.0002$),
  Poisson-LC ($\Delta = -0.115$, $p < 0.0002$), Renshaw--Haberman
  ($\Delta = -0.101$, $p = 0.0002$)---leaves CBD unchanged ($p = 0.681$) and
  significantly \emph{worsens} the sparse VAR ($\Delta = +0.168$, $p = 0.0008$,
  on the 22 cells where its native arm survives the explosive-draw rejection of
  Addendum~3~\S7). The control gives the same three improvements at the same
  order of magnitude ($-0.100$, $-0.073$, $-0.075$, each at $p \le 0.0002$, on
  442 cells) and the same sparse VAR penalty ($+0.237$, $p < 0.0002$, on 223
  cells), CBD favouring its native arm there ($+0.096$, $p = 0.016$). These
  improvements are an order of magnitude smaller than the conformal repair of
  the same families (Poisson-LC, $-0.115$ against $+1.895$): resampling the
  estimator moves a broken interval but does not fix it.
\item \emph{Joint-path conformal versus split conformal, all families.} Reading
  the conformal confidence sets above with the joint column of
  Table~\ref{tab:h3-joint-main}: the copula arm holds joint path coverage of
  0.894--0.942 in the control and 0.805--0.927 under the break where the split
  arm holds 0.760--0.842 and 0.692--0.862, yet on the single-level interval
  score it survives as sole set member only for Poisson-LC under the break and
  is eliminated in five families where EnbPI's narrower band wins. A
  practitioner who needs the path pays for it at the level; the study reports
  the price rather than adjudicating it.
\end{enumerate}
