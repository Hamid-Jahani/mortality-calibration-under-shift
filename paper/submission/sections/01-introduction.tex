\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Longevity risk is priced off the interval}

A life annuity is a bet on a distribution. An annuity book's best-estimate
reserve is a functional of the central mortality projection, but its risk
margin, its longevity capital and the price at which a longevity swap or
bulk annuity transfer clears are functionals of the projection's
\emph{spread}: under the Solvency~II standard formula a permanent 20\% fall
in rates \citep{eiopa2015delegated}, under an internal model the 99.5\%
one-year quantile of the change in own funds, taken from a stochastic
model's own predictive distribution. What the balance sheet carries is thus
a tail of a forecast distribution, and its adequacy rests on one property: a
nominal 95\% interval must contain the realised outcome about 95\% of the
time. Too narrow under-reserves, too wide over-prices the hedge, and neither
error is visible in a point-accuracy comparison. Classical mortality models
and their neural extensions both emit intervals
\citep{lee1992modeling,marino2023neural,miyata2022extending,schnurch2022point};
whether those intervals are \emph{calibrated} is a separate question from
whether they exist, and it decides what they are worth to an actuary.

\subsection{The break that makes the question answerable}

From the mid-1950s until 2019 the mortality surfaces of the high-income
populations in the Human Mortality Database (HMD) improved smoothly enough
that almost any trend extrapolation looked calibrated in sample. The years
2020--2024 broke that surface: life expectancy at birth fell from 2019 to
2020 in 27 of the 29 countries analysed by \citet{aburto2022quantifying},
and the recovery was uneven by country and by age. Any interval built from
data ending in 2019 was, for the first time in the modern record, put to a
genuine out-of-sample test by reality, and that test has not been scored:
the closest study, \citet{barigou2023bayesian}, evaluates by
leave-future-out validation, log score and CRPS, but its pandemic analysis
is a simulation, because in 2021 real post-shock data did not exist. It
exists now. The HMD vintage of 15~June~2026 (Methods Protocol v6) is the
first with final single-age data through 2024 for the twenty populations
audited here.

\subsection{What the literature measures}

We searched the full text of the mortality-forecasting library assembled for
this study (about twenty modelling papers; term counts and quotations are in
the project repository); Section~\ref{sec:related} gives the detail. Point
accuracy is the default and often the only metric:
\citet{richman2021neural}, the multi-population neural Lee--Carter that
later work builds on, reports mean squared error and no interval, coverage
or proper score, as do
\citep{scognamiglio2022calibrating,nigri2019deep,petnehazi2019mortality,perla2021time};
elsewhere intervals are drawn but never checked against realisations
\citep{miyata2022extending}. Where coverage is checked it is reported without a sharpness penalty,
under pass rules in which over-coverage is fulfilment
\citep{schnurch2022point} or a coverage probability of $1$ is the best
outcome \citep{marino2023neural}, so that wider and better are
indistinguishable; the latter also reports, without follow-up, that
classical ARIMA intervals cover ``around 50\%'' of realisations and, for
Spanish males, ``stable around 33\%'', over a window containing no
break---nominal 95\% Lee--Carter intervals covering a third to a half of
outcomes; and \citet{miao2025gradient} declare the closest of marginal
coverages 90\%, 94\% and 96\% ``the most properly calibrated'', again with
no proper score and no width penalty.
\citet{dowd2010backtesting}, the direct ancestor of this audit,
do backtest densities ex post, but on one population, on models with
native intervals, and without a break. Absent, so far as we can find:
PIT or rank histograms, measured joint (path) coverage, formal
forecast-comparison testing but for \citet{shang2018model}, and, in the
2025--2026 conformal mortality line
\citep{shang2025intervals,shang2026conformal,shang2026subnational}, any
crossing of a designated break. The principle violated is old and precise:
maximise the sharpness of the predictive distributions subject to
calibration \citep{gneiting2007probabilistic}; coverage without width is not
calibration, width without coverage is not sharpness, and neither is a
proper score \citep{gneiting2007strictly}. We score every forecast by both.

\subsection{This study}

The question is whether the prediction intervals of stochastic,
machine-learning and neural mortality models attain nominal coverage when
the evaluation window crosses the COVID-19 break, whether the answer depends
on the model family, on the uncertainty mechanism or on both, and whether it
differs from what the same intervals do when nothing breaks. We treat
\emph{model family} and \emph{uncertainty mechanism} as separate crossed
factors---ten families, seven mechanisms, fifty admissible cells---in three
regimes: a \emph{stable} control (expanding origins 1990--2014, every test
year before 2020), the \emph{shift} regime (train to 2019, test 2020--2024),
and a \emph{placebo} break (train to 1913, test 1914--1922) asking whether
what we find is specific to COVID-19. The population count differs by regime
and by family---twenty across the break and sixteen for the Gaussian
process, eighteen and sixteen in the stable control, eleven in the
placebo---so it is stated wherever a mean is taken.

Five hypotheses were fixed and hash-stamped before any model touched real
data. They are reproduced verbatim from the pre-registration.

\begin{hypothesis}[H1]
Model rankings by RMSE and by CRPS disagree (rank correlation $<1$; at least
one top-3 inversion) in the shift regime.
\end{hypothesis}
\begin{hypothesis}[H2]
Nominal 95\% intervals under-cover in the shift regime for every model
family; the shortfall exceeds the stable-regime shortfall.
\end{hypothesis}
\begin{hypothesis}[H3]
Joint path coverage over $h = 1,\dots,5$ is materially below marginal
coverage at the same nominal level for every family (reported gap; test:
paired difference $>0$).
\end{hypothesis}
\begin{hypothesis}[H4]
Coverage failure is non-uniform in age: shift-regime coverage at ages 65--99
is lower than at ages 25--64 for every family.
\end{hypothesis}
\begin{hypothesis}[H5]
Interval miscalibration propagates to derived quantities: empirical coverage
of 95\% intervals for $e_{65}$ and annuity factor $\annuity$ (2\%) departs
from nominal by more than the corresponding stable-regime departure.
\end{hypothesis}

No directional claim is registered about neural-versus-classical ordering;
the crossed design estimates it. The coverage split runs through the middle
of the neural group rather than between neural and classical, and is open
already in the stable control, before any break (Contribution~2;
Section~\ref{sec:results}; Table~S3).

\subsection{Contributions}

This paper proposes no new mortality model. Its contributions, with all
three regimes complete, are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The audit, and the finding the control forces.} The first
  evaluation of probabilistic mortality forecasts---classical, neural and
  conformal---against realised 2020--2024 mortality across twenty HMD
  populations, sixteen of them for the multi-output Gaussian process, whose
  forty-year training floor removes four and whose window registered
  Addendum~4 caps at sixty years (Table~S11), every interval built strictly
  from pre-2020 data, beside a stable control of thirteen expanding origins
  over 1990--2014 in which no test year is later than 2019. Most of the
  miscalibration predates the pandemic: the native 95\% intervals of the
  classical cores already cover 0.702 (Lee--Carter), 0.682 (Poisson
  Lee--Carter) and 0.731 (Renshaw--Haberman) of realised outcomes in the
  control, against 0.692, 0.624 and 0.654 across the break (Table~S3). The
  break widens a failure that was there beforehand rather than creating one,
  and the shift regime alone would have misattributed all of it to COVID-19.
  CBD is where the break contributes most, and only the control shows it:
  0.901 in the quiet decades against 0.799 across 2020--2024, so about
  two-thirds of its shift-regime departure from nominal is break-induced and
  about a third is not. \Hone\ holds, with the
  registered top-three inversion present, the RMSE and CRPS orderings
  correlating at $\rho = 0.95$ and RMSE with the Poisson log score at
  $\rho = 0.12$ (Table~S2).
\item \emph{The crossed design.} Family and mechanism as separate factors,
  so that a coverage failure can be attributed to the architecture, the
  mechanism or their interaction; claims are contrasts within admissible
  sub-grids, never main effects over a ragged grid. The failure is not a
  neural-versus-classical split: across the break the negative-binomial
  distributional head (0.933) and the CNN under dropout (0.962) hold
  marginal coverage near nominal and the Gaussian process stays within five
  points at 0.905, while the LSTM (0.647 ensembled, no better in the control
  at 0.638) and the ensembled neural Lee--Carter (0.694) sit with the
  classical cores far below nominal in both regimes (Table~S3). Nor is the
  mechanism contrast a pandemic effect: on the 95\% interval score $\IS$ the
  split conformal wrapper beats the native interval for the same four
  classical families in the control as across the break and with the same
  sign, at different effect sizes (Poisson Lee--Carter $+1.038$ to
  $+1.895$), significantly for all four across the break ($p$ from 0.004 to
  0.018) but in the control only for Lee--Carter and Poisson Lee--Carter
  ($p = 0.006$ and $p = 0.032$, against $p = 0.086$ for Renshaw--Haberman
  and $p = 0.198$ for CBD). The native interval beats its wrapper,
  significantly in both regimes, for the three families whose native law was
  already right: the negative-binomial head ($-0.873$ control, $-1.024$
  shift), the Gaussian process ($-0.772$, $-0.413$) and the sparse VAR
  ($-0.236$, $-0.318$), all at $p \le 0.0002$ in the control
  (Diebold--Mariano with a wild cluster bootstrap over populations;
  Table~S9). Conformal wrapping repairs families whose native law was wrong
  and damages families whose native law was right, in quiet periods as much
  as in the pandemic. The 1914--1922 placebo, on eleven populations, does
  not reproduce that ordering intact: it reverses the contrast for the four
  classical families, though none of the four reversals is significant ($p$
  from 0.071 to 0.689), while the native advantage of the negative-binomial
  head ($-0.461$, $p = 0.008$) and of the Gaussian process ($-0.172$,
  indistinguishable from zero) persists.
\item \emph{Registered directions that fail, reported as such.} Two
  registered directions are contradicted outright; a third, \Hfive's
  comparative clause, returns a split verdict. \Htwo\ registered one-sided
  under-coverage across the break for \emph{every} family, worsening on the
  control. The registered two-sided departure $\lvert c - 0.95 \rvert$ grows
  from stable to shift in 27 of the 50 primary cells and raw coverage falls
  in 34 of 50, but a fall is degradation only for an arm that began below
  nominal, one marginal exception aside, and most arms that began above
  nominal move \emph{towards} it (sparse VAR native 0.988 to 0.937). What
  the pandemic mostly does is sort families by the side of nominal they
  started on---a tendency and not a law, describing 33 of the 50 cells. Five
  over-covering arms widen their departure instead: four copula-conformal
  bands, of which the Poisson Lee--Carter's crosses nominal on the way down
  (0.960 to 0.939, its registered departure growing from 0.010 to 0.011, the
  marginal exception above), and the CNN under dropout, above nominal in
  both regimes (0.954 to 0.962). Twelve under-covering arms move back
  towards nominal, eight of them conformal wrappers recalibrated on pre-2020
  residuals (EnbPI on Lee--Carter, 0.890 to 0.915), the rest led by LSTM
  arms too far below nominal to fall further. \Hfour\ fails in its universal
  form and settles a disagreement with the prior: where
  \citet{dowd2010backtesting} found age~84 better behaved than age~65, the
  native Lee--Carter interval here covers 0.888, 0.760 and 0.501 across ages
  0--24, 25--64 and 65--99, in the stable control and before any break
  (Table~S5). Old-age concentration is real for the Lee--Carter line and the
  LSTM arms but not universal: the CBD arms cover worse below age 65 than
  above it (native, shift: 0.626 against 0.849). CBD is fitted on ages
  55--99, so that 0.626 is a 55--64 stub rather than the registered 25--64
  band and is not comparable with the full-age families.
\item \emph{Resolution of the failure by horizon and in money.} Joint path
  coverage over $h = 1,\dots,5$ lies below marginal coverage in all 100 of
  the 100 (regime, cell) pairs scored, with mean gaps of 0.127 stable and
  0.168 across the break (Table~S4). That count is largely arithmetic, since
  independent horizons alone would put joint coverage at $0.95^{5} = 0.774$,
  a gap of 0.176 wider than either mean; the registered marginal-versus-joint
  comparison therefore cannot on its own evidence miscalibration, and we do
  not read it as the study's strongest result. Against the informative
  benchmark---each arm's joint coverage under independence \emph{at its own
  marginal rate}---the grid measures what the literature has only asserted:
  joint coverage exceeds that value in 50 of 50 stable cells, 50 of 50 shift
  cells and 45 of 46 placebo cells, by as much as 0.34. Horizons are
  strongly positively dependent---a dependence \citet{dowd2010backtesting}
  name but never measure---and quantifying it is \Hthree's contribution
  here. The registered re-specified comparator, each cell's
  model-implied joint coverage (Addendum~3, \S8), is not computable from the
  columns the production runner emitted, so \Hthree\ carries no registered
  verdict in this paper and we say so where the verdict would go; what
  survives is that joint coverage falls below the nominal independence value
  0.774 in 21 of the 50 stable cells and 30 of 50 across the break. The
  copula conformal band, the only mechanism calibrated for the path, narrows
  the marginal-to-joint gap furthest on average without closing it
  (0.805--0.927 joint coverage across the break), and is not narrowest
  everywhere: on the negative-binomial head the dropout arm's gap across the
  break, 0.075, beats the copula band's 0.085. \Hfive\ is the result an
  actuary should read first. Derived quantities are far worse calibrated
  than the rates beneath them in every arm whose derived coverage is
  computable but three---the two CBD arms, fit and integrated on ages 55--99
  alone, and the sparse VAR's native law, level in the control until the
  break separates the two scales (Section~\ref{sec:actuarial})---and this
  too is a quiet-period property: in the stable control the native
  Lee--Carter 95\% interval covers 0.253 of realised annuity factors
  $\annuity$ and 0.256 of realised $e_{65}$, and the LSTM ensemble 0.157 of
  annuity factors, so a nominal 95\% interval on an annuity price, from
  models in routine use, contains the realised value about a quarter of the
  time in a period with no pandemic in it. The registered clause was
  comparative---a larger departure from nominal across the break than in the
  control---and in that strict form it holds for 14 of the 20 distributional
  arms on $e_{65}$ and 12 of 20 on $\annuity$, the exceptions being arms
  already so far below nominal in the control that the break could only move
  them towards it (Table~S6).
\item \emph{The protocol.} A pre-registered, synthetic-truth-validated,
  oracle-parity-checked harness in which every model emits predictive
  samples through one interface and is scored through one code path, with
  cluster-bootstrap inference respecting the fact that a pandemic is one
  shock, not twenty. It is reusable for any future break, and the stable
  control is the part other studies most often omit.
\end{enumerate}
