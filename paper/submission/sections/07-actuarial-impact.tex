\subsection{H5: propagation into life expectancy and annuity factors}

\inputtable{tab-h5-actuarial-main}

Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial-main} gives, by arm and regime, empirical
coverage of the nominal 95\% interval and the mean error for $e_0$,
$e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ at 2\%, on the period table of the first test
year, computed from each arm's own latent rate paths and compared with the
same functional of the observed rates; Table~S6 carries the full
fifty-cell fragment and its notes. Three conventions bind the reading.
Conformal cells have no such paths---their samples are uniform draws
inside a band, a convention for interval metrics only---so their rows are
not applicable and every count here is over the twenty distributional
arms, not the fifty admissible cells. CBD is fit on ages 55--99: it
contributes no $e_0$, and its $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ are exact on its
truncated table but rest on a fit that never saw the younger ages, so its
rows are not on the same footing as the full-age families'. And no row
carries its regime's full complement of (population, sex, origin)
units---520 stable, 40 shift, 22 placebo---because error rows (Table~S11)
are excluded from every mean: the fullest stable rows reach 442 of 520,
the sparse VAR's native law is scored on 297 stable units and its
bootstrap on 238, the multi-output GP on sixteen of the twenty stable
populations, and the sparse VAR's native placebo cell produced nothing at
all, 0 of 22. Addendum~3 (\S8) restricts \Hfive\ to the ex-post coverage
of these derived intervals, against attenuation---that life-table
integration averages a rate-surface failure away
\citep{cairns2011mortality}---as the named competitor.

\Hfive\ was registered as: \emph{interval miscalibration propagates to
derived quantities---empirical coverage of 95\% intervals for $e_{65}$ and
the annuity factor $\annuity$ at 2\% departs from nominal by more than the
corresponding stable-regime departure}. Read strictly, as the universal
ordering the registration states, it is not supported: the shift departure
exceeds the stable departure for 14 of the 20 distributional arms on
$e_{65}$ and 12 of 20 on $\annuity$---a majority, not the universal the
clause asserts, and the same standard by which Section~\ref{sec:results}
declines to call \Htwo's registered form supported at 27 of 50. What
survives is the substantive claim the ordering stood proxy for, and it is
this audit's most consequential actuarial finding: miscalibration of the
rate surface reaches the reserve, it does so in every regime, and it is
not a pandemic effect. The derived intervals are already broken in the
stable control, the regime constructed so that nothing breaks in it.
Covering realised $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ against nominal 0.95, SVD
Lee--Carter's native law reaches 0.256 and 0.253 on its 442 scored stable
units, the LSTM ensemble 0.182 and 0.157, LSTM dropout 0.205 and 0.191,
Poisson-LC 0.434 and 0.430, the neural-LC ensemble 0.468 and 0.459, CNN-LC
dropout 0.559 and 0.548, APC/RH 0.654 and 0.643. Only two arms come within
0.05 of nominal on $\annuity$ there---the sparse VAR's native law at 0.980
on 297 units and CBD's bootstrap at 0.934 on its 55--99 support---and
thirteen of the twenty sit below 0.700. An interval containing the
realised value in a quarter of population--sex origins is not a usable
reserving input, and it says as little in the quiet decades 1990--2014 as
it does across 2020.

The registered comparison orders two-sided departures
$|\text{coverage}-0.95|$; there the result is partial. The six exceptions
on $e_{65}$ are both SVD Lee--Carter arms, both LSTM arms, CNN-LC under
dropout and Poisson-LC under the Poisson bootstrap; on $\annuity$ the same
six plus CNN-LC's ensemble and Poisson-LC's native law. They are not
intervals the break spared but mostly the arms whose stable departure was
already largest, moved \emph{towards} nominal---SVD Lee--Carter's
bootstrap from a departure of 0.645 to 0.400 on $\annuity$. The rule does
not separate cleanly, and we do not claim that it does: Poisson-LC's
native law and the neural-LC ensemble start further from nominal on
$e_{65}$ (0.516 and 0.482) than CNN-LC under dropout (0.391) and still
move away. The rate-scale sorting of \Htwo\ (Section~\ref{sec:results})
cannot operate here, because nineteen of the twenty arms already
under-cover $\annuity$ in the control and the one that does not, the
sparse VAR under its native law, is the arm the break damages most, from a
departure of 0.030 to one of 0.364. What operates instead is a floor: the
eight arms that moved towards nominal span 0.157 to 0.558 in the control,
and an interval already missing four cells in five has little room left to
miss more.

The attenuation alternative is answered the same way in both regimes. Set
beside the marginal rate coverage of Table~S3, all eighteen full-age
distributional arms cover realised $\annuity$ less often than a single log
rate, in the control and the shift regime alike; CBD's two
truncated-support arms are excluded from the count and run the other way.
The two sides are not scored on identical horizons---marginal coverage
pools $h=1\ldots5$, the functional uses the first test year alone---so an
$h$-aggregated rate figure meets an $h=1$ functional; the 18-of-18
direction does not turn on that, the annuity being scored at the horizon
most favourable to it, but the sizes should be read with the mismatch in
view. The gap runs from 0.008 (the sparse VAR, native law) to 0.481 (the
LSTM ensemble) and averages 0.252 in the control and 0.289 under COVID.
Integration does not average the failure away; it concentrates it, in
quiet periods too. The instructive cells look calibrated on rates and are
not on liabilities: CNN-LC under dropout covers 0.954 of single log rates
in the control against 0.548 of realised annuity factors, the NB head's
ensemble 0.988 against 0.814.

The mechanism is \Hthree's, moved from the horizon axis to the age axis: a
period functional integrates a whole scored age range, so a common-factor
miss leaving many single-age intervals near but inside their bounds still
displaces it, and one displaced functional is one uncovered cell. Priced
from the same marginal baseline on the same eighteen arms, the age axis is
the dearer: mean marginal-to-joint-path gaps of 0.153 in the control and
0.208 in the shift regime (Tables~S3 and S4), against marginal-to-annuity
gaps of 0.252 and 0.289 on those same arms. The 0.127 and 0.168 quoted for \Hthree\ in
Section~\ref{sec:results} average over all fifty cells, thirty of them
conformal and carrying no derived quantity, and are not the like-for-like
comparator. The horizon gap is also the weaker quantity, most of it
arithmetic: independence at nominal coverage over five horizons gives
$1-0.95^5=0.176$, above either grid mean, and all fifty stable and fifty
shift cells clear the independence benchmark set at their \emph{own}
marginal rate, by as much as 0.34. The comparator is that benchmark, not
each model's own implied path law: Addendum~3's re-specified comparator
(\S8), a cell's model-implied joint coverage, is not computable from the
columns the runner emitted, and we record it as a limitation rather than
work around it. The annuity gap admits no arithmetic discount in any case:
$\annuity$ is one event on one test year.

Two families qualify the mechanism rather than contradict it. On the
registered functionals only CBD has derived coverage above its own
rate-scale coverage: in the control its native law covers 0.930 of
realised $e_{65}$ against a marginal 0.901, and its bootstrap 0.950 and
0.934 against a marginal 0.921, the native arm's 0.889 on $\annuity$ the
one figure of the four falling just short. CBD is also the only family fit
and integrated on ages 55--99 alone, and family and truncated support are
not separable in this design. The sparse VAR is the one full-age arm
calibrated when nothing breaks (0.983 and 0.980) and the arm the break
damages most (0.621 and 0.586, on 29 admissible shift units); its two
scales disagree about one model, rates moving \emph{towards} nominal from
0.988 to 0.937 while the liability moves 0.980 to 0.586, so reporting one
scale alone would call this family improved or destroyed at the analyst's
choice. On $e_0$, which \Hfive\ does not register, two classical families
also run the other way: Poisson-LC covers 0.744 and APC/RH 0.808 in the
control, above their rate-level marginals of 0.682 and 0.731, while SVD
Lee--Carter reaches 0.369 and the LSTM ensemble 0.267. We report that
column descriptively and read no mechanism into it.

\subsection{Reading a coverage shortfall in balance-sheet terms}

For an annuity-due of one per annum at age 65, the interval on $\annuity$
is an interval on the best-estimate liability of a unit annuitant. In
Solvency~II terms, an internal model covering the realised value in
materially fewer than 95\% of population--sex cells has a miscalibrated
longevity distribution---too narrow for most of these arms, mislocated for
the few that overstate---and Table~\ref{tab:h5-actuarial-main} puts every
distributional arm in that position in the shift regime and all but two in
the control; under COVID the best figure is 0.850 (CBD, on its truncated
table) and no full-age arm exceeds 0.750.

The direction of the central miss matters. In the control the mean error
on $\annuity$ is negative for 19 of the 20 arms---the central factor below
the realised one, understating the liability---the exception being the
NB-head ensemble at $+0.02$ annuity units, the understatement reaching
$-0.63$ for the LSTM ensemble and $-0.58$ for SVD Lee--Carter, whose
median $e_{65}$ path sits a mean of 0.84 years below the realised value in
the control and 0.57 years below it at the break. Across the pandemic the
signs split, twelve positive against eight negative: both SVD Lee--Carter
arms, both CNN-LC arms and both LSTM arms still sit below the realised
$\annuity$ ($-0.24$ to $-0.45$) and CBD's two marginally below on its
truncated table ($-0.03$ and $-0.04$), while APC/RH, neural-LC, the sparse
VAR's native law and the NB-head ensemble sit above ($+0.24$ to $+0.39$)
and the sparse VAR's bootstrap above by $+0.14$. This is not the
single-signed pandemic effect a uniform mortality margin would absorb, and
for the arms already understating it is no pandemic effect at all: the
excess mortality of 2020 moved the realised annuity factor towards their
too-low central estimates, masking a pre-existing bias rather than
creating one.

Where an arm's 97.5\% quantile of $\annuity$ falls below the realised
value, the shortfall is the amount by which implied capital at that level
is understated; we report it relative to the central $\annuity$, portable
across populations and interest assumptions. These breach frequencies are
what the study reports at the top of the distribution in place of the
registered 99.5\% longevity quantile, which the runner did not emit (\S8).
The understating arms breach at many times the nominal 2.5\%, and more
often in the quiet control than under COVID. In the control the realised
$\annuity$ exceeds the 97.5\% quantile in 0.804 of the LSTM ensemble's 434
admissible cells, 0.779 of LSTM dropout's, 0.701 of SVD Lee--Carter's 442,
0.529 of Poisson-LC's and 0.441 of CNN-LC dropout's, with mean shortfalls
over the breached cells of 4.2\%, 4.0\%, 4.2\%, 2.0\% and 2.4\%; the same
five breach in 0.605, 0.474, 0.425, 0.150 and 0.325 of shift cells, at
3.4\%, 3.8\%, 4.3\%, 1.5\% and 2.5\%. Capital set at the model's 97.5\%
quantile is therefore understated by two to four per cent of the
best-estimate liability in a large minority of population--sex
origins---and, in the control, for four of these five arms in an outright
majority---against a nominal one in forty. The overstating arms fail on
the other side: under COVID the NB-head ensemble and its dropout arm never
breach above, but the realised value falls below their 2.5\% quantile in
0.550 and 0.250 of cells (mean excess 2.0\% and 1.6\%) and below the
neural-LC ensemble's in 0.625 (2.3\%); in the control those three are
milder and two-sided, upper breaches in 0.081, 0.106 and 0.292 against
lower breaches in 0.104, 0.090 and 0.249---reserves set too high rather
than capital set too low. CBD is again the outlier in magnitude, breaching
above in 0.050--0.075 of shift and 0.066--0.109 of stable cells with a
mean shortfall at or below 1\%. Because the quantity is relative to the
central $\annuity$, a worked example on a hypothetical portfolio would
only rescale these figures, and we add none.

\subsection{Twin crises in economic terms}

The placebo rows supply the same functionals for 1914--1922; the
comparison is descriptive, and with the control in place a shortfall the
quiet decades already show cannot be credited to either break. For the
full-age classical cores under their native laws realised $e_{65}$ falls
inside the nominal band in 0.425--0.500 of population--sex cells under
COVID and 0.455--0.636 across 1914--1922, $\annuity$ in 0.450--0.475 and
0.455--0.636---against 0.256--0.654 and 0.253--0.643 in the control, on
442 units against 40 and 22. The shortfall is economically material at
both breaks and of the same order at each, and for two of the three cores
no larger than the control already carries: SVD Lee--Carter's annuity
coverage is \emph{lowest} in the quiet control at 0.253, highest at the
placebo at 0.636, with COVID in between at 0.450, and Poisson-LC runs the
same way at 0.430, 0.475 and 0.591. APC/RH is the exception, worse at both
breaks than in the control, its $e_{65}$ and $\annuity$ falling from 0.654
and 0.643 to 0.500 and 0.475 under COVID and to 0.455 at the placebo.
CBD's native law covers 0.875 on $e_{65}$ under COVID and 1.000 on both
functionals at the placebo, against 0.889 and 0.930 in the control, and
its age-55 support lies above the excess mortality of 1918. The $e_0$
column carries the timing caveat in numbers---0.909 for Lee--Carter at the
placebo against 0.500 under the shift and 0.369 in the control---because
the placebo's first test year, 1914, predates the influenza wave while
2020 sits inside the COVID shock. Outside the sparse VAR's placebo
bootstrap row, which covers 1.000 on both functionals on the nine of
twenty-two units that survived, neither break leaves a full-age family's
derived intervals anywhere near nominal---but neither does the control,
and on the evidence of three regimes the pandemic is not distinctive in
economic kind, nor, for the worst-behaved families, even in degree.
